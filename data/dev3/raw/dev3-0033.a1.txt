IMPRESSIONS

[[CHILD|Mateo]] presents with a significant expressive and receptive language delay affecting both Spanish and English, consistent with a diagnosis of language delay / disorder rather than a primary speech sound disorder, given his age and the absence of oral-motor or structural concerns. His scores fall well below the [[KEEP|1st percentile]] across standardized and criterion-referenced measures in both languages, indicating a delay that is not attributable to typical bilingual language development alone; a child acquiring two languages typically shows at least one age-appropriate language, which is not the case here. Strong nonverbal social communication skills, including joint attention, shared affect, and nonverbal turn-taking, are a relative strength and a favorable prognostic sign for intervention.

Given his age, the magnitude of the delay in both languages, and the absence of a single stronger language, [[CHILD|Mateo]] meets criteria for Infant-Toddler Program eligibility under North Carolina's established conditions for developmental delay. A formal audiological evaluation is strongly recommended to rule out hearing loss as a contributing factor, since this has not yet been completed despite normal newborn screening results, and mild or fluctuating hearing loss related to his history of otitis media cannot be excluded on history alone.
