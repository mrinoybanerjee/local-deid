IMPRESSIONS

[[CHILD|Teo]] presents with a primary language impairment affecting both receptive and expressive language skills in his dominant language, Spanish, as well as delayed second-language (English) development consistent with limited exposure. His performance across standardized Spanish measures, including the [[KEEP|PLS-5 Spanish]] and [[KEEP|BESA]], falls within the below-average to well-below-average range, indicating a true language disorder rather than typical second-language acquisition alone. Articulation errors in both languages further reduce overall speech intelligibility, impacting his ability to communicate effectively with unfamiliar listeners, including teachers and peers at [[SCHOOL|Jesse Wharton Elementary School]].

Given the consistency of delays across both languages in morphosyntax, vocabulary, and sound production, in combination with history of recurrent otitis media and normal current hearing status confirmed by audiologic screening, [[CHILD|Teo]] meets criteria for a mixed receptive-expressive language disorder (ICD-10 code [[KEEP|F80.2]]) with concomitant phonological disorder (ICD-10 code [[KEEP|F80.0]]). These findings support continued eligibility for early intervention services through [[AGENCY|Piedmont Triad Children's Developmental Services Agency]] and warrant consideration of continued speech-language therapy services as he transitions into the public school system through [[SCHOOL|Guilford County Schools]].

[[CHILD|Teo]]'s strengths include strong nonverbal communication skills, good eye contact, appropriate social engagement with peers and family members, and a cooperative, motivated disposition during structured testing tasks. He benefits from visual supports, moderate cueing, and bilingual input, all of which should be incorporated into intervention planning.

RECOMMENDATIONS

1. [[CHILD|Teo]] should continue to receive speech-language therapy services, ideally delivered bilingually or in close coordination with a bilingual provider, at a frequency of two sessions per week, [[KEEP|30 minutes]] each, focusing on expressive vocabulary growth, sentence structure, and speech sound production in both Spanish and English.

2. Treatment approaches should include [[KEEP|Hanen]]-based strategies to support parent coaching and naturalistic language facilitation in the home, given the family's strong desire to maintain Spanish as the primary home language. [[KEEP|PROMPT]] techniques are recommended to address tactile-kinesthetic cueing for articulation targets, and a [[KEEP|Cycles]] approach is recommended to address phonological patterns including final consonant deletion and cluster reduction.

3. Coordination with occupational therapist [[PROVIDER|Jennifer Walsh]] and physical therapist [[PROVIDER|Carlos Mendoza]] is recommended to ensure consistency of cueing strategies across disciplines and to support generalization of communication goals during motor-based activities.

4. Continued coordination with pediatrician [[PROVIDER|Dr. Sarah Kim]] is recommended to monitor for any recurrence of otitis media, given the known association between chronic middle ear fluid and speech-language delay.

5. School-based consultation with Ms. [[TEACHER|Amanda Lopez]] and teaching assistant Mr. [[TEACHER|David Alvarez]] is recommended to implement classroom strategies such as pre-teaching vocabulary, allowing additional response time, and using visual schedules to support comprehension of multi-step directions.

6. Family training sessions with [[PARENT|Lucía Rodríguez]] and [[PARENT|Francisco Hernández]], supported by interpreter services as needed, are recommended to teach specific language facilitation strategies for use during daily routines, including mealtimes and bedtime.

7. Re-evaluation is recommended in approximately six months, or sooner if significant progress or concerns arise, to update standardized scores and adjust the treatment plan accordingly.

8. The family should be connected with [[AGENCY|Smart Start of Guilford County]] for information regarding additional community-based language enrichment programs, and the service coordinator, [[PROVIDER|Michelle Davis]], should assist with transition planning as [[CHILD|Teo]] approaches his third birthday relative to Part C to Part B transition under IDEA.
