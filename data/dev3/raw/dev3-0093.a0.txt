[[CHILD|Mateo]] was born at [[KEEP|35]] weeks gestation at [[HOSPITAL|Pinecrest Women's Hospital]], with a birth weight of [[KEEP|4 lbs 11 oz]] and Apgar scores of [[KEEP|6]] and [[KEEP|8]]. The family's primary concerns, in their own words, center on communication and frustration tolerance. [[PARENT|Lucía]] said, "en la guardería las otras mamás me dicen que sus hijos ya hablan en oraciones, y [[CHILD|Teo]] todavía señala y gruñe" ("at daycare the other moms tell me their children already speak in sentences, and [[CHILD|Teo]] still points and grunts"). [[PARENT|Hernán]] shared that he worries [[CHILD|Mateo]] will fall behind once he starts preschool, and that family gatherings are hard because [[CHILD|Mateo]]'s cousins, [[FAMILY|Ximena Torres]] and [[FAMILY|Joaquín Torres]], play together easily in both languages while [[CHILD|Mateo]] often wanders off alone or gets upset when he cannot make himself understood. The family's priorities for this IFSP are: increasing [[CHILD|Mateo]]'s expressive vocabulary in both English and Spanish, reducing tantrum behavior linked to communication breakdown, and helping [[CHILD|Mateo]] participate more successfully in group activities at daycare.

[[CHILD|Mateo]] attends [[DAYCARE|Little Sprouts Daycare]] on weekday mornings while [[PARENT|Lucía]] sleeps after her overnight shift. Mom works nights at [[ORG|Harrison Foods Distribution Center]], typically from [[TIME|11:00 p.m.]] to [[TIME|7:30 a.m.]], and Dad works days as a machine operator, which means [[FAMILY|Alicia Ramírez]] and [[FAMILY|Daniela Soto]] share drop-off and pickup duties during the week. At daycare, his teacher, [[TEACHER|Ms. Deborah Patterson]], reports that [[CHILD|Mateo]] enjoys circle time with classmates [[PEER|Ayden]] and [[PEER|Nilda]] but tends to watch rather than join in during songs. The assistant teacher, [[TEACHER|Ms. Karen Liu]], noted that [[CHILD|Mateo]] will point to a snack and grunt rather than attempt the word, and that he has started biting his own hand when he becomes frustrated during transitions.

At home, the family's typical morning routine runs from about [[TIME|6:45 a.m.]] to [[TIME|8:00 a.m.]]: [[CHILD|Mateo]] wakes, eats breakfast at the kitchen table with [[SIBLING|Camila]], and gets dressed with help from [[FAMILY|Alicia]] before daycare drop-off. Bath time around [[TIME|7:00 p.m.]] is a high-motivation routine; [[CHILD|Mateo]] will reach for the water and say what sounds like "[[KEEP|/wabo/]]" for "water," which the family has started modeling back as a full word in both languages. Bedtime stories are read in Spanish by [[PARENT|Lucía]] and in English by [[PARENT|Hernán]] on alternating nights, giving [[CHILD|Mateo]] regular exposure to both languages at home.

The family also receives support from several other agencies. [[PARENT|Lucía]] reports that the Smart Start playgroup has been a good place for [[CHILD|Mateo]] to be around other toddlers outside of daycare, though she notes he still prefers to play near her rather than with the other children.

[[CHILD|Mateo]]'s age-equivalent scores on the [[KEEP|Expressive Communication]] subtest rose from [[KEEP|14-16]] months at the initial evaluation to [[KEEP|19-21]] months at the six-month review, placing him within the [[KEEP|18 months]] to [[KEEP|22 months]] range overall, with receptive skills estimated at [[KEEP|2;0–2;3]] and expressive skills estimated at [[KEEP|1;7–1;9]]. This represents a gap of approximately [[KEEP|six months]] between receptive and expressive development, consistent with his pattern of understanding more than he produces.

During direct observation, [[CHILD|Mateo]] used a total expressive vocabulary estimated at [[KEEP|35]] words across both languages, with roughly [[KEEP|20]] English words and [[KEEP|15]] Spanish words, and no two-word combinations observed. He imitated the target word "[[KEEP|more]]" with [[KEEP|moderate cues]] [[KEEP|3 of 5]] trials, and produced "[[KEEP|up]]" approximated as "[[KEEP|[ʌp]]]" independently in [[KEEP|8/10]] opportunities when reaching to be picked up. When this writer modeled the Spanish word "pelota" (ball), [[CHILD|Mateo]] responded with an approximation close to "[[KEEP|[eota]]]" after [[KEEP|minimal cues]]. His play skills are emerging appropriately for his age, including functional play with cause-and-effect toys and brief pretend sequences such as feeding a stuffed animal.

Based on these findings, the IFSP team, including [[PARENT|Lucía Rodríguez]], [[PARENT|Hernán Cruz]], [[PROVIDER|Denise Walters]], and this writer, developed the following outcomes.

Outcome 1 ([[KEEP|IFSP Outcome 1]]): [[CHILD|Mateo]] will use at least [[KEEP|50]] single words across English and Spanish to request and comment during daily routines, as measured by parent report and direct observation, within six months. Strategies include modeling target words during bath time and backyard play with [[PET|Rocky]], using a core vocabulary board at daycare and at home, and having [[FAMILY|Alicia Ramírez]] and [[FAMILY|Daniela Soto]] narrate routines aloud in whichever language they are most comfortable using, since both languages support overall language growth.

Outcome 2 ([[KEEP|IFSP Outcome 2]]): [[CHILD|Mateo]] will combine two words together (for example "more water" or "más agua") in [[KEEP|3 of 5]] observed opportunities within six months. Strategies include expansion of his single-word utterances by caregivers, use of the [[KEEP|Hanen]] "It Takes Two to Talk" approach during play, and brief weekly coaching sessions with [[PARENT|Lucía]] and [[PARENT|Hernán]] on expansion techniques they can use during bedtime stories and car rides.

Outcome 3 ([[KEEP|IFSP Outcome 3]]): [[CHILD|Mateo]] will use a word, approximation, or gesture paired with a word to communicate a need instead of grunting or self-biting, in [[KEEP|4 of 5]] observed frustration episodes, within six months. Strategies include teaching [[TEACHER|Ms. Deborah Patterson]] and [[TEACHER|Ms. Karen Liu]] to prompt for a word before responding to a grunt, visual supports at daycare showing choices such as snack or outside, and consistent response from family members at home, including [[FAMILY|Alicia Ramírez]], to wait for a communicative attempt before intervening.

Services under this plan include direct speech-language therapy twice weekly for thirty minutes, delivered in the home and at daycare in both English and Spanish by [[PROVIDER|Hannah Reyes]], beginning [[DATE|March 10, 2026]] and continuing through this review date. Developmental therapy consultation is provided monthly by [[PROVIDER|Marcus Webb]], occupational therapist, to address sensory regulation strategies related to [[CHILD|Mateo]]'s frustration behaviors. Service coordination continues monthly with [[PROVIDER|Denise Walters]]. The family has consented to continued services and requested that sessions remain scheduled around [[PARENT|Lucía]]'s sleep schedule when possible, given her overnight work hours.

At this six-month review, the team notes meaningful progress. [[CHILD|Mateo]]'s total language score rose from [[KEEP|71]] to an estimated [[KEEP|78]], within the [[KEEP|73-83]] range reported above, and his self-biting behavior has decreased from several times daily to roughly twice weekly, according to both [[PARENT|Lucía]] and [[TEACHER|Ms. Deborah Patterson]]. [[CHILD|Mateo]] has begun approximating the names of his cousins, [[FAMILY|Ximena Torres]] and [[FAMILY|Joaquín Torres]], during family visits, and recently called for [[PET|Rocky]] by name across the backyard, which [[PARENT|Hernán]] described as "the best thing I've heard all year." The family continues to prioritize bilingual language exposure and has asked that future sessions include more strategies for supporting [[CHILD|Mateo]]'s participation in group settings like the [[AGENCY|Wake County Smart Start]] playgroup and at [[DAYCARE|Little Sprouts Daycare]].

The team agreed to continue the current outcomes with revised criteria reflecting progress, to add a fourth outcome addressing group participation, and to reconvene for the next six-month review on [[DATE|March 16, 2027]]. All team members present, including [[PARENT|Lucía Rodríguez]], [[PARENT|Hernán Cruz]], [[PROVIDER|Denise Walters]], and [[PROVIDER|Hannah Reyes]], signed this plan on [[DATE|September 22, 2026]], indicating agreement with the outcomes, strategies, and services described above.
