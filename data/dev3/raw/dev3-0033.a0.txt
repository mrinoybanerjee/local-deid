CLINICAL IMPRESSIONS

Based on standardized testing, parent and teacher report, and direct observation, [[CHILD|Mateo]] presents with a bilingual language disorder characterized by significantly delayed receptive and expressive language skills in both English and Spanish, alongside a moderate-severe speech sound disorder affecting intelligibility in both languages. The pattern of deficits across both languages, confirmed through comparison with sociolinguistic norms for Spanish-English bilingual children in North Carolina, indicates a true disorder rather than a difference attributable to dual language exposure. His history of chronic otitis media may have contributed to early language delays, though current hearing status is within normal limits. [[CHILD|Mateo]]'s intelligibility was estimated at approximately [[KEEP|40%]] to unfamiliar listeners and [[KEEP|65%]] to familiar listeners, consistent with teacher report from Ms. [[TEACHER|Lopez]].

[[CHILD|Mateo]] demonstrated age-appropriate social engagement, joint attention, and play skills, with no concerns raised regarding autism spectrum features during this evaluation. His difficulties appear specific to the structural and phonological domains of language rather than broader social communication or cognitive functioning, although a comprehensive developmental evaluation was not within the scope of this assessment.
