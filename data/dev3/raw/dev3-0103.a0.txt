**Speech Sound Production**

[[CHILD|Mateo]]'s speech was approximately [[KEEP|55%]] intelligible to unfamiliar listeners in connected speech and [[KEEP|75%]] intelligible to familiar listeners. Phonological processes observed included final consonant deletion, cluster reduction, and gliding of liquids (e.g., [[KEEP|/w/]] for [[KEEP|/r/]]). On a word-finding and articulation probe using family-salient vocabulary, the target word "[[KEEP|sol]]" (meaning "sun") was elicited five times; [[CHILD|Mateo]] produced it as "[[KEEP|tol]]" in [[KEEP|4 of 5]] trials, substituting [[KEEP|/t/]] for [[KEEP|/s/]] and showing stable production of the final consonant. This target was selected because it is phonetically similar to several high-frequency words in the home and because it carries personal relevance; it is not a reference to any family member's name. When shown a photo of his sister and asked to label her, [[CHILD|Mateo]] instead said "[[SIBLING|Sol]]" clearly and without substitution, suggesting the name itself is a stored whole-word form distinct from his production of the common noun "[[KEEP|sol]]."

In Spanish, cluster reduction was also noted on words such as "[[KEEP|flor]]" produced as "[[KEEP|for]]," and gliding was observed in English on "[[KEEP|rabbit]]" produced as "[[KEEP|wabbit]]." Oral mechanism exam was within normal limits for structure and function; no concerns for dysarthria or apraxia were noted, though a mild reduction in diadochokinetic rate was observed.

**Language Sample Analysis**

A 100-utterance spontaneous language sample was collected across both English and Spanish contexts. Mean length of utterance in morphemes (MLUm) was [[KEEP|2.8]] in English and [[KEEP|3.1]] in Spanish, both below age expectations ([[KEEP|3;8]]). SALT coding flagged frequent omission of grammatical morphemes, coded as [[KEEP|[EW]]] (error, word-level) and [[KEEP|[EO]]] (error, omission) across both languages. [[CHILD|Mateo]] used primarily two- and three-word utterances, relied on gesture to supplement communication, and showed better performance in structured one-on-one interaction than in group play observed briefly with his daycare classmate [[PEER|Ethan]].

**Impressions**

[[CHILD|Mateo]] presents with a bilingual speech sound disorder and expressive-receptive language delay affecting both English and Spanish, consistent with a true language disorder rather than a difference attributable to typical second-language acquisition, given comparable delays across both languages and a positive family history of late language emergence. Relevant ICD-10 code: [[KEEP|F80.9]] (Developmental disorder of speech and language, unspecified). Relevant CPT codes used for this evaluation: [[KEEP|92523]] (evaluation of speech sound production with evaluation of language comprehension and expression) and [[KEEP|92521]] (modifier not applicable).

Given his history of recurrent otitis media and recent tube placement, ongoing monitoring of hearing status in coordination with [[PROVIDER|Dr. Grace Thompson]] is recommended. Family and early-intervention history, including the brief CADINF screening in Honduras, support a developmental rather than purely environmental explanation for his current delays.
