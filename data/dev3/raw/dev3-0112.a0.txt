clinical impressions

majo is a delightful friendly four year old girl who presents with a moderate expressive language delay in both spanish and english with relatively stronger receptive skills in both languages and a moderate to severe speech sound disorder that is consistent across both of her languages her pattern of errors including final consonant deletion weak syllable deletion and gliding occurring in both spanish and english supports a true speech sound disorder rather than a language difference her bilingual exposure appears appropriate for her home and community context and her code mixing is developmentally typical for a bilingual child her age and should not be discouraged by the family or by daycare staff majo's difficulties appear to meet criteria for part c eligibility based on her scores falling more than 1.5 standard deviations below the mean in expressive language in both languages combined with a significant speech sound disorder affecting intelligibility in both languages

her strengths include good attention to tasks when motivated strong nonverbal problem solving observed informally during play good social engagement with both adults and peers and a supportive home environment with multiple caregivers invested in her progress areas of need include expressive vocabulary and grammar in both languages speech sound production affecting final consonants and weak syllables in both languages and functional intelligibility for unfamiliar communication partners

recommendations

1 majo qualifies for early intervention services through [[AGENCY|the cdsa de durham]] and it is recommended that an ifsp be developed with input from the family her service coordinator [[PROVIDER|marisol reyes]] and this evaluating team

2 individual speech and language therapy is recommended at a frequency of two sessions per week for thirty minutes each targeting both spanish and english with a bilingual provider when available given the strong evidence that a true speech sound disorder benefits from consistent intervention across a child's full linguistic repertoire rather than english only services

3 therapy should target final consonant production in words like "[[KEEP|pull]]" "[[KEEP|ball]]" and "[[KEEP|flor]]" using a cycles approach alternating target sound classes across short treatment blocks

4 therapy should also target syllable structure in multisyllabic words using a combination of auditory bombardment and structured practice with gradually increasing word length moving from two syllable to three syllable targets

5 expressive language goals should target increasing mean length of utterance and use of grammatical morphemes in both spanish and english such as plural markers and verb tense markers using naturalistic play based approaches and following the child's lead consistent with a [[KEEP|hanen]] style framework

6 the family and daycare staff should continue supporting both languages at home and at school and should be reassured that bilingual exposure is not contributing to majo's difficulties and should not be reduced
