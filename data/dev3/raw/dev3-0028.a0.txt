Impresiones / Impressions: Based on standardized testing, parent and teacher report, and clinical observation, [[CHILD|Majo]] presents with a moderate-to-severe mixed receptive-expressive language disorder affecting both Spanish and English, consistent with her prior diagnosis (ICD-10 [[KEEP|F80.2]]) and not attributable to her bilingual language exposure; her language skills are reduced relative to typically developing bilingual peers in both languages, which supports a primary language impairment rather than a difference associated with second-language acquisition. She also presents with a moderate speech sound disorder characterized by phonological process errors (final consonant deletion, cluster reduction, fronting) that reduce intelligibility across both languages, now further complicated by a history of chronic otitis media and tube placement, though her current hearing status is documented as within normal limits. [[CHILD|Majo]]'s strengths include strong nonverbal communication, social motivation to interact with peers such as [[PEER|Josué Ramírez]] and [[PEER|Emily Johnson]], and a supportive, engaged family system.

Recomendaciones / Recommendations: It is recommended that [[CHILD|Majo]] continue to receive speech-language therapy through [[AGENCY|Piedmont CDSA]] at a minimum frequency of twice weekly, delivered bilingually or in close coordination between an English-speaking and a Spanish-speaking provider, with specific attention to expanding mean length of utterance, grammatical morpheme use in both languages, and vocabulary diversity. Continued use of [[KEEP|Hanen]]-based parent coaching strategies is recommended, given the family's strong engagement, alongside structured [[KEEP|PROMPT]] tactile-cueing approaches to target the specific phonological processes identified above; a structured [[KEEP|Cycles]] approach to phonology may also be appropriate given the number of processes affecting intelligibility. It is recommended that [[CHILD|Majo]]'s IFSP be updated at the upcoming review on [[DATE|January 20, 2026]] to reflect these findings, with a new outcome addressing speech sound intelligibility alongside the existing [[KEEP|Outcome 2]] for expressive language.
