CLINICAL IMPRESSIONS

Based on standardized testing in both of [[CHILD|Mateo]]'s languages, parent report, classroom observation reported by [[TEACHER|Ms. Patel]], and informal dynamic assessment, [[CHILD|Mateo]] presents with a speech sound disorder and an expressive-receptive language disorder affecting both Spanish and English. His performance pattern, with below-average scores in both languages that show the same phonological error patterns across languages, supports a true disorder rather than a language difference secondary to dual language exposure or limited English proficiency. This distinction is clinically important: had [[CHILD|Mateo]] shown strong skills in Spanish with only weak English, a pattern consistent with typical second-language acquisition would be more likely, and services would not be indicated on that basis alone.

[[CHILD|Mateo]]'s combination of reduced intelligibility, limited phonemic inventory (consistent deletion of final consonants, fronting of velars such as [[KEEP|/k/]] and [[KEEP|/g/]], and stopping of fricatives including [[KEEP|/s/]] and [[KEEP|/f/]]), and below-average expressive and receptive language scores in both languages meets criteria for eligibility for Part C early intervention services under North Carolina's established eligibility guidelines, which this examiner understands to require a [[KEEP|25% delay]] or a score at least [[KEEP|1.5 standard deviations]] below the mean in one or more developmental domains. [[CHILD|Mateo]] meets this threshold in both speech sound production and expressive language, and is borderline in receptive language.

[[CHILD|Mateo]]'s family and community supports, including involvement with [[AGENCY|WIC]], the [[AGENCY|Guilford County Partnership for Children]], [[AGENCY|DSS]], and [[AGENCY|CRAFLCN]], along with his parents' consistent engagement in this evaluation process, are considered strong protective factors that should support his response to intervention.
