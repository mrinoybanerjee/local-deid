LANGUAGE RESULTS

On the [[KEEP|CELF-5]], [[CHILD|Adrián]] obtained a Core Language Score of [[KEEP|72]] ([[KEEP|3rd]] percentile), falling within the moderately delayed range relative to same-age peers. His Receptive Language Index of [[KEEP|78]] was higher than his Expressive Language Index of [[KEEP|69]], consistent with a pattern of relatively stronger comprehension than production, which was also reflected in parent and teacher report. On the [[KEEP|PPVT-5]], a receptive single-word vocabulary measure, he scored [[KEEP|85]] ([[KEEP|16th]] percentile), within the low-average range, suggesting single-word vocabulary comprehension is an area of relative strength compared to his sentence-level formulation skills.

Spontaneous language sampling revealed frequent omission of grammatical morphemes in both languages, including copula "is/está," plural markers, and past-tense verb forms. In English, [[CHILD|Adrián]] produced utterances such as "the dog is run" and "him go home," demonstrating reduced use of auxiliary verbs and pronoun case errors typical of a language disorder rather than typical bilingual acquisition patterns, per cross-linguistic comparison with Spanish structures. In Spanish, similar error patterns were noted, including article omission and verb conjugation errors beyond what would be expected for a bilingual learner at this age and language exposure history.

SPEECH SOUND PRODUCTION RESULTS

On the [[KEEP|GFTA-3]], [[CHILD|Adrián]] obtained a standard score of [[KEEP|73]] ([[KEEP|4th]] percentile), indicating a moderate speech sound disorder affecting both consonant and consonant cluster production. Error patterns included final consonant deletion, cluster reduction, and gliding of liquids. Stimulability testing showed that [[CHILD|Adrián]] could produce [[KEEP|/k/]] and [[KEEP|/g/]] correctly in isolation and in syllables with [[KEEP|moderate cues]], but these sounds were frequently fronted in connected speech to [[KEEP|[t]]] and [[KEEP|[d]]], respectively. He produced the target word [[KEEP|"cup"]] as [[KEEP|"tup"]] in [[KEEP|8/10]] trials ([[KEEP|80%]]) during single-word probes.

Liquid sounds [[KEEP|/l/]] and [[KEEP|/ɾ/]] (the Spanish tapped r) were both glided or distorted; [[CHILD|Adrián]] produced the target [[KEEP|"carro"]] as approximately [[KEEP|"cajo"]] with [[KEEP|minimal cues]] and showed inconsistent improvement to a distorted but closer approximation with [[KEEP|maximum cues]]. Final consonant deletion was noted in approximately [[KEEP|3 of 5]] probed words across both languages, consistent with a phonological process pattern rather than an isolated articulation error.

IMPRESSIONS

Based on standardized testing, language sampling in both English and Spanish, interview, and direct observation, [[CHILD|Adrián]] presents with a moderate mixed receptive-expressive language disorder, consistent with [[KEEP|ICD-10 F80.2]] (Mixed receptive-expressive language disorder), affecting both of his languages beyond what would be expected from typical bilingual development alone. He additionally presents with a moderate speech sound disorder, consistent with [[KEEP|ICD-10 F80.0]] (Phonological disorder), characterized by final consonant deletion, cluster reduction, gliding of liquids, and fronting, present cross-linguistically in Spanish and English.

These findings are consistent with his documented history of prematurity, NICU course, and early developmental delay, and they continue to meaningfully affect his intelligibility, academic participation, and social communication in both home and school settings. He continues to meet criteria for ongoing speech-language intervention under his IFSP and will likely require continued support through an IEP once he transitions to kindergarten-level services within [[SCHOOL|Wake County Public School System]].
