6. TEST RESULTS

6.1 Receptive and Expressive Language

On the Spanish edition of the [[KEEP|PLS-5]], [[CHILD|Mateo]] obtained an Auditory Comprehension standard score of [[KEEP|76]] ([[KEEP|5th percentile]]) and an Expressive Communication standard score of [[KEEP|68]] ([[KEEP|2nd percentile]]), yielding a Total Language Score of [[KEEP|70]] ([[KEEP|2nd percentile]]), with a [[KEEP|95 percent]] confidence interval of [[KEEP|64-76]]. On the English edition, administered to capture his emerging second-language skills, [[CHILD|Mateo]] obtained an Auditory Comprehension standard score of [[KEEP|62]] and an Expressive Communication standard score of [[KEEP|55]], both well below age expectations, which is consistent with his more limited English exposure rather than an independent area of concern.

On the [[KEEP|BESA]] Morphosyntax subtest, [[CHILD|Mateo]] scored at the [[KEEP|3rd percentile]] in Spanish, with frequent omission of articles and inconsistent use of verb tense markers. On the [[KEEP|BESA]] Semantics subtest, he scored at the [[KEEP|9th percentile]], demonstrating a reduced vocabulary relative to same-age Spanish-English bilingual peers. Screening items from the [[KEEP|CELF Preschool-3]] were administered in English and were consistent with significant delay, though results were interpreted cautiously given [[CHILD|Mateo]]'s limited English exposure.

Language sample analysis revealed a mean length of utterance of [[KEEP|1.8 morphemes]], below the expected range of [[KEEP|2.5-3.5 morphemes]] for his chronological age. [[CHILD|Mateo]]'s utterances were coded with [[KEEP|[EW]]] for several English word insertions into otherwise Spanish utterances, consistent with typical bilingual code-mixing rather than disordered language use. He combined two words in approximately [[KEEP|4 of 10]] utterances and rarely produced three-word combinations.

6.2 Articulation and Phonology

On the [[KEEP|GFTA-3]], [[CHILD|Mateo]] obtained a standard score of [[KEEP|62]] ([[KEEP|1st percentile]]), with an age equivalent of [[KEEP|2;6–3;0]]. Phonological processes noted included final consonant deletion, cluster reduction, and fronting of velar sounds. For example, [[CHILD|Mateo]] produced "[[KEEP|ato]]" for the target word "[[KEEP|gato]]" and "[[KEEP|tasa]]" for "[[KEEP|casa]]," both consistent with velar fronting affecting [[KEEP|/k/]] and [[KEEP|/g/]]. He also produced "[[KEEP|pan]]" for "[[KEEP|pan]]" correctly but omitted final consonants in words such as "[[KEEP|sol]]," produced as "[[KEEP|so]]." His speech intelligibility was estimated at approximately [[KEEP|30 percent]] to unfamiliar listeners and [[KEEP|60 percent]] to familiar listeners, consistent with parent and teacher report.

Stimulability testing showed that [[CHILD|Mateo]] could approximate [[KEEP|/k/]] in isolation with [[KEEP|moderate cues]] but was unable to maintain the sound at the word level. He demonstrated some success imitating [[KEEP|/s/]] in the final position with [[KEEP|minimum cues]], suggesting this may be a reasonable early treatment target.

6.3 Oral-Motor and Feeding

Informal oral-motor examination revealed adequate structure and strength of the lips, tongue, and jaw for speech and feeding purposes. No concerns with chewing, swallowing, or drooling were reported or observed. Diadochokinetic rates were judged to be mildly reduced for his age but not of clinical concern at this time.

7. CLINICAL IMPRESSIONS

Results of this evaluation indicate that [[CHILD|Mateo]] presents with a significant delay in both receptive and expressive language skills, as well as a moderate-to-severe speech sound disorder, in his dominant language of Spanish. These findings cannot be attributed solely to normal bilingual development, as [[CHILD|Mateo]]'s scores in Spanish, his dominant and most-exposed language, fall well below expected ranges relative to Spanish-English bilingual normative samples on the [[KEEP|BESA]]. His history of recurrent otitis media and tube placement is a relevant contributing factor to consider, though his hearing has since been documented as within normal limits.

[[CHILD|Mateo]]'s profile is consistent with Developmental Language Disorder (ICD-10 code [[KEEP|F80.9]]) co-occurring with a Speech Sound Disorder (ICD-10 code [[KEEP|F80.0]]). His reduced intelligibility, limited morphosyntactic development, and reduced vocabulary in his dominant language, combined with documented slow progress despite twelve months of home-based Early Intervention services, support continued and likely increased intervention intensity moving into the transition period from the NC Infant-Toddler Program.

8. SUMMARY

[[CHILD|Mateo Alejandro Vásquez]], a [[KEEP|4;2]]-year-old Spanish-English bilingual boy, was evaluated due to parent and provider concern regarding expressive language and intelligibility. Testing revealed significant delays in receptive and expressive language in Spanish, his dominant language, along with a moderate-to-severe speech sound disorder characterized by multiple phonological process errors. These findings are consistent with Developmental Language Disorder and Speech Sound Disorder and are not attributable to typical bilingual language acquisition patterns. [[CHILD|Mateo]]'s strengths include social engagement, use of compensatory gesture, and stimulability for several target sounds with cueing.
