A:

[[CHILD|Majo]] presents with a Mixed Receptive-Expressive Language Disorder ([[KEEP|F80.2]]) with a disproportionately expressive profile, co-occurring with a Speech Sound Disorder ([[KEEP|F80.4]]) characterized by final consonant deletion, cluster reduction, gliding, and fronting, affecting intelligibility in both of her languages. Testing in both English and Spanish, combined with consistent parent and teacher report of comprehension outpacing production in both languages, supports a true language disorder rather than typical second-language acquisition or sequential bilingual "lag." Her pattern of errors is shared across English and Spanish, which further supports a disorder affecting the underlying language system rather than an artifact of bilingual exposure. Oral mechanism, hearing, and social communication skills are not of concern and do not suggest a primary motor speech disorder or autism spectrum disorder at this time, though continued monitoring of pragmatics is reasonable given mild reported quietness in group settings per [[PERSON|Hermano Tomás]] and [[TEACHER|Ms. Thompson]].

[[CHILD|Majo]] qualifies for early intervention services under North Carolina's Infant-Toddler/Preschool eligibility criteria based on standard scores more than 1.5 SD below the mean in expressive language in at least one language of assessment, corroborated by parent and teacher report, functional impact on daily communication, and consistency of findings across both evaluation sessions.
