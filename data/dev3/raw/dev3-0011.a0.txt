Producción de Sonidos del Habla / Speech Sound Production:

In English, [[CHILD|Mateo]] demonstrated a moderate phonological disorder characterized by final consonant deletion, cluster reduction, and fronting. On the [[KEEP|GFTA-3]], he produced [[KEEP|19]] of [[KEEP|53]] target sounds in error, with [[KEEP|8/10]] trials affected for /k/, /g/, /s/, /z/, /ʃ/, /θ/, and /ɹ/, as well as frequent errors on final consonants generally. For example, target "[[KEEP|cat]]" was produced as "[[KEEP|tat]]" (fronting of /k/ to [t]), and target "[[KEEP|dog]]" was produced as "[[KEEP|da]]" (final consonant deletion). With [[KEEP|moderate cues]] (verbal model plus tactile cue), [[CHILD|Mateo]] could approximate /k/ in isolation as [[KEEP|[k]]] in [[KEEP|6 of 10]] trials.

In Spanish, intelligibility was higher overall, though similar error patterns were noted: cluster reduction (target "[[KEEP|flor]]" produced as "[[KEEP|for]]"), tap/trill substitution (target "[[KEEP|perro]]" produced as "[[KEEP|pego]]", substituting [g] for [[KEEP|/ɾ/]]), and occasional final consonant deletion in Spanish words ending in /s/ (target "[[KEEP|dos]]" produced as "[[KEEP|do]]"). Stimulability for [[KEEP|/ɾ/]] was present with [[KEEP|min cues]] in [[KEEP|3 of 5]] trials, a favorable prognostic indicator.

Overall connected speech intelligibility, rated by this examiner and corroborated by [[PROVIDER|Mireya Soto]], was estimated at approximately [[KEEP|40%]] in English and [[KEEP|65%]] in Spanish for unfamiliar listeners, and approximately [[KEEP|70%]] in English and [[KEEP|85%]] in Spanish for familiar listeners such as mother and grandmother.

Comprensión y Producción del Lenguaje / Language Comprehension and Production:

Receptively, [[CHILD|Mateo]] followed [[KEEP|2]]-step directions in both languages with inconsistent success ([[KEEP|6/10]] in English, [[KEEP|8/10]] in Spanish) and demonstrated understanding of basic spatial concepts (arriba/abajo, adentro/afuera) and most common nouns and action verbs in both languages per parent report and direct testing.

Expressively, [[CHILD|Mateo]]'s utterances were primarily [[KEEP|1-2]] words in length, with occasional [[KEEP|3]]-word combinations in Spanish, such as "[[KEEP|quiero agua ya]]" (I want water now). Mean length of utterance in morphemes (MLU-m) was calculated at [[KEEP|1.8]] across the [[KEEP|50]]-utterance bilingual sample, well below expectations for his age in either language. He used primarily nouns and a small set of high-frequency verbs, with limited use of grammatical morphology in either language, including inconsistent plural -s, absent verb tense marking, and absent Spanish gender agreement, as in "[[KEEP|el pelota]]" rather than "[[KEEP|la pelota]]." SALT coding of the sample identified frequent errors coded as [[KEEP|[EW]]] (error, word-level) across both languages, as well as occasional [[KEEP|[EO]]] (error, omission) codes for missing grammatical morphemes.

[[CHILD|Mateo]] was estimated by parental report, via a MacArthur-Bates-style vocabulary checklist, to produce approximately [[KEEP|80]] intelligible words in English and [[KEEP|140]] intelligible words in Spanish, well below the [[KEEP|50th percentile]] cutoff for his age in a monolingual comparison and still delayed when combined into a conceptual (total) vocabulary estimate accounting for bilingual overlap.

Pragmática y Comunicación Social / Pragmatics and Social Communication:

[[CHILD|Mateo]] initiated communication frequently through gesture, gaze, and vocalization and used several early communicative functions, including requesting, protesting, and commenting, in both languages. He demonstrated joint attention and reciprocal turn-taking in play-based interaction with this examiner and showed age-appropriate affect and eye contact throughout testing. No concerns regarding social reciprocity, repetitive behavior, or restricted interests were noted during today's session or reported by parent or teacher, supporting that the primary area of concern is speech and language rather than broader social-communication functioning.

Impresiones Diagnósticas / Diagnostic Impressions:

Based on comprehensive bilingual assessment, [[CHILD|Mateo]] presents with a moderate-to-severe mixed receptive-expressive language disorder ([[KEEP|ICD-10: F80.2]]) and a moderate phonological/speech sound disorder ([[KEEP|ICD-10: F80.0]]) affecting both Spanish and English. Because deficits are present and of comparable severity across both of [[CHILD|Mateo]]'s languages, and because his overall language development is substantially delayed relative to typical bilingual acquisition patterns documented in the research literature, this profile is consistent with a true language disorder rather than typical second-language acquisition, language difference, or dialectal variation.

[[CHILD|Mateo]] meets eligibility criteria for Part C early intervention services under [[PLACE|North Carolina]]'s developmental delay criteria, with documented delays exceeding [[KEEP|25%]] in the communication domain across both receptive and expressive language, based on standardized assessment administered in both of his languages.
