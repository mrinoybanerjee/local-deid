ARTICULATION AND PHONOLOGY

On the [[KEEP|GFTA-3]], [[CHILD|Mateo]] demonstrated multiple sound errors affecting intelligibility, particularly in connected speech. He substituted [[KEEP|[w]]] for [[KEEP|/r/]] in word-initial position (e.g., "wabbit" for "rabbit") and demonstrated final consonant deletion in both English and Spanish (e.g., "ga[[KEEP|t]]o" produced as "ga_o," deleting the final [[KEEP|/o/]] syllable boundary cue). He also produced the trilled [[KEEP|/ɾ/]] inconsistently in Spanish words such as "perro," often substituting a tap for the full trill. When asked to imitate target words, [[CHILD|Mateo]] produced "[[KEEP|sun]]" as "[[KEEP|tun]]" and "[[KEEP|flower]]" as "[[KEEP|fower]]," both consistent with developmental phonological patterns but delayed relative to age expectations.

During a play-based language sample, [[CHILD|Mateo]] referred to his dog by name, saying "[[PET|Luna]] run fast," demonstrating omission of the third-person singular -s marker, a pattern noted across both English and Spanish utterances (e.g., omission of gender agreement markers in Spanish noun phrases). SALT coding of the sample flagged several utterances with the [[KEEP|[EW]]] code for word-level errors, and mean length of utterance in morphemes (MLU-m) was calculated at [[KEEP|2.8]], below the expected range for his chronological age.

Connected speech intelligibility was estimated at approximately [[KEEP|60%]] to unfamiliar listeners and [[KEEP|85%]] to familiar listeners, consistent with parent and teacher report. On a structured repetition task, [[CHILD|Mateo]] correctly imitated [[KEEP|6 of 10]] multisyllabic words, and on a following-directions subtask he correctly completed [[KEEP|3 of 5]] two-step commands without visual cues, improving to [[KEEP|4 of 5]] with [[KEEP|moderate cues]].

IMPRESSIONS

Based on standardized testing, parent and teacher report, and clinical observation, [[CHILD|Mateo]] presents with a speech sound disorder and a language disorder affecting both Spanish and English, consistent with a diagnosis of Developmental Language Disorder with concomitant speech sound disorder. Scores fall consistently below the 10th percentile across both languages on standardized measures, ruling out second-language acquisition alone as the explanation for his difficulties. His speech intelligibility, phonological patterns, and expressive language deficits are likely to impact academic readiness, peer interaction, and family communication without intervention. [[CHILD|Mateo]]'s strengths include good attention to structured tasks, responsiveness to visual and verbal cueing, and an affectionate, motivated temperament that should support progress in therapy.
