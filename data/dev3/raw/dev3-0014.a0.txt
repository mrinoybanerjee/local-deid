ARTICULATION AND PHONOLOGY

In English single-word testing, [[CHILD|Teo]] demonstrated final consonant deletion (e.g., "ca" for "cat"), stopping of fricatives (e.g., [t] for [[KEEP|/s/]], producing "tun" for "sun"), and cluster reduction (e.g., "poon" for "spoon"). Velar fronting was also observed, with [[CHILD|Teo]] producing [[KEEP|[t]]] for [[KEEP|/k/]] in words such as "tup" for "cup."

During the Spanish articulation sample, [[CHILD|Teo]] was asked to label a picture of the sky and produced the target word [[KEEP|"sol"]] as [[KEEP|[tol]]], substituting [[KEEP|[t]]] for [[KEEP|/s/]] consistent with the stopping pattern noted above; this was scored as an error on the target item and does not refer to any family member. He correctly imitated [[KEEP|/ɾ/]] in "pero" with [[KEEP|moderate verbal cues]] [[KEEP|3 of 5]] trials. Overall, Spanish consonant inventory was judged age-appropriate apart from the stopping and fronting patterns shared across both languages, suggesting a phonological process disorder rather than two separate language-specific delays.

IMPRESSIONS

Results indicate a moderate-to-severe mixed receptive-expressive language disorder ([[KEEP|F80.2]]) affecting both Spanish and English, with an accompanying speech sound disorder characterized by phonological processes atypical for his age in both languages. Reduced intelligibility for unfamiliar listeners ([[KEEP|R47.0]]) is consistent with parent and teacher concern. Because error patterns and language delay are present proportionally in both Spanish and English, these findings are consistent with a true communication disorder rather than typical second-language acquisition or dialectal variation. [[CHILD|Teo]]'s nonverbal problem-solving and play skills, informally observed during testing, appeared commensurate with chronological age, supporting a primary speech-language diagnosis rather than a broader developmental concern at this time.

RECOMMENDATIONS

1. [[CHILD|Teo]] should continue to receive speech-language intervention through his IFSP, increasing frequency to twice weekly, 45-minute sessions, provided bilingually or with interpreter support as needed.
2. Intervention should target phonological processes (final consonant deletion, stopping, velar fronting) using a cycles-based approach ([[KEEP|Cycles]]) alongside language stimulation strategies drawn from [[KEEP|Hanen]] "It Takes Two to Talk" to support caregiver-implemented strategies at home.
3. [[KEEP|PROMPT]] techniques may be incorporated to support motor planning for speech production given the cross-linguistic nature of the sound errors.
4. The family is encouraged to continue rich dual-language input rather than restricting either language, as bilingual exposure is not contributing to the delay.
5. Coordination with [[TEACHER|Ms. Patterson]] at [[DAYCARE|Little Sprouts Bilingual Academy]] is recommended to reinforce communication strategies during peer interactions.
6. Re-evaluation of articulation and language skills is recommended in [[KEEP|6 months]] or at the next IFSP review on [[DATE|3 de marzo de 2026]], whichever comes first.
7. IFSP Outcome [[KEEP|2]] and Outcome [[KEEP|3]] should be updated to reflect the revised frequency and the phonological treatment approach outlined above.
