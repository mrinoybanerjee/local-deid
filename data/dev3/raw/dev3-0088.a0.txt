Confidence intervals for the Total Language Score ranged from [[KEEP|68-78]] (English) and [[KEEP|74-84]] (Spanish), both falling within the moderately delayed range relative to age expectations. Age equivalents for expressive language fell between [[KEEP|2;0]] and [[KEEP|2;6]], reflecting a gap of approximately [[KEEP|18-24 months]] relative to chronological age.

The [[KEEP|Goldman-Fristoe Test of Articulation, Third Edition]] ([[KEEP|GFTA-3]]) was administered in English, yielding a standard score of [[KEEP|65]] ([[KEEP|1st percentile]]), with a Sounds-in-Words raw score reflecting multiple phonological process errors including final consonant deletion, cluster reduction, and fronting. Spanish phonological skills were assessed informally using a Spanish articulation screening, revealing similar patterns of final consonant deletion and cluster reduction consistent with a phonological disorder rather than a language-specific difference.

Specific error patterns observed included substitution of [[KEEP|/t/]] for [[KEEP|/k/]] in word-initial position (e.g., "[[KEEP|tat]]" for "cat"), deletion of final consonants (e.g., "[[KEEP|ca]]" for "cat"), and cluster reduction (e.g., "[[KEEP|poon]]" for "spoon"). [[CHILD|Mateo]] produced the target word "ball" as "[[KEEP|baw]]," demonstrating liquid gliding of [[KEEP|/l/]] to [[KEEP|[w]]]. In Spanish, he produced "[[KEEP|pelo]]" for "perro," showing substitution of the trilled [[KEEP|/r/]] with the lateral [[KEEP|/l/]], a pattern that may reflect both phonological process and typical developmental variation in Spanish-speaking children his age.

An oral-motor examination revealed adequate structure and function for speech, with mildly reduced diadochokinetic rates. No concerns were noted regarding feeding at this time, consistent with resolution of earlier feeding difficulties addressed under IFSP outcome [[KEEP|#5]].

A language sample was collected during structured and unstructured play, yielding a mean length of utterance (MLU) of [[KEEP|1.8 morphemes]] in English and [[KEEP|2.1 morphemes]] in Spanish. SALT analysis coding indicated frequent use of [[KEEP|[EW]]] (errored word) codes for substituted or omitted grammatical morphemes, and [[KEEP|[EO]]] codes for omitted obligatory forms such as articles and plural markers.

Parent report via the [[KEEP|MacArthur-Bates Communicative Development Inventories]] ([[KEEP|CDI]]), Spanish-English bilingual version, indicated an expressive vocabulary of approximately [[KEEP|85 words]] combined across both languages, below the [[KEEP|10th percentile]] for bilingual peers of similar age and language exposure.

IMPRESSIONS

[[CHILD|Mateo]] presents with a mixed receptive-expressive language disorder and a moderate phonological disorder affecting intelligibility in both English and Spanish. His language profile is consistent with a true language impairment rather than a language difference attributable to bilingual exposure, as deficits are observed across both languages relative to monolingual and bilingual normative data. His phonological error patterns (final consonant deletion, cluster reduction, stopping, gliding) are present in both languages and are not accounted for by typical cross-linguistic transfer patterns alone.

[[CHILD|Mateo]] continues to meet eligibility criteria for early intervention services under current North Carolina criteria, with standard scores more than [[KEEP|1.5 standard deviations]] below the mean in both receptive and expressive domains. Given his age ([[KEEP|4;2]]), his family should be informed of the upcoming transition process to preschool special education services through the local school district, as he will likely require continued speech-language services beyond his third birthday.
