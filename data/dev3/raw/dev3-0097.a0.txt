SPEECH SOUND PRODUCTION

On the [[KEEP|GFTA-3]], [[CHILD|Majo]] scored at the [[KEEP|3rd percentile]] overall, with error patterns consistent across both languages: final consonant deletion, cluster reduction, and substitution of [[KEEP|/ɾ/]] for trilled [[KEEP|/r/]]. During the language sample, [[CHILD|Majo]] produced "[[KEEP|peyo]]" for target [[KEEP|"perro"]] (dog) and "[[KEEP|ka]]" for target [[KEEP|"casa"]] (house), omitting the final [[KEEP|/s/]]. In English, she produced "[[KEEP|do]]" for target [[KEEP|"dog"]] and "[[KEEP|ka]]" for target [[KEEP|"cat"]], showing the same final-consonant deletion pattern cross-linguistically, which supports a true phonological disorder rather than typical second-language interference alone.

Interestingly, [[CHILD|Majo]] referred to the family dog by name during the sample, saying "[[PET|Canela]] vino" (Canela came) with the final [[KEEP|/n/]] intact, suggesting some variability by word familiarity and frequency.

Stimulability testing showed [[CHILD|Majo]] could produce final consonants with [[KEEP|moderate cues]] (verbal model plus visual cue) in [[KEEP|6 of 10]] trials, and could approximate [[KEEP|/r/]] with [[KEEP|maximal cues]] in [[KEEP|3 of 10]] trials. She was stimulable for syllable-final [[KEEP|/s/]] with [[KEEP|minimal cues]] in single words but not in connected speech.

IMPRESIONES CLÍNICAS / CLINICAL IMPRESSIONS

[[CHILD|Majo]] presents with a bilingual speech sound disorder and language delay affecting both Spanish and English, with error patterns and standard scores below expectations in both languages. Because deficits are present across both of her languages rather than limited to the less-dominant one, this pattern is consistent with a true communication disorder rather than a difference attributable solely to second-language acquisition or dialectal variation. [[CHILD|Majo]] meets criteria for speech and language services under [[KEEP|ICD-10]] codes [[KEEP|F80.1]] (expressive language disorder) and [[KEEP|F80.0]] (phonological disorder).

Given [[CHILD|Majo]]'s young age, bilingual environment, and the family's strong use of Spanish at home, intervention should be delivered in both languages, with primary emphasis on Spanish given its role as the dominant home language and the language in which [[CHILD|Majo]]'s grandmother, [[FAMILY|Rosa]], and babysitter, [[FAMILY|Yolanda]], primarily interact with her.

RECOMENDACIONES / RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Individual speech-language therapy twice weekly, [[KEEP|30]] minutes per session, targeting phonological processes (final consonant deletion, cluster reduction) and expressive morphosyntax in both Spanish and English, billed under [[KEEP|CPT 92507]].
2. Initial evaluation billed under [[KEEP|CPT 92523]] (speech sound production and language evaluation).
3. A [[KEEP|Cycles]] phonological approach is recommended given the number and consistency of error patterns identified.
4. Continued coordination with [[PROVIDER|Monica Díaz]] at the [[AGENCY|Catawba County Partnership for Children]] regarding Early Intervention eligibility and IFSP Outcome [[KEEP|2]] (communication) development.
5. Classroom-based strategies for Ms. [[TEACHER|Davis]] and Srta. [[TEACHER|Pérez]] at [[DAYCARE|Little Sprouts Early Learning Center]], including modeling full-word productions and allowing extra response time.
6. Home strategies for [[PARENT|Carmen]] and [[PARENT|Javier]], including modeling target sounds in context rather than correcting [[CHILD|Majo]] directly, and encouraging extended family members such as [[FAMILY|Rosa]] and [[FAMILY|Yolanda]] to maintain rich Spanish language input.
7. Re-evaluation in [[KEEP|6]] months to monitor progress across both languages.
8. Referral to [[AGENCY|Catawba County DSS]] and [[AGENCY|WIC]] services, which the family is already connected with, to ensure continuity of support services.

This evaluation was conducted in accordance with best practices for bilingual assessment, using normed tools in both of [[CHILD|Majo]]'s languages and considering her full linguistic repertoire rather than evaluating English proficiency alone.
