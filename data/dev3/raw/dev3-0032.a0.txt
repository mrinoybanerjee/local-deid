Summary: [[CHILD|Mateo]] is a [[KEEP|4;2]]-year-old bilingual (Spanish/English) boy referred by [[AGENCY|Piedmont CDSA]] for a comprehensive speech-language evaluation due to parent and daycare concern regarding intelligibility, expressive language delay, and limited mean length of utterance in both languages. This evaluation included standardized and informal measures in both English and Spanish, parent interview conducted in Spanish with interpreter support, and classroom observation. Results indicate a moderate-to-severe mixed receptive-expressive language disorder and a moderate speech sound disorder affecting intelligibility across both languages, consistent with a true bilingual language impairment rather than second-language acquisition alone.

Antecedentes / Background

[[CHILD|Mateo]] lives with his mother, [[PARENT|Lucía Flores Ramírez]], and his older brother in a rented duplex at [[PLACE|412 Hartley Lane]], [[PLACE|Clayton]], [[PLACE|27520]]. His parents separated when he was an infant; his father, [[PARENT|Jorge Hernández]], lives in a different state and has limited in-person contact, though he speaks with [[CHILD|Mateo]] by phone on Sundays. [[PARENT|Lucía]] works two jobs to support the family: she is a housekeeper for [[ORG|Sparkle Clean Services]] during the day and takes an overnight shift three nights a week at [[ORG|IHOP]]. Because of this schedule, [[CHILD|Mateo]]'s primary daytime caregiver during the week is his grandmother, [[FAMILY|Rosa Ramírez]], who speaks only Spanish to him, while his evenings and weekends are split between [[FAMILY|Rosa]] and his mother. [[CHILD|Mateo]]'s brother, [[SIBLING|Diego]], age seven, attends [[SCHOOL|Clayton Elementary]] and often plays with [[CHILD|Mateo]] at home; their play was referenced repeatedly during the evaluation.

[[PARENT|Lucía]] reported, in Spanish, "[[CHILD|Mateo]] no habla mucho, pero cuando quiere algo lo pide a su manera." She described [[CHILD|Mateo]] as an affectionate, social child who prefers to play with his family's dog, [[PET|Canela]], and who becomes frustrated when he cannot make himself understood, sometimes hitting or crying. [[FAMILY|Rosa]] reported similar concerns, noting that unfamiliar adults frequently cannot understand [[CHILD|Mateo]] even when his family can.

Procedimientos de Evaluación / Evaluation Procedures

This evaluation was conducted across two sessions, totaling approximately [[KEEP|3 hours]] of direct assessment, at [[TIME|9:30 a.m.]] and [[TIME|1:15 p.m.]] respectively, supplemented by caregiver interview, classroom observation, and review of prior [[AGENCY|Piedmont CDSA]] records. Standardized measures were administered in English and, where normed or informally translated, in Spanish, with interpretation support from [[PROVIDER|Marisol Peña]] for portions of parent interview. Given the lack of fully normed Spanish equivalents for several tools, Spanish administration was supplemented with dynamic assessment and language sample analysis to reduce bias against [[CHILD|Mateo]]'s dominant language.

Resultados de las Pruebas / Test Results

Cognitive-Developmental Screening

The [[KEEP|Bayley Scales of Infant and Toddler Development, Fourth Edition]] (Bayley-4), Cognitive subtest, was administered to screen nonverbal cognition given [[CHILD|Mateo]]'s age falling near the upper boundary of the tool's typical range. [[CHILD|Mateo]] obtained a Cognitive scaled score of [[KEEP|9]] ([[KEEP|37th percentile]]), within the average range, suggesting that his language difficulties are not attributable to a global cognitive delay.

Receptive and Expressive Language — English

The [[KEEP|Preschool Language Scales, Fifth Edition]] (PLS-5) was administered in English. [[CHILD|Mateo]] obtained an Auditory Comprehension standard score of [[KEEP|78]] ([[KEEP|7th percentile]]; [[KEEP|95% CI 71-85]]) and an Expressive Communication standard score of [[KEEP|65]] ([[KEEP|1st percentile]]; [[KEEP|95% CI 58-72]]), yielding a Total Language standard score of [[KEEP|69]] ([[KEEP|2nd percentile]]). Age equivalents were [[KEEP|3;0]] for Auditory Comprehension and [[KEEP|2;6]] for Expressive Communication.

Receptive and Expressive Language — Spanish and Bilingual Measures

The [[KEEP|Rossetti Infant-Toddler Language Scale]] was used informally in Spanish to supplement standardized findings given its broader age-referenced items and suitability for dynamic, play-based assessment. [[CHILD|Mateo]]'s performance across the Interaction-Attachment, Pragmatics, Gesture, Play, Language Comprehension, and Language Expression domains was consistent with functioning at approximately the [[KEEP|2;6–3;0]] age range in Spanish, with comprehension stronger than expression across all domains, mirroring the English profile.

A spontaneous language sample of [[KEEP|100 utterances]] was collected in each language during play with toy animals, a play kitchen, and a picture book. In Spanish, mean length of utterance was [[KEEP|2.1 morphemes]]; in English, [[KEEP|1.6 morphemes]]. Across both languages, [[CHILD|Mateo]] used primarily single words and early two-word combinations ("[[KEEP|más agua]]," "[[KEEP|no quiero]]," "mommy go"), with frequent omission of grammatical morphemes (articles, plurals, verb tense markers) beyond what is expected for typical bilingual development at this age. He used possessives inconsistently, producing "[[KEEP|mi perro]]" but also "the ball Diego" for "[[SIBLING|Diego]]'s ball," omitting the possessive marker.

Several utterances referenced family members and the family pet by name, illustrating both his vocabulary and his morphosyntactic limitations: he said "[[SIBLING|Diego]] run" when his brother ran past during play, "[[PET|Canela]] sit" when directing the family dog in a toy scenario, and, in Spanish, "[[SIBLING|Diego]] no" when protesting a turn-taking rule during a game. These productions were counted toward the language sample but were not scored for intelligibility given their functional, communicative intent.

Articulation and Phonology

The [[KEEP|Goldman-Fristoe Test of Articulation, Third Edition]] (GFTA-3) was administered in English, yielding a raw score of [[KEEP|34]] and a standard score of [[KEEP|72]] ([[KEEP|3rd percentile]]). A parallel informal Spanish phonological screening was conducted using single-word picture naming. Across both languages, [[CHILD|Mateo]] demonstrated the following error patterns: fronting of velars (/k/ → [t], /g/ → [d]), stopping of fricatives (/s/ → [t]), final consonant deletion, and liquid simplification, with /ɾ/ produced as [d] in Spanish words such as "[[KEEP|pero]]" rendered as "[[KEEP|pedo]]" in context (noted as a clinically significant but developmentally common substitution at this age). Intelligibility in connected speech was judged to be approximately [[KEEP|40%]] to unfamiliar listeners and [[KEEP|70%]] to familiar listeners across both languages, which is consistent with caregiver report.

Stimulability testing showed that [[CHILD|Mateo]] could produce /k/ and /s/ in isolation with [[KEEP|moderate cues]] but not spontaneously in words, suggesting a favorable prognosis for these sounds with direct intervention. He required [[KEEP|maximal cues]] for /ɾ/ and liquid clusters.

Vocabulary

The [[KEEP|Peabody Picture Vocabulary Test, Fifth Edition]] (PPVT-5) was administered in English, yielding a standard score of [[KEEP|74]] ([[KEEP|4th percentile]]). An informal Spanish receptive vocabulary checklist completed by [[PARENT|Lucía]] and [[FAMILY|Rosa]] suggested stronger receptive vocabulary in Spanish than reflected by the English PPVT-5 score, consistent with [[CHILD|Mateo]]'s reported home language dominance.

Impresiones / Impressions

Based on standardized and informal assessment across English and Spanish, parent and teacher report, and direct observation, [[CHILD|Mateo]] presents with a moderate-to-severe mixed receptive-expressive language disorder affecting both of his languages, and a moderate speech sound disorder characterized by multiple phonological process errors that reduce intelligibility to unfamiliar listeners. Nonverbal cognitive screening is within normal limits, supporting that language and speech deficits are specific rather than secondary to global developmental delay. Because deficits are present across both of [[CHILD|Mateo]]'s languages relative to same-age bilingual peers, and because his rate of development has lagged consistently since early intervention eligibility, his profile is consistent with a true language impairment rather than a difference attributable solely to simultaneous bilingual acquisition or reduced English exposure.

[[CHILD|Mateo]]'s strengths include relatively stronger receptive language and comprehension in Spanish, good social engagement, symbolic play skills within normal limits, favorable stimulability for several misarticulated sounds, and a supportive, engaged family system, including his mother, grandmother, and brother, all of whom provide rich language models and consistent routines. Risk factors include history of prematurity, NICU stay, recurrent otitis media during a critical period of early language development, and limited availability of Spanish-English bilingual intervention resources locally, compounded by his mother's demanding work schedule across two jobs, which limits her availability for carryover activities despite her strong motivation and engagement throughout this evaluation.

2. Speech sound disorder, phonological, moderate ([[KEEP|ICD-10 F80.0]])

Recomendaciones / Recommendations

1. [[CHILD|Mateo]] is recommended for direct speech-language therapy a minimum of [[KEEP|two times]] per week, [[KEEP|30 minutes]] per session, provided bilingually or with coordinated bilingual support, targeting expressive syntax, morphology, phonology, and functional vocabulary in both Spanish and English. Services should be billed under [[KEEP|CPT 92523]] for combined evaluation of speech and language and [[KEEP|CPT 92507]] for individual treatment sessions going forward.
2. Given [[CHILD|Mateo]]'s age, referral is recommended to the local public school system for a Developmental Disabilities (Developmental Delay) eligibility determination and consideration for a Preschool Handicapped placement, with evaluation to be coordinated through [[AGENCY|Piedmont CDSA]] and [[PROVIDER|Angela Smith]] prior to his transition from Part C to Part B services.
3. A home program emphasizing [[KEEP|Hanen]]-based strategies ("It Takes Two to Talk") is recommended to support [[PARENT|Lucía]], [[FAMILY|Rosa]], and [[SIBLING|Diego]] in providing consistent language-rich interaction during daily routines, with written materials provided in Spanish. Given [[PARENT|Lucía]]'s work schedule, home program activities should be brief ([[KEEP|5-10 minutes]]) and embeddable into existing routines such as bath time and mealtime, with [[FAMILY|Rosa]] identified as the primary carryover agent on weekdays.
4. A [[KEEP|PROMPT]]-informed approach is recommended to address [[CHILD|Mateo]]'s speech sound errors given his favorable stimulability profile, focusing initially on velar sounds and final consonants, which showed the best response to [[KEEP|moderate cues]] during this evaluation.
5. Continued monitoring of hearing status is recommended given history of recurrent otitis media; a repeat audiological evaluation is recommended if any additional ear infections occur, and the family should maintain follow-up with [[PROVIDER|Dr. Patricia Nguyen]].
6. Coordination between [[DAYCARE|Little Sprouts Bilingual Daycare]] staff, including Ms. [[TEACHER|Brown]] and the bilingual classroom aide, and the treating speech-language pathologist is recommended to ensure consistent strategies across home, daycare, and therapy settings, including use of visual supports and repeated modeling of target structures in both languages.
7. Re-evaluation is recommended in [[KEEP|6 months]] to monitor progress and determine continued eligibility and appropriate service intensity, with specific attention to rate of growth in Spanish given its status as [[CHILD|Mateo]]'s dominant home language.
8. The family should be connected, if not already enrolled, with [[AGENCY|WIC]] and [[AGENCY|Smart Start]] programs locally to support broader developmental needs, and the possibility of respite or childcare support should be explored with [[AGENCY|Piedmont CDSA]] given [[PARENT|Lucía]]'s work demands across her two jobs.
