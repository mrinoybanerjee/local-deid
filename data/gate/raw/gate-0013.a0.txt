Outcomes, strategies, and services

Outcome one: By [[DATE|January 12, 2027]], [[CHILD|Nico]] will communicate a choice, request, or request for help during breakfast, dressing, and play in [[KEEP|8/10]] naturally occurring opportunities across two consecutive weeks, using any effective combination of gestures, pictures, signs, or words, with no more than [[KEEP|min cues]]. The family will select one manageable opportunity within each routine. Adults will position choices where the child can see them, model a short message once, and allow a comfortable pause. They will acknowledge the first meaningful signal and provide the requested assistance when possible. Success will reflect communication that works, including Spanish and English messages.

For this outcome, the speech-language pathologist will coach the parents through demonstration, guided practice, and reflection. Models may include [[KEEP|"help me"]], [[KEEP|"más agua"]], and [[KEEP|"open please"]], adjusted to the child’s current attempts. Adults will expand a message without requiring repetition. If the child becomes distressed, the adult will address the immediate need and return to practice later. Mother and father will record a brief example on three days each week, noting the routine, message, and support. The clinician will review these examples during visits and compare performance across caregivers. The family may use spoken notes instead of written logs when that fits their schedule.

Outcome two: By [[DATE|January 12, 2027]], [[CHILD|Nico]] will participate in a shared play sequence with another child for at least [[KEEP|3]] reciprocal turns in [[KEEP|4/5]] opportunities across home and daycare routines, with [[KEEP|min cues]]. Adults will begin with familiar activities, such as rolling a ball, feeding a toy animal, or adding blocks to a tower. Teachers will model [[KEEP|"my turn"]], [[KEEP|"your turn"]], or a matching gesture. Classmate [[PEER|Maya]] may participate when she chooses, and cousin [[PEER|Teo]] may join during family visits. Other children will remain play partners with their own choices and interests.

The team will define a reciprocal turn as an action or message that responds to the partner and allows the interaction to continue. Taking an object without responding will not count as a reciprocal turn. Adults will reduce language demands when the activity becomes difficult and restore an enjoyable rhythm before adding another model. Classroom staff will record one brief example weekly. The clinician will compare classroom reports with family examples and observe at least one group routine before the next review. The team will also note whether the child seeks a partner independently. Longer play will be encouraged when enjoyable, without making duration the only measure of success.

Outcome three: By [[DATE|January 12, 2027]], [[CHILD|Nico]] will signal discomfort or a need for a break during dressing, cleanup, and classroom transitions in [[KEEP|4/5]] observed opportunities across two weeks, with [[KEEP|min cues]].
