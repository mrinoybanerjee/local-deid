**Articulation/Phonology Observations:**
[[CHILD|Mateo]] demonstrated fronting of velar sounds in both languages, producing [[KEEP|[t]]] for [[KEEP|/k/]] (e.g., "tasa" for target "[[KEEP|casa]]") and [[KEEP|[d]]] for [[KEEP|/g/]] (e.g., "dato" for target "[[KEEP|gato]]"). The Spanish tap [[KEEP|/ɾ/]] was produced inconsistently, often omitted in medial position (e.g., "paa" for target "[[KEEP|para]]"). These patterns are consistent with a phonological process disorder rather than an isolated articulation delay, and notably affect both of [[CHILD|Mateo]]'s languages, supporting a true language-based difficulty rather than a second-language-acquisition pattern alone.

[[CHILD|Mateo]] required [[KEEP|moderate cues]] to imitate multisyllabic words accurately and achieved correct productions on [[KEEP|4 of 10]] trials ([[KEEP|40%]]) with [[KEEP|min-mod cues]] during structured probes.

**9. CLINICAL IMPRESSIONS / Impresiones Clínicas**

Results of this bilingual evaluation indicate that [[CHILD|Mateo]] presents with a **mixed receptive-expressive language disorder** affecting both English and Spanish, alongside a co-occurring **phonological process disorder**. Because deficits are present across both of [[CHILD|Mateo]]'s languages at comparable severity, these findings reflect a true language disorder rather than a typical pattern of sequential bilingual acquisition or a language difference attributable to reduced exposure.

Per [[KEEP|ICD-10]] code [[KEEP|F80.2]] (Mixed Receptive-Expressive Language Disorder) and [[KEEP|F80.0]] (Phonological Disorder), [[CHILD|Mateo]] demonstrates significant delays in expressive vocabulary, grammatical morphology, sentence formulation, and speech sound production relative to same-age peers in both languages. As [[PARENT|Lucía]] summarized during the feedback session, "Entonces no es que esté confundido por los dos idiomas — de verdad necesita ayuda." This is an accurate characterization: bilingualism is not the cause of [[CHILD|Mateo]]'s difficulties, and continued exposure to both English and Spanish at home should be strongly encouraged.

[[CHILD|Mateo]]'s social communication, play skills, and nonverbal engagement are notable strengths and support a positive prognosis with intervention. His family's engagement with community supports — including [[AGENCY|Wake County CDSA]], [[AGENCY|El Puente Hispanic Family Center]], and the [[AGENCY|Wake County Public Libraries]] story-time program — provides a strong foundation for carryover of therapy strategies into daily routines.

**10. RECOMMENDATIONS / Recomendaciones**

1. [[CHILD|Mateo]] qualifies for continued early intervention services and should transition to a speech-language therapy plan addressing both languages, with services provided in Spanish, English, or both as appropriate, ideally by a bilingual provider or with consistent interpreter support (e.g., continued collaboration with [[PROVIDER|Tom Alvarez]]).

2. Individual speech-language therapy is recommended at a frequency of **two sessions per week, 30 minutes each**, targeting:
   - Expressive vocabulary growth in both languages
   - Two- and three-word combinations and basic grammatical morphemes (plurals, present progressive -ing, Spanish verb conjugations)
   - Phonological awareness and velar sound production ([[KEEP|/k/]], [[KEEP|/g/]]) and the Spanish tap [[KEEP|/ɾ/]]
   - Functional communication strategies to reduce frustration-related behaviors

3. A [[KEEP|Cycles]] approach to phonological intervention is recommended given the pattern of multiple affected processes across both languages.

4. Continue bilingual language exposure at home. Parents and [[FAMILY|doña Lupe]] should continue speaking their strongest, most comfortable language (Spanish) with [[CHILD|Mateo]], as this supports — rather than hinders — overall language development.

5. Coordinate with [[TEACHER|la maestra Carmen]] and [[TEACHER|Miss Ana]] at [[DAYCARE|El Nido de Sueños Early Learning]] to implement classroom strategies, including modeling expanded utterances and offering structured choices to reduce reliance on gesture alone.

6. Continue coordination with [[PROVIDER|Jennifer Shaw]] (OT) and [[PROVIDER|Mark Delgado]] (PT) regarding any overlapping sensorimotor goals, and maintain communication with [[PROVIDER|Dr. Angela Ruiz]] regarding developmental screening follow-up.

7. Re-evaluate in **6 months**, or sooner if progress plateaus, to monitor growth in both languages and reassess the need for continued services as [[CHILD|Mateo]] approaches kindergarten entry.
