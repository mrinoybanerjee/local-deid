Clinical Impressions

[[CHILD|Teo]] presents with a bilingual speech sound disorder and expressive language disorder affecting both Spanish and English, consistent with a true communication disorder rather than solely second-language acquisition, as deficits are present in his dominant home language (Spanish) as well as English. His receptive language skills, while below age expectations, are relatively stronger than his expressive skills, suggesting a primarily expressive profile with secondary mild receptive involvement. His speech intelligibility deficits, characterized by consistent phonological process use (fronting, cluster reduction, gliding), are significant enough to interfere with communication across both familiar and unfamiliar listeners and likely contribute to the social withdrawal and reduced verbal participation reported by Ms. [[TEACHER|Patterson]] at school.

Given his history of chronic otitis media and tympanostomy tube placement, it is possible that early fluctuating hearing loss contributed to delayed phonological development, though current hearing is within normal limits. The combination of speech sound disorder and language delay places [[CHILD|Teo]] at risk for later academic difficulties, including early literacy challenges, if not addressed promptly. His relative social strengths, including appropriate eye contact, turn-taking attempts, and interest in peers such as [[PEER|Dylan]] and [[PEER|Emma W.]], are encouraging prognostic indicators.
