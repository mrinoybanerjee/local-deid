**Speech Sound Production**

[[CHILD|Mateo]]'s connected speech was estimated to be approximately [[KEEP|40%]] intelligible to unfamiliar listeners in English and approximately [[KEEP|55%]] intelligible in Spanish, with context available to the listener. Single-word intelligibility in Spanish was higher, at approximately [[KEEP|8/10]] words understood by the examiner without the gloss, compared with roughly [[KEEP|5/10]] in English.

Phonological processes observed in Spanish included final consonant deletion, stopping of fricatives, and liquid simplification, including gliding of /ɾ/ to [w] and deletion of /s/ in consonant clusters. On a single-word Spanish articulation probe, [[CHILD|Mateo]] was shown a picture of the sun and asked to name it; his target word was [[KEEP|"sol"]], which he produced as [[KEEP|"to"]], omitting the final liquid consistent with the pattern noted above. The clinician noted that this target happens to be homophonous with the name of [[CHILD|Mateo]]'s sister, [[SIBLING|Sol Hernández Martínez]], and confirmed with the family, through the interpreter, [[PROVIDER|Diana Castillo]], that [[CHILD|Mateo]] did not appear to confuse the word with his sister's name during testing, nor did he reference her when prompted for the target.

In English, [[CHILD|Mateo]] demonstrated fronting of /k/ and /g/ to [t] and [d] respectively (for example, "cat" produced as [[KEEP|"tat"]]), cluster reduction, and final consonant deletion. He produced /θ/ as [f], consistent with common developmental patterns among English learners of his age. Stimulability testing showed that [[CHILD|Mateo]] could produce /k/ in isolation with [[KEEP|moderate cues]] and /s/ in the initial position with [[KEEP|minimal cues]], suggesting good candidacy for direct therapy using a [[KEEP|Cycles]] approach targeting phonological patterns across both languages.

**Language Sample**

A spontaneous language sample was collected in both languages during structured play with toy animals and a farm set, with [[PROVIDER|Diana Castillo]] interpreting for the Spanish portions. In Spanish, [[CHILD|Mateo]] produced primarily single words and occasional two-word combinations (for example, "más agua," "no quiero"), with a mean length of utterance of approximately [[KEEP|1.8 morphemes]]. In English, his utterances were almost exclusively single words, with a mean length of utterance of approximately [[KEEP|1.2 morphemes]]. He used gesture and pointing extensively to supplement verbal output in both languages, particularly when frustrated or when a listener did not understand him on the first attempt.

[[CHILD|Mateo]] demonstrated understanding of basic spatial concepts (in, on, under) and several body part names in both languages, but had difficulty with multi-step directions, temporal concepts (before, after), and quantitative concepts (more, less) in formal testing, consistent with teacher report from Ms. [[TEACHER|Lopez]]. He initiated communication with the examiner and interpreter spontaneously but relied on single words or short phrases rather than extended utterances, even when given models.

**Impressions**

Based on standardized testing, parent and teacher report, and clinical observation, [[CHILD|Mateo]] presents with a moderate-to-severe mixed receptive-expressive language disorder affecting both Spanish and English ([[KEEP|ICD-10 F80.2]]), along with a moderate speech sound disorder affecting both languages ([[KEEP|ICD-10 F80.0]]). His difficulties are present across both languages and cannot be attributed solely to the process of bilingual acquisition, supporting a true disorder rather than typical second-language learning differences. Reduced intelligibility, limited expressive vocabulary, and difficulty with multi-step directions are consistent with, and likely contributing to, the concerns raised by his family and by his teacher, [[TEACHER|Jennifer Lopez]].

[[CHILD|Mateo]]'s strengths include social engagement, appropriate use of compensatory gesture, and stimulability for several error sounds with cueing, all of which are favorable prognostic indicators for intervention. His history of recurrent otitis media during a key period of early language development is also a relevant contributing factor to consider in the overall clinical picture, though current hearing is within normal limits.

**Recommendations**

1. [[CHILD|Mateo]] is recommended to continue speech-language therapy services under his IFSP, with a change in frequency from once weekly to twice weekly, [[KEEP|30]]-minute sessions, to address both expressive and receptive language goals and speech sound production goals in both Spanish and English.
2. Therapy should employ a [[KEEP|Cycles]] phonological approach for speech sound goals and incorporate [[KEEP|Hanen]]-based strategies to support parent-implemented language facilitation in the home.
