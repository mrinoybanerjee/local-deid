SPEECH SOUND PRODUCTION / PRODUCCIÓN DE SONIDOS DEL HABLA

In English, [[CHILD|Mateo]] demonstrated inconsistent production of /s/, /r/, /θ/, and consonant clusters, with frequent final consonant deletion and cluster reduction (e.g., "poon" for "spoon," "ca" for "cat"). In Spanish, similar patterns of final consonant deletion and liquid simplification were noted, particularly on words containing /ɾ/ and /l/ in medial and final positions.

During a structured naming task using family-relevant vocabulary, the evaluator elicited the target word "sol" (sun) using a picture of the sun, deliberately avoiding any reference to [[CHILD|Mateo]]'s sister, [[SIBLING|Sol Hernández]], to prevent confusion between the common noun and the family member's name. [[CHILD|Mateo]] produced the target word "sol" as "[[KEEP|[to]]]" with moderate cues, omitting the final /l/ consistent with his broader pattern of final consonant deletion. When later asked informally, "¿Y cómo se llama tu hermana?" (And what is your sister's name?), he responded clearly, "[[SIBLING|Sol]]," correctly producing the final /l/ in the proper noun context — suggesting the earlier omission was phonological rather than lexical.

Additional single-word productions with cueing level noted:

IMPRESSIONS / IMPRESIONES

[[CHILD|Mateo]] presents with a bilingual (Spanish-English) speech sound disorder and mixed receptive-expressive language disorder affecting both of his languages. His performance on standardized measures in Spanish, his language of primary exposure, fell within the below-average to significantly below-average range across comprehension, expression, and morphosyntax, which rules out a primary explanation of "second language acquisition" or "insufficient English exposure." Because deficits are present in both languages relative to same-age monolingual and bilingual norms, this profile is consistent with a true developmental language disorder (DLD) rather than typical bilingual language development or second-language learning alone.

His speech sound errors (final consonant deletion, cluster reduction, liquid simplification) are also present across both languages and are judged to reflect a phonological disorder rather than dialectal variation or typical bilingual phonological patterns. Intelligibility deficits are functionally significant, contributing to peer frustration, caregiver burden (siblings acting as informal interpreters), and anticipated difficulty in the transition to an English-medium kindergarten classroom at [[SCHOOL|Walnut Creek Elementary School]].

[[CHILD|Mateo]]'s strengths include strong social engagement with familiar partners, intact joint attention and nonverbal communication, supportive extended family involvement (including doña [[FAMILY|Lupe]]), and a consistent, engaged home language environment. These are considered strong prognostic indicators for continued intervention.
