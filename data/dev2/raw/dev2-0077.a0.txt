CLINICAL IMPRESSIONS

- Results of this bilingual speech-language evaluation indicate a significant expressive language delay and a moderate receptive language delay across both Spanish and English, consistent with a diagnosis of Developmental Language Disorder.
- [[CHILD|Mateo]]'s delay is evident in both languages and cannot be attributed solely to typical bilingual acquisition patterns; combined vocabulary across languages remains well below age expectations.
- Cognitive and play skills are age-appropriate, supporting a primary speech-language diagnosis rather than a broader developmental concern at this time.
- Limited phonetic inventory and reliance on early-developing sounds in both languages suggest an associated speech sound delay, though formal articulation scoring was not possible due to restricted verbal output.
- Hearing has been appropriately screened and ruled out as a contributing factor.
- [[CHILD|Mateo]] meets eligibility criteria for Early Intervention services under North Carolina's Infant-Toddler Program based on a [[KEEP|33%]] delay in the communication domain, as calculated from standardized assessment results above.
- Family engagement, home language support, and participation in community programs including [[ORG|Iglesia Bautista Monte Sion]] and [[AGENCY|Durham Early Head Start]] are notable strengths that will support intervention.

RECOMMENDATIONS

- [[CHILD|Mateo]] is recommended for ongoing speech-language therapy services through the Early Intervention system, with a recommended frequency of two sessions per week, [[KEEP|30 minutes]] each, in a bilingual service delivery model.
- Treatment should address both Spanish and English expressive vocabulary growth, early phonological patterns, and functional communication strategies such as requesting and protesting using words rather than gestures alone.
- A total communication approach, including modeling of core vocabulary and incorporation of a few basic signs, is recommended to reduce frustration while verbal output develops.
- The family should continue to use Spanish as the primary home language; there is no evidence that bilingual exposure is contributing to [[CHILD|Mateo]]'s delay, and continued dual-language input is supported.
- Coordination with [[TEACHER|Amanda Lopez]] at [[AGENCY|Durham Early Head Start]] is recommended to ensure consistent communication strategies across home and classroom settings.
- Reassessment of articulation and phonology is recommended in approximately six months, once expressive vocabulary has increased sufficiently to allow formal scoring on the [[KEEP|GFTA-3]] or a parallel Spanish measure.
- Continued audiological monitoring is recommended given the speech-language delay, even though current screening results were normal.
- The family may benefit from continued participation in library-based bilingual literacy programs, such as the Spanish storytime at the [[ORG|Durham County Public Library]] South Branch, to support language-rich exposure outside of therapy.
- Referral to a bilingual parent coaching resource through [[AGENCY|Smart Start of Durham County]] is suggested to reinforce home-based language strategies.
