On the [[KEEP|PLS-5]], [[CHILD|Mateo]] achieved a Total Language standard score of [[KEEP|73]] ([[KEEP|73-83]] confidence interval), falling within the Below Average range and indicating delays in both receptive and expressive language relative to same-age peers in both languages. IMPRESSIONS

[[CHILD|Mateo]] presents with a moderate-to-severe speech sound disorder and moderate expressive and mild-to-moderate receptive language delay, evident across both English and Spanish and therefore consistent with a true communication disorder rather than a typical pattern of bilingual language development or second-language acquisition. His history of chronic otitis media, resolved with bilateral myringotomy tube placement under the care of [[PROVIDER|Dr. Priya Nair]] at [[HOSPITAL|UNC Health Rex Ear, Nose & Throat and Audiology, Rex Hospital Campus]], likely contributed to early speech and language delays, though current audiological status is within normal limits per [[PROVIDER|Dr. Megan Caldwell]]'s most recent evaluation. His history of prematurity, with a NICU stay at [[HOSPITAL|WakeMed Cary Hospital]] under the care of [[PROVIDER|Dr. Linda Chao]], is also a known risk factor for speech-language delay. [[CHILD|Mateo]]'s strengths include strong social communication skills, good attention and engagement during structured tasks, intact oral-motor structures, and a supportive bilingual home and school environment.

[[CHILD|Mateo]] meets eligibility criteria for continued speech-language services under the category of Speech or Language Impairment as he transitions from [[AGENCY|Wake County CDSA]] Part C services to Part B preschool special education services coordinated through [[SCHOOL|Wake County Public School System]].

RECOMMENDATIONS

1. [[CHILD|Mateo]] should receive direct speech-language therapy services, recommended at a minimum of twice weekly for 30-minute sessions, targeting articulation and expressive language goals in both English and Spanish.
2. Therapy should utilize a cycles approach ([[KEEP|Cycles]]) for phonological pattern remediation, targeting final consonant deletion and cluster reduction as priority patterns, supplemented by minimal pairs activities.
3. A combination of [[KEEP|PROMPT]] and traditional articulation therapy techniques is recommended to address placement and manner errors for [[KEEP|/ɾ/]], [[KEEP|/l/]], and consonant clusters in both languages.
4. [[KEEP|Hanen]]-based strategies (It Takes Two to Talk) are recommended for home carryover, with coaching provided to [[PARENT|Lucía Rodríguez]], [[PARENT|Diego Flores]], and babysitter [[FAMILY|Yesenia Torres]] to support language modeling in the home.
5. Continued collaboration with Ms. [[TEACHER|Patricia Lopez]] and classroom staff at [[SCHOOL|Oak Grove Elementary School]] is recommended, including visual supports and bilingual direction-giving strategies to support [[CHILD|Mateo]]'s comprehension in the classroom.
6. Continued monitoring of hearing status is recommended given history of chronic otitis media; routine audiological re-evaluation with [[PROVIDER|Dr. Megan Caldwell]] or another audiologist at [[HOSPITAL|UNC Health Rex Ear, Nose & Throat and Audiology, Rex Hospital Campus]] is recommended if any recurrence of ear infection symptoms occurs.
7. Referral for an updated IFSP/IEP transition meeting is recommended, to be coordinated by [[PROVIDER|Brianna Hayes]] at [[AGENCY|Wake County CDSA]], in collaboration with [[SCHOOL|Wake County Public School System]] Exceptional Children's staff.
8. Family should continue to use both Spanish and English consistently in the home, as there is no evidence that bilingual exposure is contributing to [[CHILD|Mateo]]'s delays; rather, a true underlying speech-language disorder is present across both languages.
