[[CHILD|Mateo]] demonstrated the following phonological patterns across both languages: final consonant deletion (e.g., "[[KEEP|mas]]" for [[KEEP|/mas/]] produced as [[KEEP|[ma]]]), stopping of fricatives (e.g., [[KEEP|/s/]] realized as [[KEEP|[t]]]), and gliding of liquids (e.g., [[KEEP|/ɾ/]] realized as [[KEEP|[w]]] in words such as [[KEEP|"carro"]], produced as [[KEEP|[kawo]]]). IMPRESSIONS

[[CHILD|Mateo]] presents with a mixed receptive-expressive language disorder and a phonological process disorder affecting both English and Spanish, consistent with [[KEEP|ICD-10]] codes [[KEEP|F80.2]] (mixed receptive-expressive language disorder) and [[KEEP|F80.0]] (phonological disorder). Deficits were observed across both languages and across multiple measures, including conceptually scored standardized testing, bilingual criterion-referenced measures, and naturalistic language sampling, supporting a true language disorder rather than a language difference attributable to bilingual or dialectal variation. [[CHILD|Mateo]]'s history of chronic otitis media and NICU stay, along with his family history of later-emerging language in [[SIBLING|Sol]] per parent report, are considered contributing but not sole explanatory factors, as [[CHILD|Mateo]]'s current profile is more significantly delayed than typical bilingual acquisition patterns would predict at [[KEEP|4;2]].

[[CHILD|Mateo]]'s strengths include good attention, strong nonverbal communication and gesture use, willingness to engage in structured tasks, and responsiveness to cueing, all of which are favorable prognostic indicators. Family involvement, including support from [[PARENT|Lucía]], [[PARENT|Carlos]], [[FAMILY|doña Lupe]], and [[SIBLING|Sol]], is a significant strength and should be leveraged in treatment planning.
