Impresiones / Clinical Impressions

Based on standardized testing, parent report, and clinical observation, [[CHILD|Mateo]] presents with a mixed receptive-expressive language disorder, consistent with [[KEEP|ICD-10 code F80.2]], affecting both his Spanish and English language systems. His speech sound production also falls significantly below expected norms for his age, consistent with a moderate phonological disorder. These findings are not attributable to hearing loss, given his documented normal audiological results, nor fully explained by reduced exposure, as [[CHILD|Mateo]] receives consistent language input in the home in Spanish and increasing exposure to English at [[DAYCARE|Little Sprouts Bilingual Daycare]]. His difficulties appear to reflect a true language-learning disability affecting both languages roughly proportionally, rather than a language difference secondary to bilingual acquisition alone.

[[CHILD|Mateo]]'s limited intelligibility and reduced expressive output likely contribute to his increased frustration and reliance on [[SIBLING|Valentina]] to communicate for him, a pattern that may reduce his opportunities to practice independent verbal expression. Continued speech-language intervention is clinically indicated.

Recomendaciones / Recommendations

1. [[CHILD|Mateo]] should receive individual speech-language therapy twice weekly, billed under [[KEEP|CPT 92523]], provided in both Spanish and English by a bilingual speech-language pathologist, to address expressive vocabulary, two-step direction following, and speech sound production.

2. This clinician recommends a [[KEEP|Cycles]] phonological approach to target final consonant deletion and fronting patterns, alternating target sound classes across sessions to support generalization.

3. [[KEEP|PROMPT]] techniques may be incorporated to provide tactile-kinesthetic cueing for [[CHILD|Mateo]]'s motor planning difficulties with [[KEEP|/k/]] and [[KEEP|/ɾ/]].

4. Given [[CHILD|Mateo]]'s reliance on gesture and his sister's interpreting on his behalf, introducing a structured augmentative system such as [[KEEP|PECS]] during the initial phase of intervention may reduce communication frustration and support more independent requesting, with a planned fade as verbal output increases.

5. Parent coaching is strongly recommended. Given [[PARENT|Lucía Rodríguez]]'s demanding work schedule across two jobs, flexible scheduling and a brief, high-yield home program are essential. This clinician recommends [[KEEP|Hanen]]-based strategies, specifically the [[KEEP|It Takes Two to Talk]] program, adapted for bilingual delivery with support from interpreter [[PROVIDER|Yesenia Campos]] as needed, so that [[FAMILY|Rosa]] and [[PARENT|Lucía Rodríguez]] can both participate in brief coaching sessions during existing childcare hours.
