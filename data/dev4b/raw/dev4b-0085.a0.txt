SPEECH SOUND PRODUCTION AND INTELLIGIBILITY

- Connected speech intelligibility was judged at approximately [[KEEP|50%]] for unfamiliar listeners and [[KEEP|80%]] for familiar listeners, consistent with parent report.
- Consistent phonological processes included final consonant deletion, fronting of velars, and cluster reduction, observed in both Spanish and English samples.
- [[CHILD|Teo]] produced /k/ as [t] in word-initial position in English, saying "tookie" for "cookie" and "tat" for "cat."
- In Spanish, [[CHILD|Teo]] substituted /ɾ/ with [d] in medial position, producing "pedo" for "perro," though no dog-related confusion was noted in context.
- During play, [[CHILD|Teo]] pointed at the family dog in a photograph and said "[[PET|Rocky]] due," an apparent attempt at "[[PET|Rocky]] runs," omitting the final consonant cluster.
- When shown a family photo, [[CHILD|Teo]] pointed to his sister and said "[[SIBLING|Cami]] go," demonstrating early two-word combination with consistent final consonant deletion on "go" for "going."
- Gliding of liquids was noted, with /r/ and /l/ both realized as [w] in English, for example "wabbit" for "rabbit" and "yewwow" for "yellow."
- Stimulability testing showed that [[CHILD|Teo]] could approximate /k/ in isolation with [[KEEP|moderate cues]], achieving [[KEEP|6 of 10]] correct productions, but the target did not generalize to the word level without continued [[KEEP|maximal cues]].
- Target word probes for /s/ clusters showed [[KEEP|3 of 10]] correct productions in English and [[KEEP|4 of 10]] in Spanish, both below age expectations.
- Repetition of multisyllabic words resulted in syllable deletion, for example "[[KEEP|"nana"]]" for "banana" and "[[KEEP|"puter"]]" for "computer."
- Overall phonological inventory is reduced relative to age expectations in both languages, consistent with a phonological disorder rather than an isolated articulation disorder.

- SALT analysis coded frequent omission of grammatical morphemes, tagged [EW] for word-level errors and [EO] for omissions throughout the transcript.
- [[CHILD|Teo]] relied heavily on single-word utterances and two-word combinations, with only occasional three-word utterances observed, such as "[[SIBLING|Cami]] no go," intended to mean that his sister should not leave.
- Pronoun use was inconsistent, with frequent omission of subject pronouns and substitution of "me" for "I" in English utterances.
- Spanish utterances showed similarly reduced grammatical complexity, with verb conjugation errors and omission of articles noted throughout the sample.
- Narrative and sequencing skills could not be formally assessed due to limited expressive output but appeared emerging based on gesture-supported storytelling about a recent family outing.
- Pragmatic skills, including requesting, commenting, and protesting, were present but frequently achieved through gesture or vocalization rather than words.

CLINICAL IMPRESSIONS

- [[CHILD|Teo]] presents with a moderate-to-severe mixed receptive-expressive language disorder affecting both Spanish and English, consistent with [[KEEP|F80.2]].
- Speech sound production is significantly impaired in both languages, consistent with a phonological disorder, [[KEEP|F80.0]], rather than a language difference secondary to bilingual exposure.
- History of recurrent otitis media with effusion, [[KEEP|H65.93]], and documented mild conductive hearing loss likely contributed to delayed phonological development, though the current presentation exceeds what hearing history alone would explain.
- Language and speech sound deficits are present across both of [[CHILD|Teo]]'s languages, confirming a true impairment rather than typical second-language acquisition or dialectal variation.
- Reduced intelligibility, limited MLU, and inconsistent grammatical morpheme use place [[CHILD|Teo]] at significant risk for continued academic and social-emotional impact without intervention.
