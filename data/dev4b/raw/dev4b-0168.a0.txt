Language Sample and Pragmatics

During a structured play sample, [[CHILD|Mateo]] produced a combination of single words, occasional two-word combinations, and frequent gestures. Representative utterances included "[[PET|Rocky]] no" when his dog was moved away from a toy during a video call with his grandmother, and "[[SIBLING|Sol]] no" when his sister attempted to take a toy truck from him during the second session, which his mother had brought along. Mean length of utterance was calculated at [[KEEP|1.4 morphemes]], consistent with the Expressive Communication scores above.

Pragmatically, [[CHILD|Mateo]] demonstrated appropriate eye contact, joint attention, and turn-taking in play-based interactions, and he readily initiated communication through gesture even when verbal attempts were unsuccessful. He showed some frustration behaviors, including brief tantrums, when he was not understood, which both his mother and Miss [[TEACHER|Ana]] reported occur several times daily at home and at daycare respectively.

Hearing and Vision

Impressions / Impresiones Clínicas

Based on parent report, teacher report, standardized and criterion-referenced assessment results, and clinical observation, [[CHILD|Mateo]] presents with a moderate expressive and receptive language delay affecting both English and Spanish, along with a moderate-to-severe speech sound disorder characterized by multiple phonological process errors and a restricted consonant inventory. His difficulties appear consistent across both languages he is exposed to, which supports a primary diagnosis of a speech-language disorder rather than a language difference attributable solely to bilingual exposure. His social-communicative skills, joint attention, and nonverbal cognitive abilities appear to be relative strengths, suggesting a good prognosis for progress with consistent, intensive intervention.

[[CHILD|Mateo]] meets eligibility criteria for Part C early intervention services in North Carolina on the basis of a 25% or greater delay in the communication domain, as documented by standardized testing above. This diagnosis and the resulting eligibility determination will be formally reviewed and documented by the [[AGENCY|CDSA de Durham]] team, with service coordination continuing through [[PROVIDER|Rosa Jiménez]] at [[AGENCY|Coordinación Infantil del Triángulo]].

Recommendations / Recomendaciones

1. [[CHILD|Mateo]] is recommended for speech-language therapy services through the IFSP process, to address both receptive and expressive language delays and speech sound production errors. Intervention should be provided bilingually, in both English and Spanish, to support continued development in both of [[CHILD|Mateo]]'s home languages.

2. A bilingual, play-based therapeutic approach is recommended, incorporating elements of [[KEEP|Hanen]] "It Takes Two to Talk" strategies to support parent-implemented language facilitation in the home, alongside direct articulation intervention using a [[KEEP|Cycles]] approach to target [[CHILD|Mateo]]'s phonological process errors.

3. An occupational therapy evaluation is recommended to screen for any fine motor or sensory regulation concerns that may be contributing to frustration behaviors, given his borderline fine motor performance on the [[KEEP|PDMS-2]].

4. Continued close collaboration with daycare staff, including Miss [[TEACHER|Ana]], is recommended to support generalization of therapy targets across settings.

5. Family education regarding bilingual language development is recommended to address concerns raised by extended family members, including doña [[FAMILY|Lupe]], regarding whether speaking two languages is contributing to [[CHILD|Mateo]]'s delays; current research does not support this concern, and continued exposure to both English and Spanish is recommended.

6. Re-evaluation is recommended in approximately [[KEEP|6 months]] to monitor progress and adjust the treatment plan as needed.
