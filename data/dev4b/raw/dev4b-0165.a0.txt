IMPRESSIONS / IMPRESIONES

- Based on standardized testing, parent and teacher report, and clinical observation, [[CHILD|Mateo]] presents with a mixed receptive-expressive language disorder, consistent with [[KEEP|F80.2]], affecting both his home language (Spanish) and his school language (English), though the impairment is proportionally more pronounced in expressive language and in speech sound production across both languages.
- His language difficulties are not attributable solely to bilingual language exposure; deficits are present relative to same-age peers in both Spanish and English, which supports a true language disorder rather than a language difference.
- Speech sound production deficits, including reduced intelligibility (approximately [[KEEP|50%]] in connected speech), are consistent with a moderate speech sound disorder with phonological process patterns atypical for his age in both languages.
- [[PARENT|Lucía]] expressed concern that [[CHILD|Mateo]]'s unintelligibility is affecting his confidence at school: "le da pena cuando los niños no le entienden, y se queda callado" ("he feels embarrassed when the other children don't understand him, and he goes quiet").
- [[TEACHER|Ms. Lopez]]'s classroom observations of difficulty following multi-step directions and limited peer-directed communication are consistent with the standardized findings.
- Given the family's strong Spanish-language home environment, continued bilingual intervention — rather than English-only therapy — is clinically indicated to support both languages and to avoid further widening the gap between home and school communication.
