IMPRESSIONS

Based on parent and teacher report, record review, and today's assessment, [[CHILD|Mateo]] presents with a bilingual (Spanish-English) mixed receptive-expressive language disorder and a moderate speech sound disorder affecting intelligibility in both languages, consistent with a primary language impairment rather than a language difference secondary to bilingual exposure alone, given that delays are present and comparable across both of [[CHILD|Mateo]]'s languages. His scores on the [[KEEP|Preschool Language Scale-5]] fell in the moderately delayed range for Auditory Comprehension ([[KEEP|78]], [[KEEP|73–83]]) and Expressive Communication ([[KEEP|72]], [[KEEP|66–78]]), with Expressive Communication falling further below age expectations than Auditory Comprehension, a pattern consistent with his early history of expressive language delay documented by CDSA using the [[KEEP|Rossetti Infant-Toddler Language Scale]] and the [[KEEP|Bayley Scales of Infant and Toddler Development, Fourth Edition]].

Articulation testing with the [[KEEP|Goldman-Fristoe Test of Articulation-3]] placed his sounds-in-words performance in the severely delayed range ([[KEEP|68]], [[KEEP|61–75]]), consistent with clinical observation of final consonant deletion, cluster reduction, and gliding of liquids affecting both English and Spanish phoneme inventories. English receptive vocabulary as measured by the [[KEEP|Peabody Picture Vocabulary Test-5]] fell in the low-average range ([[KEEP|82]], [[KEEP|76–88]]), suggesting a relative strength in single-word vocabulary recognition compared to connected language use and expressive output.

These findings are consistent with [[CHILD|Mateo]]'s reported history of prematurity, NICU stay, recurrent otitis media with documented conductive involvement prior to tube placement, and an early expressive language delay first identified at [[KEEP|18 months]]. His current audiological status, confirmed within normal limits as of [[DATE|September 18, 2025]] at [[HOSPITAL|Carolina Ear, Nose and Throat and Audiology Associates, Lenoir Office]], supports that his current language and speech sound difficulties are not attributable to an active hearing loss, though his otologic history remains a relevant factor to monitor given known associations between recurrent otitis media and speech sound disorder. [[CHILD|Mateo]] meets criteria for speech-language therapy services under North Carolina's Exceptional Children's program eligibility category of Speech-Language Impairment, and continued monitoring of his hearing is recommended given his history.

For billing and documentation purposes, this evaluation is coded under ICD-10 [[KEEP|F80.2]] (mixed receptive-expressive language disorder) and ICD-10 [[KEEP|F80.0]] (phonological disorder), and under CPT [[KEEP|92523]] (evaluation of speech sound production with evaluation of language comprehension and expression) with modifier [[KEEP|-59]] applied to distinguish the combined evaluation from separately billed interpreter services.
