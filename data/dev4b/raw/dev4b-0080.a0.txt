**Clinical Impressions / Impresiones Clínicas**

Results indicate a language disorder affecting both Spanish and English, consistent with a primary language impairment rather than second-language acquisition alone, given that [[CHILD|María José]]'s scores fall well below expected ranges even in her dominant language (Spanish). Her expressive skills are notably weaker than her receptive skills in both languages, and she demonstrates reduced mean length of utterance relative to same-age bilingual peers. Articulation errors are mild and likely to resolve with typical development or minimal intervention, but language delay is the primary concern.

[[CHILD|María José]] meets criteria for Developmental Delay under the North Carolina Infant-Toddler Program eligibility guidelines (DSM-5-TR code [[KEEP|F80.9]], unspecified developmental language disorder; ICD-10 code [[KEEP|F80.9]]). CPT code for the bilingual evaluation performed was [[KEEP|92523]] with modifier [[KEEP|-59]].

Family and caregiver mix of languages, while rich and loving, has resulted in some inconsistency in modeling, which likely contributes to but does not fully explain [[CHILD|MJ]]'s delay. [[FAMILY|Rosa]] reported, "Ella entiende todo, pero no me dice casi nada" ("She understands everything, but she barely tells me anything"), which is consistent with the receptive-expressive gap observed during testing.

1. [[CHILD|María José]] qualifies for ongoing speech-language intervention services under [[AGENCY|Central Regional CDSA]]'s Infant-Toddler Program, two sessions weekly, [[KEEP|30 minutes]] each, in a bilingual service delivery model.
