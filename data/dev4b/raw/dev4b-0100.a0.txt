CLINICAL IMPRESSIONS

Based on standardized assessment, parent and teacher report, and direct observation, [[CHILD|Teo]] presents with a significant mixed receptive-expressive language disorder, with expressive language functioning substantially below both his receptive language and his nonverbal cognitive skills, across both of his languages, Spanish and English. His speech sound production also falls significantly below age expectations, with error patterns that are consistent cross-linguistically, supporting a true speech sound disorder rather than a pattern attributable solely to typical second-language phonological transfer or dialectal variation within Honduran Spanish.

The relative consistency of [[CHILD|Teo]]'s delays across both of his languages indicates that his difficulties reflect a genuine language impairment rather than a difference attributable to bilingual language exposure alone. His cognitive skills, which fall within the low average range, are notably higher than his language scores, further supporting the presence of a specific language impairment rather than a global developmental delay. The combination of reduced intelligibility, limited expressive vocabulary, and emerging behavioral frustration (hitting, throwing toys when not understood, as reported by [[TEACHER|Patricia Núñez]]) suggests that [[CHILD|Teo]]'s communication difficulties are beginning to affect his social-emotional functioning and peer interactions at [[AGENCY|Early Head Start]], including his developing friendship with [[PEER|Liam]].

[[CHILD|Teo]] continues to meet eligibility criteria for ongoing early intervention services under the North Carolina Infant-Toddler Program, given standard scores more than 1.5 standard deviations below the mean across multiple domains of communication. Given his upcoming third birthday, transition planning toward preschool special education services (Part B, Section 619) should begin promptly, in coordination with his service coordinator, [[PROVIDER|Dinora Castellanos]].

RECOMMENDATIONS

1. [[CHILD|Teo]] should continue to receive speech-language therapy services at a frequency of at least two 45-minute sessions per week, provided by a bilingual speech-language pathologist or with the support of a qualified Spanish interpreter, with a focus on both receptive and expressive language in Spanish, his primary home language, with concurrent support for English given his daycare environment.

2. Therapy should incorporate a combination of approaches, including [[KEEP|Hanen]]-based parent coaching strategies and the [[KEEP|PROMPT]] approach to support motor planning for speech production, given [[CHILD|Teo]]'s inconsistent sound substitution patterns.

3. A home program should be developed collaboratively with [[PARENT|Lucía]], [[PARENT|Carlos]], and [[FAMILY|Rosa]], given her significant role in [[CHILD|Teo]]'s daily care, with materials and strategies provided in Spanish to maximize carryover, including modeling expansion of [[CHILD|Teo]]'s one-word utterances into two- and three-word combinations.

4. Continued collaboration with [[AGENCY|Early Head Start]] staff, including [[TEACHER|Patricia Núñez]], is recommended to support classroom-based communication strategies and to monitor [[CHILD|Teo]]'s frustration-related behaviors, which appear linked to communication breakdown.

5. Referral for a transition evaluation to determine eligibility for preschool special education services (Part B) is recommended, to be initiated by [[AGENCY|Children's Developmental Services Agency of the Triangle]] in coordination with the local school district, well in advance of [[CHILD|Teo]]'s third birthday.

6. Re-evaluation of speech and language skills is recommended in approximately [[KEEP|6 months]] to monitor progress and adjust goals as needed, or sooner if significant changes in communication or behavior are observed by caregivers or teaching staff.

The family should continue to be connected with available community resources, including the [[AGENCY|Chatham County WIC Program]] and [[AGENCY|Chatham County Partnership for Children]], to support continued access to subsidized childcare and nutritional services as [[CHILD|Teo]] transitions between programs.
