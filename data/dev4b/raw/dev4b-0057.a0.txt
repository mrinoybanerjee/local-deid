6.2 Articulation and Phonology

On the GFTA-3, [[CHILD|Mateo]] demonstrated the following sound errors, consistent across English and Spanish contexts: final consonant deletion of /s/, gliding of /l/ and /r/ to [[KEEP|[w]]] (e.g., target "pelota" produced as "pewota"), and stopping of /f/ to [[KEEP|[p]]]. During the spontaneous language sample, [[CHILD|Mateo]] produced the target word "sopa" as "[[KEEP|topa]]," and "flor" as "[[KEEP|pol]]." Intelligibility to an unfamiliar listener was judged to be approximately [[KEEP|40%]] in connected speech.

On a 10-trial probe of word-initial /k/ production with [[KEEP|mod cues]], [[CHILD|Mateo]] achieved [[KEEP|8/10]] ([[KEEP|80%]]) accuracy, suggesting stimulability for this sound with support. On a comparable probe for /r/, he achieved [[KEEP|3 of 5]] trials correct with [[KEEP|max cues]], indicating this sound remains significantly more difficult.

6.3 Language Sample Analysis

A 50-utterance spontaneous language sample was coded using SALT conventions. Mean length of utterance (MLU) in English was [[KEEP|2.8 morphemes]], and in Spanish was [[KEEP|3.1 morphemes]], both below age expectations. Several utterances were coded [[KEEP|[EW]]] for word-finding difficulty, and [[CHILD|Mateo]] frequently relied on gesture to supplement verbal output when a target word was not accessible.

7. CLINICAL IMPRESSIONS / IMPRESIONES CLÍNICAS

[[CHILD|Mateo]] presents with a mixed receptive-expressive language disorder affecting both English and Spanish, alongside a moderate-to-severe speech sound disorder affecting intelligibility in both languages. His difficulties are not attributable to his bilingual environment; weaknesses were documented across both English and Spanish measures using instruments normed for bilingual populations (BESA), ruling out typical second-language acquisition patterns or normal cross-linguistic transfer as the sole explanation.

[[CHILD|Mateo]]'s pattern of errors—gliding of liquids, final consonant deletion, and reduced intelligibility across both languages—along with below-average scores in both receptive and expressive language domains in both languages, is consistent with a true bilingual language impairment rather than a language difference. Hearing has been ruled out as a contributing factor per [[PROVIDER|Dr. Michael Osei]]'s records. Oral-motor structure and function were within normal limits, with no evidence of dysarthria or significant oral-motor weakness; findings are inconsistent with childhood apraxia of speech, though mild groping behaviors were noted on a small number of multisyllabic word attempts and should continue to be monitored.

8. DIAGNOSTIC IMPRESSION

Based on the results of this evaluation, [[CHILD|Mateo]] meets diagnostic criteria for:

- [[KEEP|F80.2]] — Mixed Receptive-Expressive Language Disorder
- [[KEEP|F80.0]] — Phonological Disorder (Speech Sound Disorder)

These diagnoses are supported by standardized testing at or below the [[KEEP|10th percentile]] across multiple domains in both of [[CHILD|Mateo]]'s languages, parent and teacher report of functional communication difficulty, and direct clinical observation.
