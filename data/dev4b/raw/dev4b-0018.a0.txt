CLINICAL IMPRESSIONS

Based on standardized assessment, parent and teacher report, and clinical observation, [[CHILD|Mateo]] presents with a moderate expressive and mild-to-moderate receptive language delay affecting both English and Spanish, consistent with a primary language impairment rather than a sequential bilingual acquisition pattern alone. His pattern of errors, including limited MLU, restricted vocabulary across both languages, and reduced grammatical complexity, cannot be attributed to typical bilingual language development; true bilingual children generally show at least one language developing within normal limits, which is not the case here.

Articulation errors noted are largely consistent with normal developmental patterns for his age in both languages, with the exception of the English [[KEEP|/s/]] distortion, which warrants continued monitoring. History of recurrent otitis media with effusion, now resolved following tube placement, may have contributed to early language-learning delays, though normal hearing screening results in [[DATE|2024]] suggest hearing is not currently a limiting factor.

[[CHILD|Mateo]]'s strong pragmatic and social communication skills, along with his family's consistent engagement and bilingual home environment, are significant protective factors supporting a favorable prognosis with intervention. Continued exposure to both English and Spanish is recommended and should not be discontinued; research consistently shows that bilingual exposure does not cause or worsen language delay.
