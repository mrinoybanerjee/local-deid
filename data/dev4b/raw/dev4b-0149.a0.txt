In Spanish, [[CHILD|Mateo]]'s mean length of utterance (MLU) was [[KEEP|3.2]] morphemes, within age expectations. In English, MLU was [[KEEP|2.1]] morphemes, below age expectations. CLINICAL IMPRESSIONS

Results indicate that [[CHILD|Mateo]] presents with a moderate expressive and mild-to-moderate receptive language disorder in English, with relative strength in Spanish that is nonetheless also somewhat delayed for his age ([[KEEP|4;2]]). This pattern is consistent with a primary language impairment affecting both languages rather than a difference attributable solely to typical second-language acquisition, given that scores in his stronger language (Spanish) remain below average. A concomitant speech sound disorder is present, affecting intelligibility in both languages, with error patterns (final consonant deletion, stopping, liquid substitution) consistent with a phonological process disorder rather than a motor speech disorder.

[[CHILD|Mateo]]'s classroom teacher, Ms. [[TEACHER|Patricia Lopez]], corroborates these findings, noting that [[CHILD|Mateo]] often relies on gesture and peer mediation (particularly from [[PEER|Ethan]] and [[PEER|Sofia]]) to communicate during circle time. Parent-reported use of [[KEEP|Hanen]] "It Takes Two to Talk" strategies has reportedly supported some gains in turn-taking, but [[CHILD|Mateo]] continues to demonstrate significant gaps relative to same-age bilingual peers.

RECOMMENDATIONS

1. [[CHILD|Mateo]] qualifies for and would benefit from ongoing speech-language therapy services, recommended at a frequency of 2x/week for 45-minute sessions, provided bilingually or with interpreter support.

2. Therapy should target: expansion of expressive vocabulary and sentence structure in both languages; reduction of phonological processes (final consonant deletion, velar fronting/backing patterns, liquid substitution) using a [[KEEP|Cycles]] approach; and functional communication strategies incorporating [[KEEP|PROMPT]] tactile-cueing techniques to support motor planning for speech sound production.

3. Given [[CHILD|Mateo]]'s frustration with communication breakdowns, introduction of a visual support system modeled on [[KEEP|PECS]] principles is recommended for use at [[DAYCARE|Little Sprouts Daycare]] and at home to reduce behavioral frustration during communication attempts.

4. Continued caregiver coaching using [[KEEP|Hanen]]-based strategies is recommended, with consideration of a [[KEEP|Kaufman]] Speech to Language Protocol approach as a supplement for apraxia-like motor planning difficulties should intelligibility not improve with phonological intervention alone.

5. Referral to [[AGENCY|Wake County CDSA]] is recommended for continued service coordination through [[PROVIDER|Brenda Jacobs]] to support transition planning toward [[SCHOOL|Oak Ridge Elementary]] and potential preschool special education evaluation through the local school system prior to [[DATE|August 2026]].

6. Continued bilingual language exposure at home is strongly encouraged. Parents should not be advised to reduce Spanish exposure, as bilingualism is not contributing to [[CHILD|Mateo]]'s delays. Continued participation in the [[AGENCY|Wake County Public Library]] bilingual storytime program and at [[ORG|Iglesia Pentecostal Monte Sion]]'s children's programming is encouraged to support socialization and language modeling in naturalistic contexts.
