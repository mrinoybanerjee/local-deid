SPEECH SOUND PRODUCTION

Oral-motor structures and function were judged adequate for speech. Diadochokinetic rates were mildly reduced but within functional limits for age. Conversational speech intelligibility was estimated at approximately 40% to unfamiliar listeners and 65% to familiar listeners such as [[PARENT|Marisol Reyes Flores]] and [[FAMILY|Hortensia Flores Pérez]].

Error patterns observed across English and Spanish samples included final consonant deletion, cluster reduction, and gliding of liquids. For example, [[CHILD|Mateo]] produced "ba" for [[KEEP|"bath"]], "poon" for [[KEEP|"spoon"]], and "wed" for [[KEEP|"red"]]. In Spanish, [[CHILD|Mateo]] produced "pato" for [[KEEP|"plato"]] and substituted [[KEEP|[w]]] for [[KEEP|/ɾ/]] in words such as [[KEEP|"perro"]], producing [[KEEP|"pewo"]]. He demonstrated stimulability for [[KEEP|/r/]] in isolation with [[KEEP|moderate cues]], and for final [[KEEP|/t/]] and [[KEEP|/d/]] in single words with [[KEEP|minimal cues]]. He correctly produced [[KEEP|/k/]] and [[KEEP|/g/]] in initial position in [[KEEP|8 of 10]] trials. No dysfluencies, resonance abnormalities, or voice quality concerns were noted in either language.

RECEPTIVE AND EXPRESSIVE LANGUAGE

In English, [[CHILD|Mateo]] followed one-step directions in [[KEEP|9 of 10]] trials but only [[KEEP|4 of 10]] two-step directions without repetition. He identified basic vocabulary (body parts, common objects, and colors) with [[KEEP|85%]] accuracy but struggled with spatial concepts, correctly identifying "under" and "behind" but not "between" or "next to." Expressively, [[CHILD|Mateo]]'s utterances were primarily two- to three-word combinations such as "[[PET|Luna]] go park" and "want juice now." Mean length of utterance was calculated at [[KEEP|2.4 morphemes]], below the expected range of [[KEEP|3;0-4;0]] for his age.

In Spanish, [[CHILD|Mateo]] demonstrated comparable receptive skills to English, following simple commands from his grandmother, [[FAMILY|Hortensia Flores Pérez]], with minimal difficulty, but his expressive Spanish was similarly limited to short phrases such as "quiero agua" and "[[PET|Luna]] corre." Code-mixing was observed in approximately 20% of utterances, consistent with typical bilingual development rather than a language-specific deficit, though the overall reduced utterance length and limited grammatical morphology were present across both languages, supporting a primary language impairment rather than a language-difference profile.

SUMMARY AND IMPRESSIONS

[[CHILD|Mateo]] is a [[KEEP|4;2]]-year-old bilingual (Spanish-English) boy presenting with a moderate speech sound disorder and a moderate expressive and receptive language disorder affecting both languages. Standardized testing revealed scores in the below-average to well-below-average range across receptive language ([[KEEP|78]], [[KEEP|7th percentile]]), expressive language ([[KEEP|73]], [[KEEP|4th percentile]]), and articulation ([[KEEP|70]], [[KEEP|2nd percentile]]). Because deficits were observed consistently across both English and Spanish, these results are interpreted as reflecting a true communication disorder rather than typical second-language acquisition or bilingual code-mixing.

[[CHILD|Mateo]]'s history of recurrent otitis media and tympanostomy tube placement may have contributed to early speech and language delay, though his most recent audiological evaluation indicates hearing is now within normal limits. His strong social engagement, use of compensatory gesture, and stimulability for target sounds with cueing are favorable prognostic indicators for intervention.
