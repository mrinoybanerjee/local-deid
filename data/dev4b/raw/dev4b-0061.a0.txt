Clinical Impressions

Based on today's results, parent and teacher report, and [[CHILD|Chuy]]'s developmental and medical history, he presents with a moderate expressive and mild-to-moderate receptive language delay, along with a moderate speech sound disorder affecting intelligibility in both Spanish and English. These findings are consistent with diagnosis codes [[KEEP|F80.2]] (mixed receptive-expressive language disorder) and [[KEEP|F80.0]] (phonological disorder).

It is important to understand that being raised bilingually is not the cause of [[CHILD|Chuy]]'s delays. Children who grow up hearing two languages develop language on a similar overall timeline to children who hear one language, as long as they are getting rich exposure to both, which [[CHILD|Chuy]] clearly is at home. His difficulties appear across both Spanish and English, which tells us this is a true language and speech sound disorder rather than something related to learning two languages at once. His history of prematurity, NICU stay, and chronic ear infections prior to tube placement are all plausible contributing factors, and his strong social engagement, play skills, and nonverbal communication are real strengths that will support his progress in therapy.
