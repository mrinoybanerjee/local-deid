FAMILY CONCERNS AND PRIORITIES
In the family's own words, shared through interpreter [[PROVIDER|Marisol Delgado]]: "Queremos que [[CHILD|Mateo]] pueda decir lo que necesita sin frustrarse. En la guardería lo entienden poco porque mezcla español e inglés, y en casa a veces solo yo lo entiendo," said [[PARENT|Lucía Ramírez]]. She added, "Su hermana [[SIBLING|Sol]] habla por él, y no quiero que se acostumbre a eso." [[PARENT|Carlos Fernández]] shared a similar concern by phone: "I want him to talk to his cousins without getting upset when they don't understand him."

The family's top priorities, as agreed at the meeting, are: (1) increasing [[CHILD|Mateo]]'s intelligibility in both languages so that extended family and daycare staff can understand him without [[SIBLING|Sol]] interpreting; (2) decreasing frustration-related tantrums when he is not understood; and (3) building home routines that support bilingual language growth without pressuring [[CHILD|Mateo]] to favor one language over the other. The family also expressed interest in strategies they can use consistently between [[PARENT|Lucía Ramírez]]'s nursing shifts and [[FAMILY|Rosa Ramírez]]'s caregiving hours.

DEVELOPMENTAL STATUS / ASSESSMENT SUMMARY
Direct assessment was completed using the [[KEEP|Preschool Language Scales, Fifth Edition]] ([[KEEP|PLS-5]]), administered bilingually with items credited in either language per examiner's manual guidelines. [[CHILD|Mateo]] obtained an Auditory Comprehension standard score of [[KEEP|79]] ([[KEEP|8th percentile]]), falling within the [[KEEP|73-83]] confidence interval, and an Expressive Communication standard score of [[KEEP|71]] ([[KEEP|3rd percentile]]), within the [[KEEP|65-77]] confidence interval. Age-equivalent scores placed receptive skills at [[KEEP|20 months]] and expressive skills within the [[KEEP|16-18 month]] range, against a chronological age of [[KEEP|2;6]]. A supplemental nonverbal cognitive screening yielded an age equivalent within [[KEEP|2;6–3;0]], consistent with age-level nonverbal reasoning and contrasting with the more significant expressive delay. Parent report using the [[KEEP|MacArthur-Bates Communicative Development Inventories]] estimated an expressive vocabulary of approximately [[KEEP|45 words]] across both languages combined. Oral-motor examination was within normal limits, and hearing screening passed bilaterally on [[DATE|August 14, 2026]].

Phonetic inventory in Spanish included /p/, /m/, /n/, and /a/, with inconsistent production of /s/ and /t/ in word-initial position. During structured play, [[CHILD|Mateo]] was stimulable for the target word "sol" with [[KEEP|moderate cues]], producing an approximation transcribed as [ʔo] in [[KEEP|2 of 10]] trials and improving to [[KEEP|6 of 10]] trials following tactile cueing modeled on [[KEEP|PROMPT]] principles. This target was chosen for its phonetic simplicity and everyday functional relevance, distinct from the name of his sister, [[SIBLING|Sol]]; the family confirmed during the session that the word was understood as referring to the sun, not to [[SIBLING|Sol]] herself.

PRESENT LEVELS OF FUNCTIONING
[[CHILD|Mateo]] communicates primarily through gesture, single-word approximations, and vocalizations. He follows simple one-step directions in context ([[KEEP|8 of 10]] trials) but struggles with directions outside routine context ([[KEEP|3 of 10]] trials). He imitates two- to three-word phrases inconsistently and shows emerging turn-taking skills during structured play. Social engagement with [[PEER|Diego]] and [[PEER|Valentina]] is positive, though [[CHILD|Mateo]] becomes frustrated and occasionally drops to the floor or hits when peers or adults misunderstand his requests. Classroom observation by [[TEACHER|Brenda Collier]] corroborates these patterns, noting improved engagement when visual supports are available.

OUTCOMES AND STRATEGIES
Outcome 1: [[CHILD|Mateo]] will increase expressive vocabulary to [[KEEP|75 words]] across English and Spanish combined, as measured by parent log and direct observation, within six months. Strategies include modeling and expansion techniques drawn from [[KEEP|Hanen]]'s [[KEEP|It Takes Two to Talk]] program, which [[PARENT|Lucía Ramírez]] and [[FAMILY|Rosa Ramírez]] will practice during daily routines with coaching from [[PROVIDER|Hannah Okafor]] every two weeks.

Outcome 2: [[CHILD|Mateo]] will combine two words to request preferred items in at least [[KEEP|8 of 10]] opportunities across settings. Strategies include a core vocabulary board supplemented by [[KEEP|PECS]]-based exchange for daycare transitions, with visual supports introduced at home and shared with [[TEACHER|Brenda Collier]] for classroom consistency.

Outcome 3: [[CHILD|Mateo]] will reduce frustration behaviors, including hitting and dropping to the floor, to no more than [[KEEP|2 episodes]] per session by increasing functional communication attempts. The team will track phonological progress using the [[KEEP|Cycles]] approach alongside behavior charts shared between home and [[DAYCARE|Little Sprouts Early Learning Center]].

Outcome 4: [[PARENT|Lucía Ramírez]] and [[PARENT|Carlos Fernández]] will report increased confidence supporting bilingual language development, as measured by a parent confidence survey administered at each review. Service coordinator [[PROVIDER|Patricia Nguyen]] will provide a [[KEEP|Kaufman]] Speech to Language Protocol home-practice guide translated into Spanish and will follow up on the [[AGENCY|Smart Start of Catawba County]] playgroup referral by [[DATE|October 15, 2026]].
