SUMMARY AND CLINICAL IMPRESSIONS

[[CHILD|Juanito]] presents with a moderate-to-severe mixed receptive-expressive language disorder affecting both Spanish and English, accompanied by a moderate phonological disorder characterized by consistent, language-independent error patterns (final consonant deletion, stopping, gliding). His difficulties are not attributable to hearing loss, oral-motor structural impairment, or bilingual language exposure alone; his error patterns and language scores are depressed relative to typical bilingual peers in both of his languages, which supports a true language disorder rather than typical second-language acquisition variability. His sociable affect, intact play skills, and appropriate joint attention argue against an autism spectrum disorder at this time, though continued monitoring of behavior related to communicative frustration is warranted.

Diagnostic impressions, for coding purposes, correspond to ICD-10 [[KEEP|F80.2]] (mixed receptive-expressive language disorder) and ICD-10 [[KEEP|F80.0]] (phonological disorder). This evaluation was billed under CPT [[KEEP|92523]] (evaluation of speech sound production with evaluation of language comprehension and expression).

RECOMMENDATIONS

1. [[CHILD|Juanito]] is recommended for speech-language therapy twice weekly, bilingual Spanish-English service delivery, for an initial period of [[KEEP|12 weeks]] with re-evaluation of progress at that time.
2. Treatment approaches recommended for this case include [[KEEP|Hanen]] (It Takes Two to Talk) coaching for caregivers to increase responsive language strategies in the home, [[KEEP|PROMPT]] tactile-kinesthetic cueing to address the motor-based components of his sound errors, and the [[KEEP|Cycles]] approach for phonological pattern remediation given the consistency of his error patterns across both languages.
3. Given his limited expressive output and frustration behaviors, introduction of a modified [[KEEP|PECS]] (Picture Exchange Communication System) visual support at home is recommended as a bridge strategy to reduce communicative frustration, to be faded as verbal output increases.
4. If expressive vocabulary growth remains slow despite direct treatment, a trial of the [[KEEP|Kaufman]] Speech to Language Protocol is recommended to target motor planning for multisyllabic word production.
5. Caregiver training, including [[FAMILY|Rosa Hernández]] and both parents, in home-based strategies consistent with [[KEEP|It Takes Two to Talk]] principles is strongly recommended, given that [[FAMILY|Rosa Hernández]] serves as the primary daytime caregiver.
6. Family is encouraged to continue exposing [[CHILD|Juanito]] to both Spanish and English rather than restricting either language, as bilingual exposure is not contributing to his disorder and dual-language input supports long-term outcomes.
7. Continued collaboration with [[PROVIDER|Dr. Amara Chen]] regarding monitoring of middle ear status is recommended given history of recurrent otitis media and tube placement.
8. Re-evaluation of behavioral/social-emotional functioning is recommended if frustration-related behaviors increase or if autism spectrum features emerge, with referral to [[PROVIDER|Dr. Priya Mehta]], developmental pediatrician, as needed.
9. Family should contact [[AGENCY|Piedmont CDSA]] service coordinator [[PROVIDER|Nadia Fournier]] regarding possible continued service coordination support given the family's prior Part C involvement, even though [[CHILD|Juanito]] has aged out of Part C eligibility.
