On [[KEEP|Auditory Comprehension]], [[CHILD|Nico]] identified familiar objects, actions, and several body parts. He understood routine requests supported by the immediate context. Performance decreased when directions contained an unfamiliar location word, multiple attributes, or a change in the expected routine. During informal probes, he followed related directions in [[KEEP|7/10]] opportunities with gestures and in [[KEEP|4/10]] opportunities without gestures.

He demonstrated emerging understanding of location concepts during toy play. For the target [[KEEP|"under"]], he initially placed an animal beside a box. After a demonstration, he placed it correctly in [[KEEP|3 of 5]] opportunities with [[KEEP|mod cues]]. When the examiner changed the materials, accuracy decreased. This pattern suggested that learning occurred, but the concept was not yet stable across activities.

On [[KEEP|Expressive Communication]], he named familiar objects and used short combinations to request, protest, and comment. He produced fewer messages to describe an absent event or explain why something happened. He sometimes omitted grammatical elements in both languages. The examiner considered these patterns across repeated opportunities and did not interpret a single mixed-language utterance as evidence of disorder.

A play-based language sample included spontaneous messages, responses to adult questions, and brief pretend routines. Productions included [[KEEP|"yo quiero agua"]], [[KEEP|"bus go fast"]], and [[KEEP|"no cabe aquí"]]. He also said "[[PET|Miso]] eat" while pretending to feed a toy dog. Because that production contained the pet's name, the name received an identifier label rather than a clinical preservation label around the entire phrase.

### Impressions and recommendations

The combined findings support a mixed receptive-expressive language disorder, ICD-10 [[KEEP|F80.2]], and a speech sound disorder, ICD-10 [[KEEP|F80.0]]. Difficulty appeared across Spanish and English opportunities and affected daily participation. Bilingual exposure contributed to differences in vocabulary and familiarity with tasks, but the broader pattern could not be explained by language exposure alone. These impressions integrate testing, observed communication, caregiver history, and response to teaching.

Strengths include social interest, purposeful gestures, pretend play, persistence, and willingness to learn from supportive models. Communication needs include understanding less familiar directions, building flexible sentences, improving speech clarity, and repairing misunderstood messages. The family described these same needs at home and in preschool. Treatment should connect speech and language practice to activities that matter to the child and his caregivers.

Speech-language therapy is recommended with a bilingual provider when available. Services should include direct intervention, caregiver coaching, and collaboration with classroom staff. Spanish should remain available during instruction and home routines. The proposed schedule below allows practice in the clinic and observation within preschool activities. It is a planning recommendation, not confirmation that transportation, staffing, coverage, or appointment availability has been arranged.

Initial language treatment should use [[KEEP|Enhanced Milieu Teaching]], [[KEEP|focused stimulation]], and [[KEEP|expansions]] within play and daily routines. Adults should model messages slightly longer than the child's current independent productions. They should allow a response interval and accept gestures alongside speech. Practice should include requesting assistance, describing a problem, commenting on an action, and sharing information that the listener cannot already see.

For speech clarity, the clinician may use a developmentally selected [[KEEP|Cycles]] framework if repeated baseline sampling confirms suitable patterns. Initial targets should be functional words and sound contrasts that improve understanding across languages. Spanish [[KEEP|/ɾ/]] should not become an early priority solely because it appeared inaccurate during this visit. Target selection should consider developmental expectations, stimulability, family priorities, and the child's tolerance for structured practice.

Proposed [[KEEP|Goal 1]] addresses following related directions with less contextual support. The initial criterion is [[KEEP|8/10]] opportunities with [[KEEP|min cues]] across familiar activities. Proposed [[KEEP|Goal 2]] addresses independent messages for requesting, commenting, or explaining a problem in [[KEEP|4 of 5]] opportunities. Performance should be recorded separately for independent responses, modeled responses, and responses requiring additional support.

Proposed [[KEEP|Goal 3]] addresses communication repair. The child will add a word, gesture, or clearer repetition after a listener signals misunderstanding in [[KEEP|4/5]] opportunities with [[KEEP|mod cues]]. Proposed [[KEEP|Goal 4]] addresses selected speech targets in functional words at [[KEEP|80%]] accuracy with [[KEEP|mod cues]]. Final target words should follow additional baseline sampling and should include vocabulary used by the family.
