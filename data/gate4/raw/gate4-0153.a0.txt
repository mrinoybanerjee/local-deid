COMPRENSIÓN / RECEPTIVE LANGUAGE

On [[KEEP|Auditory Comprehension]], [[CHILD|Nicolás]] identified familiar objects, common actions, and basic body parts. He understood routine directions with visible context. He followed simple location directions in [[KEEP|8/10]] opportunities with [[KEEP|min cues]].

Performance decreased when a direction required remembering several details. He completed unfamiliar directions containing linked actions in [[KEEP|3 of 5]] opportunities with [[KEEP|mod cues]]. When the examiner reduced sentence length and added gestures, his responses became more consistent.

He showed emerging understanding of descriptive concepts. Comparisons involving size were easier than questions involving sequence or exclusion. During picture discussion, he sometimes selected an associated object instead of the object that matched the full question.

Errors appeared in both languages. Spanish support improved access to familiar vocabulary but did not consistently resolve difficulty with longer instructions. His responses suggested that vocabulary knowledge and sentence processing both affected comprehension. Classroom instructions should therefore include clear wording, visible support, and a check of understanding.

EXPRESIÓN / EXPRESSIVE LANGUAGE

On [[KEEP|Expressive Communication]], he named familiar objects, combined words, and used language for requesting, commenting, and protesting. Longer responses often omitted grammatical elements or the relationship between ideas. This pattern appeared in Spanish and English samples.

He answered direct questions about visible events more successfully than questions about events outside the room. He could identify who was acting but often needed [[KEEP|mod cues]] to explain why something happened. Specific action words improved after modeling.

During storytelling, he named characters and actions but did not independently establish a clear sequence. With pictures arranged in order, he described the beginning and ending. The middle event remained incomplete until the clinician used [[KEEP|max cues]].

Some English forms reflected expected cross-language influence. Those forms were not treated as disorder evidence by themselves. Concern centered on limited sentence expansion, weak event organization, and difficulty communicating essential information across both languages.

IMPRESIONES CLÍNICAS

Findings support a mixed receptive-expressive language disorder and a speech sound disorder. Proposed diagnostic codes are [[KEEP|ICD-10 F80.2]] and [[KEEP|ICD-10 F80.0]]. The conclusion rests on converging functional concerns, direct observations, bilingual sampling, and assessment results.

Bilingualism does not explain the full pattern. Difficulties occurred across languages and remained when familiar vocabulary, visual support, and additional processing time were available. At the same time, language differences and code-switching must continue to be respected.

Strengths include social engagement, pretend play, communicative intent, family participation, and responsiveness to teaching. Speech errors reduce clarity, while language organization limits the detail of his messages. Both areas affect participation with unfamiliar adults and peers.

This evaluation did not assess literacy, cognition, or broader developmental functioning. Current findings do not support conclusions in those areas. Progress monitoring should examine communication during meaningful routines, not only accuracy during structured practice.
