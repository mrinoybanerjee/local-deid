IMPRESSIONS / IMPRESIONES CLÍNICAS

Findings support mixed receptive-expressive language difficulties that affect participation across home and daycare. The strongest evidence includes limited message length in both languages, difficulty with novel directions, caregiver concerns, classroom reports, and current assessment results. Bilingual exposure and code-switching do not explain the full pattern.

Speech sound difficulties also reduce intelligibility. The observed patterns include errors shared across language samples and patterns that require language-specific interpretation. Continued assessment should distinguish developmental variation from persistent difficulty. The present sample supports intervention while that distinction becomes clearer.

Clinical coding: ICD-10 [[KEEP|F80.2]] for mixed receptive-expressive language disorder and ICD-10 [[KEEP|F80.0]] for speech sound disorder. These codes summarize the fictional clinical impressions. No diagnosis of childhood apraxia of speech was established from this evaluation.

Strengths include social interest, shared enjoyment, gesture use, family involvement, and improved responses after teaching. Prognosis for functional progress is favorable with consistent access, caregiver coaching, and coordinated practice. Progress may vary with sleep, attendance, task demands, and opportunities to communicate.
