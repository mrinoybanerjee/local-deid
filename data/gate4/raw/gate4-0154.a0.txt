### 1. Reason for Referral / Motivo de consulta

#### 1.1 Presenting concerns

[[CHILD|Mateo Aranda]] was referred for a bilingual evaluation because his family and classroom team reported difficulty understanding longer directions, combining ideas, and producing clear speech. His mother, [[PARENT|Lucía Aranda]], described a difference between familiar routines and new situations: “En casa sabemos lo que quiere, pero cuando cambia la pregunta, se pierde.” His father, [[PARENT|Diego Aranda]], reported that communication breakdowns sometimes lead to crying or leaving an activity.

The referral requested information about skills across Spanish and English. It also requested practical recommendations for home and school. The family wants [[CHILD|Mateo]] to ask for help, explain basic needs, and participate with other children without depending on an adult to interpret each message.

#### 1.2 Evaluation questions

The evaluation examined whether observed difficulties reflected bilingual development, a language disorder, speech sound difficulties, or a combination. It also examined how visual support, repetition, and adult modeling affected performance.

This report integrates caregiver information, classroom observations, standardized testing, play samples, and supported teaching tasks. No single score determined the impressions. Spanish and English responses were considered in relation to exposure, familiarity, and communication demands.

### 10. Clinical Impressions / Impresiones clínicas

#### 10.1 Language

Findings are consistent with a mixed receptive-expressive language disorder in a bilingual child. The impression reflects difficulty across languages, caregiver concerns in the stronger language, functional limitations, and supported learning observations.

The recommended language diagnosis is ICD-10-CM [[KEEP|F80.2]]. Bilingual exposure does not explain the full pattern observed. English proficiency remains developing and should be described separately from disorder.

#### 10.2 Speech

Findings also support a speech sound disorder, with clinically relevant errors affecting intelligibility. The recommended speech diagnosis is ICD-10-CM [[KEEP|F80.0]].

Language-specific expectations must guide target selection. Spanish and English have different sound systems, and a difference between those systems should not automatically become a treatment target.

#### 10.3 Strengths and prognosis

Strengths include social interest, meaningful gestures, enjoyment of shared play, caregiver engagement, and a measurable response to visual support. [[CHILD|Mateo]] also attempts both languages and accepts models during motivating activities.

Prognosis for improved functional communication is favorable with consistent intervention and coordinated support. The rate of progress cannot be predicted from this evaluation alone.

#### 10.4 Limits of the evaluation

The findings represent one clinic visit plus available records and reports. Classroom information was supplied by staff rather than obtained through direct observation.

The clinician recommends an additional naturalistic sample after treatment begins. That sample should examine generalization, message repair, and communication with unfamiliar listeners.

### 11. Recommendations / Recomendaciones

#### 11.1 Direct intervention

Begin speech-language treatment with a bilingual provider or a provider who can arrange appropriate language access. Use meaningful play and daily routines to address comprehension, flexible sentences, and speech clarity.

Recommended approaches include [[KEEP|enhanced milieu teaching]], [[KEEP|focused stimulation]], and [[KEEP|recasts]]. Consider [[KEEP|Cycles]] for suitable phonological targets after further analysis. [[KEEP|PROMPT]] is not currently indicated by the available evidence.

Use visual communication supports when they improve participation. Formal [[KEEP|PECS]] implementation is not required at this time. A [[KEEP|Hanen]] caregiver program may provide useful support if available and appropriate to the family’s schedule.

#### 11.2 Home practice / En casa

Continue Spanish at home. Caregivers should use the language that supports their most natural, detailed interaction. Encourage English through enjoyable school-related routines without replacing Spanish conversation.

Model a short sentence, wait, and respond to the child’s meaning. For example, expand [[KEEP|"carro rojo"]] to [[KEEP|"quiero el carro rojo"]]. Avoid requiring repeated correction during every exchange.

Use [[KEEP|dialogic reading]] with familiar picture books. Offer choices, pause for responses, and connect pictures to daily experiences. Brief practice within routines is preferable to long drills.

#### 11.3 School supports

Provide short directions with visual cues. Confirm understanding through action instead of repeatedly asking whether he understands. Offer extra response time and predictable opportunities to speak.

Pair [[CHILD|Mateo]] with supportive classmates during structured play. Staff should model requesting, turn-taking, and repair phrases. Useful targets include [[KEEP|"ayúdame"]], [[KEEP|"otra vez"]], and [[KEEP|"I need help"]].
