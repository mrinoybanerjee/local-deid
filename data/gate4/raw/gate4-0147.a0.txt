Resumen / Summary

[[CHILD|Emilio]] presents with reduced speech intelligibility and difficulty organizing longer messages across Spanish and English. He communicates readily through gestures, short sentences, facial expression, and shared play. His participation improves when the listener allows extra response time and offers visual support. His family identifies being understood during meals, school transitions, and sibling play as the main priorities.

Findings support a speech sound disorder and concern for an expressive language disorder. His skills vary across languages and activities. English scores describe performance under the stated conditions; they do not independently establish a disorder. The clinical conclusion also considers Spanish sampling, caregiver history, classroom participation, and response to teaching. Continued bilingual assessment will clarify his language profile.

Comprensión y expresión / Language findings

During familiar Spanish routines, [[CHILD|Emilio]] followed related directions in [[KEEP|8/10]] opportunities without repetition. Performance decreased to [[KEEP|3 of 5]] when directions included an unfamiliar location term. Visual demonstration improved performance to [[KEEP|5 of 5]]. This suggests benefit from contextual support, rather than a uniform difficulty understanding spoken language.

English responses included labeling, requesting, and simple location information. Spanish responses showed broader vocabulary for household routines. He sometimes selected a word from the other language: [[KEEP|"quiero more agua"]]. This code-switching was accepted as meaningful communication.

Longer messages contained omissions and unclear references. During pretend cooking, he said [[KEEP|"yo pon aquí"]], then pointed to a bowl. With [[KEEP|mod cues]], he expanded to [[KEEP|"yo pongo sopa aquí"]]. After a model, he produced [[KEEP|"the dog is sleeping"]], but did not consistently generate similar sentence structures independently.

In a picture sequence, he identified the main event but needed [[KEEP|max cues]] to explain the cause. He improved after demonstration and guided practice. The improvement shows learning potential, while continued support needs indicate that a single successful imitation should not be treated as mastery.

Impresiones clínicas / Clinical impressions

The combined evidence supports reduced speech clarity across languages, with errors that affect daily participation. Differences between Spanish and English sound systems were considered before selecting targets. Bilingual exposure alone does not explain the observed shared speech difficulties.

Expressive language concerns also appear across caregiver reports, language samples, and classroom activities. Receptive skills are stronger in familiar contexts. Additional Spanish assessment and repeated natural samples will help separate limited task familiarity from persistent language difficulty.

Working diagnostic codes are [[KEEP|F80.0]] for phonological disorder and provisional [[KEEP|F80.1]] for expressive language disorder. The second code remains subject to further bilingual evidence. Strengths include social engagement, gesture use, family participation, and improvement with supported teaching.

Recomendaciones / Recommendations

1. Begin bilingual treatment using the proposed schedule. Address functional speech clarity and sentence formulation through familiar play and routines. Use [[KEEP|Cycles]] principles selectively for recurring sound patterns, with targets appropriate to each language.

2. Teach initial [[KEEP|/k/]] in meaningful words and short phrases. Establish baseline accuracy before setting the next criterion. A preliminary objective is [[KEEP|8/10]] accurate productions with [[KEEP|min cues]], across repeated sessions.

3. Support descriptions of actions and recent events. Aim for complete messages in [[KEEP|4 of 5]] structured opportunities with [[KEEP|mod cues]], then reduce assistance as independence improves.

4. Provide caregiver coaching informed by [[KEEP|Hanen]] strategies. Follow the child’s interest, pause for a response, and expand the message naturally. Families may respond in the language that feels most comfortable.

5. Use classroom visuals, advance previews, and extra response time. Invite clarification without requiring repeated public correction. Practice repair phrases such as [[KEEP|"otra vez"]], [[KEEP|"quiero decir"]], and [[KEEP|"let me show you"]].

6. Review progress on [[DATE|January 14, 2027]]. Compare spontaneous communication, caregiver priorities, and classroom access. Repeat formal testing only when clinically justified.
