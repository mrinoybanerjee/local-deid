A: ANÁLISIS / IMPRESIONES CLÍNICAS

Findings support mixed receptive-expressive language disorder, ICD-10 [[KEEP|F80.2]], within this fictional case. Comprehension weaknesses affect unfamiliar directions, relational concepts, and event questions. Expressive weaknesses affect flexible combinations, event descriptions, and repair. Evidence includes below-expected standardized performance, difficulty across both languages, functional caregiver concerns, and direct observation. Bilingual exposure alone does not explain the full pattern. English experience remains relevant to item familiarity and school performance. The clinician considered that context throughout interpretation.

Severity impression: Moderate functional language support need at present. This description reflects participation demands and cue dependence, not a fixed prediction. Child communicates many basic needs through words and gestures. Breakdowns increase when partners lack shared context or require an explanation. School group directions create added processing demands. At home, familiar routines can conceal comprehension gaps because caregivers anticipate the next step. Treatment should measure communication with less familiar materials and partners while preserving predictable supports for success.

Strengths / fortalezas: Social engagement, pretend play, persistence with preferred materials, and response to supported teaching. Parents notice communication attempts and accept both languages. Child uses code-switching purposefully and has meaningful vocabulary distributed across languages. A Spanish-only or English-only view would undercount some abilities. Dynamic improvement indicates capacity to learn with an appropriate model and practice. Generalization still requires verification. Team members should value a successful bilingual message even when it differs from the adult's preferred language.

Speech sound impression: No separate speech sound diagnosis assigned today. The available sample was limited, and observed variability did not establish a stable pattern. Some productions reduced intelligibility, but language formulation also contributed. Reassessment should examine repeated words and connected speech across languages. Current therapy should avoid assigning developmentally inappropriate sound expectations. A focused speech intervention such as [[KEEP|Cycles]] or [[KEEP|PROMPT]] is not selected from these data. Selection would require a clearer speech profile and a specific functional rationale.

Educational implications: Child may miss instructions even while appearing attentive. Repetition of the same long sentence may increase confusion. Short directions, visual information, and a check through action can improve access. Teachers should allow responses in either language when the activity permits. Peer support can help entry into play, but peers should not carry responsibility for communication. Adult modeling should show how to request, clarify, and repair. Participation data should include independent attempts and the amount of support required.

Limitations: Parent history includes recall estimates. Teacher information came from a written report, without direct classroom observation. Assessment occurred during one clinic visit. Standardized scores cannot fully describe bilingual communication in daily life. Age-equivalent ranges must not determine placement or replace functional judgment. Prognosis for useful progress is favorable with consistent support, based on observed learning and caregiver participation. Rate of progress remains uncertain. Continued data collection will test whether clinic gains transfer to home and school.
