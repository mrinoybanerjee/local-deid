IMPRESSIONS / IMPRESIONES CLÍNICAS

The combined findings support mixed receptive-expressive language disorder, coded [[KEEP|F80.2]]. Difficulties affect understanding relationships in spoken messages, creating flexible word combinations, and adding information when communication breaks down. These concerns appear across Spanish and English and exceed differences explained by vocabulary exposure alone.

The child also shows speech sound patterns that reduce clarity. A separate speech sound diagnosis is deferred pending a broader bilingual sample and monitoring during treatment. Hearing history remains relevant because fluctuating access could affect listening. The current report does not establish a hearing disorder or attribute the language findings to hearing alone.

Strengths include social interest, meaningful gestures, functional vocabulary, family engagement, and learning with contextual teaching. Prognosis for functional improvement is favorable with consistent support, although the rate cannot be predicted from this session. Progress should be measured through communication across partners and settings, alongside structured treatment data.

RECOMMENDATIONS / RECOMENDACIONES

Begin bilingual speech-language treatment under [[KEEP|92507]] with modifier [[KEEP|GN]], subject to coverage requirements. Initial treatment should combine direct interaction with caregiver coaching. Use [[KEEP|Enhanced Milieu Teaching]], [[KEEP|Focused Stimulation]], and principles from [[KEEP|Hanen]] to increase meaningful practice during routines. Select strategies according to response and family preference.

Continue Spanish at home and provide English opportunities through preschool and community activities. Adults should use comfortable, natural language. Follow the child's focus, model a slightly longer message, and pause. For example, expand “[[KEEP|agua]]” to “[[KEEP|quiero agua]]” or “[[KEEP|más agua fría]]” without requiring repeated correction before honoring the request.

Teach understanding of relationships through movement and objects before relying on picture worksheets. Practice “[[KEEP|under]],” “[[KEEP|behind]],” “[[KEEP|debajo]],” and “[[KEEP|detrás]]” within play. Use [[KEEP|mod cues]] initially, then reduce support as performance improves. Check the same concept with different materials and both familiar and unfamiliar partners.

Use shared books with [[KEEP|Dialogic Reading]] strategies. Adults can comment, offer choices, and invite short descriptions. Questions should support participation rather than become a rapid test. Accept a gesture or mixed-language response, then provide a clear spoken model. Coordinate selected classroom vocabulary with home activities when the family finds that useful.
