## 10. Clinical Impressions

### 10.1 Integrated findings

The assessment supports receptive and expressive language needs that affect participation across home and preschool settings. Difficulties appeared in both languages, although English exposure influenced task performance. The child’s bilingual development does not itself explain the full pattern of reported and observed concerns.

Speech sound errors also reduce clarity. Priorities include patterns that affect many words and occur across languages. Spanish sound development and English sound development require separate consideration.

Strengths include social interest, symbolic play, clear communicative intent, caregiver involvement, and a positive response to supported teaching. These strengths provide useful entry points for treatment.
