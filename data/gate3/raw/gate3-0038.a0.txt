CLINICAL IMPRESSIONS

The combined findings support mixed receptive-expressive language difficulties and a speech sound disorder affecting functional communication. Difficulties appeared in both languages, persisted during familiar activities, and affected classroom and home participation. Bilingual exposure does not explain the full observed pattern. The English standardized scores provide supporting descriptive information but are not the sole basis for these impressions.

Strengths include social interest, pretend play, responsiveness to visual models, and caregiver engagement. The child can communicate clear intentions and benefits from brief, meaningful teaching. Needs include understanding longer messages, organizing sentences, describing events, improving speech clarity, and repairing misunderstood communication. Intervention should connect these skills to activities the child already values.

The clinical diagnoses are ICD-10 [[KEEP|F80.2]] and ICD-10 [[KEEP|F80.0]]. Educational eligibility remains the responsibility of the school team. Prognosis for functional improvement is favorable with consistent support, although the rate of progress cannot be predicted from this evaluation. Hearing changes, attendance, practice opportunities, and classroom demands may influence outcomes.

RECOMMENDATIONS

Begin speech-language intervention with a bilingual clinician when available. An initial schedule of weekly direct treatment with caregiver participation is reasonable. Review the schedule against functional progress and family access. Use English and Spanish according to communication partners and routines. The family should continue meaningful Spanish interaction rather than reducing the home language.

Teach comprehension through concrete activities, visible sequences, and gradual increases in sentence complexity. Ask the child to show or explain what she will do. Avoid relying only on repetition or yes-or-no confirmation. Provide processing time before adding another direction. Practice unfamiliar vocabulary in several relevant contexts, including books, play, and classroom routines.

Build expressive language through responsive modeling, expansions, and opportunities to describe actions. Models should be short enough to imitate and useful enough to reuse. Accept gestures and meaningful language mixing while supporting clearer messages. Teach repair strategies such as showing, pointing, adding a word, and asking for help. Coordinate speech practice with words that matter to the child.

Share this report with the school team only through the family's authorized process. Classroom supports should include visual directions, previewed vocabulary, supported peer play, and opportunities to answer without time pressure.
