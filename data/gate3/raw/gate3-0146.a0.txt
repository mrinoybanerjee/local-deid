Findings support a mixed receptive-expressive language disorder, [[KEEP|ICD-10 F80.2]], with a co-occurring speech sound disorder, [[KEEP|ICD-10 F80.0]]. Difficulties affect comprehension of longer directions, grammatical organization, message expansion, and intelligibility. Functional effects occur during home routines and classroom participation. This impression reflects the combined evidence, not an isolated test cutoff. Severity judged moderate for functional language needs; speech clarity also requires intervention.

Language differences alone do not explain the profile. Weaknesses appeared during familiar Spanish tasks and during supported English tasks. Parents and staff described comparable communication breakdowns. Code-switching, Spanish influence on English production, and uneven vocabulary distribution across languages were treated as language variation. Los dos idiomas count toward communication ability. Treatment should support the family's preferred bilingual environment.

Strengths include social interest, shared attention, symbolic play, persistence, and improvement with models. These strengths support participation in direct therapy and caregiver coaching. Learning response was positive but depended on substantial scaffolding. Limited independent generalization suggests that repeated practice within meaningful routines will be necessary. Family involvement and preschool collaboration can help maintain consistent language models across settings.

Speech patterns affecting shared sounds warrant treatment consideration. English-specific patterns require continued monitoring against bilingual exposure and developmental expectations. Stimulability suggests an initial focus on final consonants and velar placement in meaningful words. No single error pattern establishes a motor planning disorder. Reassess if inconsistency, disrupted transitions, or unusual prosody become prominent in future samples.

Current communication needs exceed what classroom exposure alone is likely to address. Skilled SLP services are indicated to select appropriate bilingual targets, adjust cueing, monitor generalization, and coach communication partners. No feeding treatment indicated from today's referral information. Medical follow-up remains with established providers. Hearing changes, recurrent ear symptoms, or loss of skills should prompt reassessment through the appropriate clinical team.

Prognosis for improved functional communication is favorable with consistent attendance, accessible practice, and coordinated supports. Exact rate of progress cannot be predicted from one visit. Barriers include rotating caregiver schedules and reduced intelligibility outside familiar routines. Plan should use brief activities that several adults can implement.
