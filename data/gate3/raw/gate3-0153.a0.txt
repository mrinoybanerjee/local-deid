A: ANÁLISIS / IMPRESIONES

Findings support mixed receptive-expressive language disorder and speech sound disorder affecting functional communication across Spanish and English. Clinical codes: ICD-10 [[KEEP|F80.2]] and [[KEEP|F80.0]]. Evidence includes below-expected standardized performance, difficulty with familiar concepts in both languages, limited independent explanation, persistent speech patterns, and caregiver/classroom concerns. Bilingual exposure and code-switching are normal features of this child's communication environment.

Language needs extend beyond limited English experience. Spanish performance also showed difficulty understanding novel directions and forming clear explanations. Teaching improved performance, yet independent transfer remained limited. Strong engagement, social interest, gesture, and caregiver responsiveness support intervention. The assessment did not measure every dialect feature or all language contexts; conclusions apply to the sampled skills and corroborating reports.

Speech findings reduce access to classroom participation and peer conversation. Some liquid differences remain developmentally expected; intervention should prioritize patterns with the greatest intelligibility effect. Cross-language analysis is necessary before selecting each target. Avoid counting a Spanish-influenced English production as disordered without examining the relevant sound system, dialect, age, and production in Spanish.

Functional impact: Adults frequently infer needs. Unfamiliar listeners miss key words. Child sometimes stops speaking after unsuccessful repair. Longer directions require support, and classroom explanations remain brief. These limitations affect participation rather than reflecting unwillingness to communicate. Family reports match observed difficulty, although performance may differ with familiar partners and quieter surroundings.

Prognosis: Favorable for measurable functional gains with consistent, bilingual intervention and caregiver participation. Rate of progress cannot be predicted from this visit. Barriers include variable parent work schedules, limited opportunities for individual classroom support, and reduced intelligibility during unfamiliar tasks. Strengths include interest in interaction, responsiveness to models, familiar routine vocabulary, and supportive caregivers.

No feeding therapy recommendation based on current history and observation. Recurrent ear infections warrant continued pediatric follow-up. Current hearing information supports access to speech during this assessment; hearing status may change with illness. A repeat evaluation should be considered if new hearing concerns arise or progress differs substantially from expectations.
