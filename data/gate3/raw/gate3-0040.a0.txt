Impressions and recommendations

The combined findings support a mixed receptive-expressive language disorder and a speech sound disorder in this fictional case. Suggested diagnostic references are ICD-10 [[KEEP|F80.2]] and [[KEEP|F80.0]]. Difficulties affected both languages and limited classroom explanations, independent requesting, and communication with unfamiliar listeners. Bilingual exposure does not explain the full pattern. Strengths included social interest, pretend play, persistent communication attempts, and clear benefit from structured teaching.

Scores require cautious interpretation because no standardized measure captures every bilingual child’s experience. The clinician did not compare separate language scores as if they measured identical exposure. Language sampling and dynamic assessment strengthened the interpretation, while variable attention and limited observation across settings remain constraints. Recommendations should be reviewed with the family and school team after an initial treatment period.

Begin individual speech-language therapy twice weekly, using Spanish and English according to each activity and communication partner. Use focused stimulation, expansions, visual supports, and selected principles from [[KEEP|Hanen]]. Consider a [[KEEP|Cycles]] approach for speech patterns if follow-up probes show stable targets. [[KEEP|PROMPT]] and [[KEEP|PECS]] are not included in the initial plan. Their use would require a documented clinical reason and appropriate clinician training.
