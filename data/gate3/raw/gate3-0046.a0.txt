OUTCOME [[KEEP|1]]: REQUESTS DURING MEALS

By [[DATE|February 28, 2027]], the child will communicate a food, drink, or help request during family meals using a gesture, picture, spoken word, or word combination. The criterion is [[KEEP|4 of 5]] natural opportunities across consecutive observed meal routines, with [[KEEP|min cues]].

Baseline: Requests occurred in [[KEEP|3 of 10]] opportunities without an adult model. Adults often anticipated needs or supplied several items before the child selected one.

Strategies: Place familiar choices within view. Offer a small serving, then pause when another serving is appropriate. Model [[KEEP|"more"]], [[KEEP|"mas"]], [[KEEP|"water"]], or [[KEEP|"agua"]] with a gesture. Accept either language. Respond promptly to a clear request. Do not require imitation before giving food or drink.

The clinician will use [[KEEP|Enhanced Milieu Teaching]] and family coaching. Early practice may include [[KEEP|mod cues]], followed by less adult support. Parents will identify comfortable pauses and signs that the child needs immediate help.

Progress will be reviewed through caregiver examples and brief observation. A request counts when the child directs a clear message toward another person. Repeated adult questions do not count as independent communication.

OUTCOME [[KEEP|2]]: SHARED PLAY AND TURN TAKING

By [[DATE|February 28, 2027]], the child will participate in short play exchanges with an adult, sibling, or classmate. The child will use a gesture or word to request a turn, offer an object, or continue play in [[KEEP|8/10]] opportunities with [[KEEP|min cues]].

Baseline: Exchanges required [[KEEP|max cues]] when another child held a preferred object. Adult-supported exchanges were more successful with duplicate toys.

Strategies: Start with enjoyable games that have clear turns. Adults will model [[KEEP|"my turn"]], [[KEEP|"mi turno"]], [[KEEP|"go"]], and [[KEEP|"va"]]. They will keep turns brief and acknowledge reaching, pointing, and appropriate words. Adults may offer duplicate objects when waiting becomes too difficult.

During assessment, [[CHILD|Majo]] said "[[SIBLING|Nico]] go" while handing over a block. This phrase showed an emerging effort to direct another person's action. The clinician will build on that function through play.

The clinician may introduce selected [[KEEP|Hanen]] interaction strategies, including following the child's interest and balancing turns. The family is not enrolled in a complete parent course. Staff will practice the same strategies during classroom play.

OUTCOME [[KEEP|3]]: PARTICIPATION IN TRANSITIONS

By [[DATE|February 28, 2027]], the child will communicate a choice, protest, or request for help during arrival, cleanup, and departure in [[KEEP|3 of 4]] opportunities with [[KEEP|min cues]].

Baseline: Adults reported crying or dropping to the floor during some departures. A familiar object and advance notice often helped. The team did not observe enough transitions to estimate a reliable frequency.

Strategies: Show a simple picture before changing activities. Use a short statement and allow processing time. Offer a meaningful choice when possible. Model [[KEEP|"help"]], [[KEEP|"ayuda"]], [[KEEP|"all done"]], and [[KEEP|"ya"]]. Honor an appropriate protest when the activity can wait.

Parents will practice during leaving the house. Classroom staff will practice during cleanup and pickup. The clinician will coach adults to reduce rapid questions and use predictable language. Adults will record useful examples rather than count every difficult moment.

Progress will include the child's participation and the family's report of easier routines. The team will adjust the strategies if the child needs more support, becomes distressed, or stops using a previously helpful message.
