IMPRESIONES CLÍNICAS

Findings support a mixed receptive-expressive language disorder and a speech sound disorder in this fictional case. Communication difficulty appeared across Spanish and English, including familiar contexts. Pt’s bilingual exposure explains some vocabulary differences and code-switching. It does not fully explain the reduced comprehension, limited sentence detail, and frequent breakdowns observed across languages.

Language strengths include communicative intent, gesture use, familiar vocabulary, interest in shared play, and a positive response to teaching. Pt can learn new phrases when adults connect language to a meaningful action. He also notices misunderstandings and attempts repair. These strengths provide clear entry points for intervention.

Primary language needs involve understanding directions beyond familiar routines, expanding action vocabulary, answering simple questions, and combining information into clearer messages. Speech needs involve increasing intelligibility through selected sound contrasts and word shapes. Targets should support useful communication rather than increase drill demands without a clear functional purpose.

Functional effects include missed classroom directions, reduced independence during peer play, and reliance on familiar adults to interpret speech. CG reported frustration during pickup and sibling play. The current findings support direct intervention plus CG and classroom coaching. Treatment should preserve Spanish and provide meaningful access to English.

Prognosis for measurable functional improvement is favorable given participation, CG involvement, and learning response. Progress rate remains uncertain. Attendance, hearing status, opportunities for practice, and classroom support can affect outcomes. The clinician will review progress using repeated functional probes, language samples, and reports from the adults who support pt.
