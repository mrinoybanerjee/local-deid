Resultados y estrategias

Outcome [[KEEP|1]]: During meals and play, [[CHILD|Emilio]] will communicate a choice or request through a gesture, sign, picture, sound, or word in [[KEEP|8/10]] natural opportunities across two caregivers. The family will record brief examples during ordinary routines. The clinician will model [[KEEP|Enhanced Milieu Teaching]] strategies, including accessible choices, responsive language models, and pauses. Caregivers will begin with [[KEEP|mod cues]] and reduce support toward [[KEEP|min cues]] when the child initiates more often. Target words include "[[KEEP|más]]", "[[KEEP|agua]]", "[[KEEP|pan]]", "[[KEEP|more]]", and "[[KEEP|open]]", selected according to the routine and language used.

For Outcome [[KEEP|1]], breakfast practice can begin with two foods already available. The caregiver will hold them where the child can see them, name them, and wait. If he points, the caregiver will acknowledge the choice and provide it. A short expansion can follow, such as "[[KEEP|más pan]]". The clinician will ask the caregiver to explain what happened and choose one adjustment. The family may try [[KEEP|Hanen]] responsive interaction strategies during shared play. The team will use caregiver examples to judge whether the approach fits the family’s day.

Outcome [[KEEP|2]]: During dressing, toy play, and transitions, [[CHILD|Emilio]] will request help or indicate a problem in [[KEEP|4 of 5]] opportunities with [[KEEP|min cues]]. Adults will accept a gesture, a help sign, or words such as "[[KEEP|ayuda]]", "[[KEEP|help]]", and "[[KEEP|no]]". The clinician will demonstrate [[KEEP|Functional Communication Training]] within safe, naturally occurring difficulties, such as a closed container or a tangled sleeve. Adults will respond promptly. They will not create distress to obtain a response. Progress will include clearer communication and smoother participation, even when the child’s spoken form remains approximate.

For Outcome [[KEEP|2]], the parents will choose one predictable situation each week. Papá [[PARENT|Daniel]] selected shoes because he usually helps with them after work. The same simple response will follow each attempt: acknowledge, help, and model. If the child becomes upset, the adult will provide support and return to communication later. The coordinator will check whether the selected situations remain practical when work schedules change.

Outcome [[KEEP|3]]: During sibling or childcare play, [[CHILD|Emilio]] will join a shared activity for at least four reciprocal turns in [[KEEP|3 of 4]] observed routines with [[KEEP|mod cues]] or less. Strategies include [[KEEP|Naturalistic Developmental Behavioral Intervention]] techniques within preferred play, simple turn signals, and short language models.
