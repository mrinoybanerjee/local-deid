Resumen / Evaluation Summary

[[CHILD|María José]] participated in a bilingual evaluation with her mother and later joined the clinician for play. She showed strong interest in pretend cooking, picture books, and helping adults organize materials. Her communication included gestures, short phrases, and longer messages when the topic was familiar. Spanish was her stronger language during storytelling and family discussion; English appeared frequently during classroom routines and toy play.

Findings support difficulty understanding longer directions and organizing spoken messages across both languages. Speech sound patterns also reduced clarity, especially in longer words. These findings reflect information from several settings and do not rest on an English score alone. The family’s language practices provide useful communication resources. Continued use of Spanish at home is recommended.

Her mother described the main concern this way: “Ella sabe lo que quiere decir, pero se queda buscando las palabras.” The evaluation identified strengths that can support treatment: shared attention, persistence, symbolic play, and willingness to try again when an adult gives clear support.

Comprensión y expresión

During [[KEEP|Auditory Comprehension]] tasks, the child identified familiar objects, actions, and basic spatial relationships. She had difficulty retaining directions with several elements and interpreting sentences that changed the order of people or actions. She sometimes selected the last object named, especially when pictures contained several plausible choices.

During [[KEEP|Expressive Communication]] tasks, she named familiar items and combined words to request, describe, and protest. Longer responses often omitted information that a listener needed. Spanish verb forms and English sentence structure varied, but errors were interpreted in context rather than counted automatically as evidence of impairment.

In play, [[CHILD|María José]] produced [[KEEP|"yo want agua"]], [[KEEP|"la bebé está sleepy"]], and [[KEEP|"he go allá"]]. Code-switching itself was appropriate to the interaction. Difficulty appeared when she could not explain who acted, what happened, or why.

After a brief model, she followed novel directions in [[KEEP|8/10]] opportunities with [[KEEP|min cues]], compared with [[KEEP|4/10]] independently. She added a missing event in [[KEEP|3 of 5]] picture sequences with [[KEEP|mod cues]]. This response suggests benefit from structured teaching.

Impresiones clínicas

The combined findings support a mixed receptive-expressive language disorder and a speech sound disorder affecting participation across Spanish and English. Proposed diagnostic codes are [[KEEP|ICD-10 F80.2]] and [[KEEP|ICD-10 F80.0]]. The clinician based these impressions on functional concerns, bilingual assessment, speech samples, and observed response to teaching.

Limited English exposure alone does not explain the difficulties described in Spanish. At the same time, the evaluation does not classify code-switching or regional Spanish forms as errors. The child showed meaningful learning potential when adults reduced task demands and gave specific models.

This evaluation represents one visit. Classroom observation and treatment data should refine priorities. Hearing documentation supports access to spoken instruction, but the family should report any new hearing concerns. Prognosis for improved functional communication is favorable with coordinated support.

Recomendaciones / Plan

1. Begin bilingual speech-language therapy weekly, with caregiver participation. Use [[KEEP|CPT 92507]] and modifier [[KEEP|GN]] when appropriate. Select activities from family routines and classroom needs.

2. Establish goal [[KEEP|1]]: follow directions containing two linked actions in [[KEEP|8/10]] opportunities with [[KEEP|min cues]]. Confirm understanding through action, rather than requiring verbal repetition.

3. Establish goal [[KEEP|2]]: describe a short event with the person, action, and relevant detail in [[KEEP|4 of 5]] opportunities with [[KEEP|mod cues]]. Accept either language and natural code-switching.

4. Establish goal [[KEEP|3]]: improve production of selected speech targets to [[KEEP|80%]] accuracy during meaningful play with [[KEEP|mod cues]]. Begin with stimulable sounds and words that affect daily understanding.

5. Use [[KEEP|focused stimulation]], [[KEEP|expansions]], and [[KEEP|recasts]] during play. Consider [[KEEP|Cycles]] for persistent phonological patterns after collecting a broader speech sample. Explain each approach to caregivers in their preferred language.

6. Support communication repair with modeled phrases such as [[KEEP|"te enseño"]], [[KEEP|"I mean this one"]], and [[KEEP|"otra vez, más despacio"]]. Praise successful clarification without requiring perfect pronunciation.

7. Continue Spanish at home. Encourage shared books, cooking conversations, and family stories. Parents can respond naturally in the language they use most comfortably.

8. Coordinate classroom supports with Miss [[TEACHER|Ana]]. Pair directions with pictures, allow processing time, and provide a model before group storytelling. Offer predictable chances to speak with peers.

9. Review progress after a treatment block. Track functional participation alongside task accuracy. Discuss school eligibility through the district’s own evaluation process.
