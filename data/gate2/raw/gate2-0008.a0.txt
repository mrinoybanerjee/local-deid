Receptive language findings

[[CHILD|Elena]] identified familiar objects, actions, and several basic concepts in Spanish. She followed routine directions when the objects were visible. She understood simple negatives and selected pictures showing familiar actions. English performance was strongest for classroom vocabulary and common routine instructions.

Difficulty increased when directions included several attributes or required holding information before acting. On a structured probe, she followed related two-step directions in [[KEEP|4/8]] opportunities without added support. Performance increased to [[KEEP|7/8]] with [[KEEP|mod cues]]. The support included repetition, a gesture, and reduced sentence length.

She answered concrete questions more consistently than questions requiring an explanation or prediction. For a picture-based inference task, she responded accurately in [[KEEP|2 of 6]] opportunities initially and [[KEEP|5 of 6]] after teaching with [[KEEP|max cues]]. Responses improved, but she did not apply the same strategy consistently to new pictures.

These findings suggest that language load affected understanding across both languages. Limited English experience could explain some English responses. It did not fully explain similar difficulty during familiar Spanish tasks. Her success with visible materials provides a useful starting point for intervention.

Expressive language findings

[[CHILD|Elena]] used single words, short combinations, and occasional longer sentences. Spanish messages included [[KEEP|"mamá mira perro"]], [[KEEP|"yo quiero esa"]], and [[KEEP|"se cayó aquí"]]. Impressions

The combined findings support a mixed receptive-expressive language disorder and a speech sound disorder. The proposed diagnostic codes are [[KEEP|F80.2]] and [[KEEP|F80.0]]. The language concerns appeared across both languages, affected daily communication, and persisted when the clinician used familiar Spanish materials. Speech patterns also reduced intelligibility across languages.

Bilingual exposure does not explain the full pattern. Some differences reflect experience, dialect, or expected development, and the clinician excluded those differences from treatment priorities. The normative articulation result contributes limited supporting information. Functional observations and cross-language patterns carry more weight.

Strengths include social interest, shared attention, symbolic play, family participation, and a positive response to brief teaching. These strengths support intervention within familiar routines. The assessment did not establish a motor speech diagnosis or identify a need for feeding treatment.

The findings should guide treatment planning and discussion with the educational team. They do not independently determine school eligibility. The family and school team should consider classroom demands, available supports, and their own assessment procedures.

Recommendations and initial goals

Begin bilingual speech-language treatment using Spanish and English according to each activity and communication partner. Preserve Spanish for family relationships and daily routines. Treatment should build understanding, message organization, and intelligibility. Use [[KEEP|Enhanced Milieu Teaching]], [[KEEP|focused stimulation]], and [[KEEP|recasts]] during meaningful play. Consider [[KEEP|Cycles]] for persistent speech patterns after obtaining additional connected-speech data.

Goal [[KEEP|1]]: Follow related two-step directions with [[KEEP|80%]] accuracy across activities using [[KEEP|min cues]].

Goal [[KEEP|2]]: Produce a message that includes a person, action, and needed detail in [[KEEP|8/10]] opportunities using [[KEEP|min cues]].

Goal [[KEEP|3]]: Produce selected functional words containing [[KEEP|/k/]] with [[KEEP|80%]] accuracy using [[KEEP|mod cues]], then reduce support.

Goal [[KEEP|4]]: Repair an unclear message through repetition, a gesture, or added information in [[KEEP|4 of 5]] opportunities using [[KEEP|min cues]].

Adults should pause after a question, accept a response in either language, and expand the message naturally. During books, discuss actions and simple sequences. Practice should remain brief and successful. Avoid repeated correction during spontaneous conversation.
