**Impresiones clínicas**

Findings support a mixed receptive-expressive language disorder, [[KEEP|ICD-10 F80.2]], with a co-occurring speech sound disorder, [[KEEP|ICD-10 F80.0]]. The conclusion rests on converging evidence: parent concerns, teacher observations, difficulties across both languages, structured assessment, and reduced functional clarity. Bilingual exposure does not explain the full pattern. The child shows difficulty understanding and formulating messages even in familiar Spanish routines with supportive communication partners.

Speech differences require continued monitoring as vocabulary and connected speech expand. Some English productions may reflect Spanish influence or limited experience. Other patterns affect familiar words in both languages and reduce understanding. Intervention should therefore target the patterns with the clearest functional effect and the strongest evidence of difficulty. The current assessment does not support treating every English sound difference as a separate error or requiring identical performance across languages.

Strengths include social interest, pretend play, purposeful gestures, willingness to imitate within play, and improved performance with visual support. [[CHILD|Elian]] notices communication breakdowns and seeks adult help. His family already provides rich interaction in Spanish and English. These strengths make daily routines useful settings for treatment. The goal is a clearer, more complete message while preserving his access to both languages and his connection with family members.

Interpretive limits include the relatively short observation period, parent-reported developmental history, and the descriptive use of English speech prompts. Attention varied across activities. The score table cannot capture every skill or predict the exact rate of progress. The clinician recommends reassessment through treatment data, classroom observations, and family feedback. Any later change in hearing, health, or participation should prompt review of how that change affects communication.
