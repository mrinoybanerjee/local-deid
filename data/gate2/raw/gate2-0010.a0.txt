Language needs exceed isolated vocabulary delay. Visual support and repetition improve performance. Independent carryover remains limited. Classroom demands likely expose these weaknesses during group directions, pretend play, and event sharing. Functional progress should therefore include successful communication in routines, not only accuracy on structured clinician prompts.

Speech findings suggest a phonological component. Velar fronting and syllable reduction reduce intelligibility across relevant contexts. Some English sound differences require cautious interpretation because exposure and cross-language influence affect production. Current evidence does not establish childhood apraxia of speech. Stable substitutions and functional prosody weigh against making that diagnosis from this visit. Additional connected samples and treatment response will help refine the speech formulation.
