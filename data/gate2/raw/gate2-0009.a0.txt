IMPRESSIONS

Findings indicate mixed receptive and expressive language difficulties affecting classroom participation and family communication. The concern extends beyond reduced English exposure because functional difficulties were reported in both languages and remained apparent during familiar Spanish activities. Strengths include social interest, symbolic play, communication intent, vocabulary distributed across languages, and a positive response to modeling.

Speech sound differences also reduce clarity. Some observed patterns require monitoring within bilingual development, while persistent substitutions across languages support direct intervention. This evaluation supports the working diagnoses [[KEEP|F80.2]], mixed receptive-expressive language disorder, and [[KEEP|F80.0]], speech sound disorder.

Diagnostic interpretation remains provisional regarding the precise severity of speech sound impairment. A longer connected speech sample across partners would improve that estimate. The present assessment does not establish a broader developmental diagnosis. Hearing access should continue to be monitored through the existing medical team.

RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Begin individual speech-language therapy weekly, with caregiver participation and coordination with preschool staff. Use [[KEEP|Enhanced Milieu Teaching]] and [[KEEP|Focused Stimulation]] within meaningful activities. Review response after twelve weeks.

2. Support Spanish and English. Adults should use the language in which they communicate most comfortably. Continue family conversations, stories, songs, and pretend play in both languages.

3. Address following directions, requesting help, answering simple questions, and explaining immediate events. Initial [[KEEP|goal 1]] should target two-step directions with visual support, progressing from [[KEEP|mod cues]] toward [[KEEP|min cues]].

4. Initial [[KEEP|goal 2]] should target functional phrase expansion in [[KEEP|8/10]] opportunities during familiar routines. Initial [[KEEP|goal 3]] should address selected speech sound contrasts after additional bilingual sampling.

5. Use [[KEEP|minimal pairs therapy]] when a stable contrast can be selected across the child's languages. Establish accurate [[KEEP|/k/]] in useful words before increasing phrase demands.

6. Teachers should pair new directions with pictures, pause for processing, and check understanding through action. Family members should model a clear phrase, then allow time for a response without requiring repeated correction.

7. Maintain follow-up with the primary care physician and audiologist. Schedule a progress review on [[DATE|January 9, 2027]]. Reassess goals using family priorities, classroom participation, and documented performance.
