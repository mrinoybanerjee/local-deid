CLINICAL IMPRESSIONS

Results of this bilingual evaluation indicate that [[CHILD|Teo]] presents with a mixed receptive-expressive language disorder affecting both English and Spanish, with relatively stronger skills in Spanish, his primary home language, consistent with typical patterns of bilingual language dominance rather than a language-specific deficit. Scores on the [[KEEP|PLS-5]] and [[KEEP|PLS-5 Spanish]] fell in the low range in both languages, with Spanish scores falling closer to, though still below, average limits. Performance on the [[KEEP|BESA]] further supports deficits across both languages in morphosyntax, with somewhat stronger semantic skills in Spanish. These cross-linguistic patterns are consistent with a true language disorder rather than second-language acquisition alone, as a disorder affecting only one language, or stronger performance in the child's less-dominant language, would instead suggest typical bilingual development.

[[CHILD|Teo]] also presents with a speech sound disorder characterized by phonological process errors (final consonant deletion, cluster reduction, stopping) that are present, though to differing degrees, across both languages, further supporting a disorder affecting underlying phonological systems rather than dialectal or second-language transfer alone. Given his history of recurrent otitis media with effusion and tube placement, a component of his speech-language delay may be attributable in part to a history of fluctuating conductive hearing loss during a critical developmental window, though current hearing is within normal limits.

[[CHILD|Teo]]'s strong social motivation, good stimulability, and relatively intact pragmatic foundation are favorable prognostic indicators. His family's engagement, bilingual home environment, and existing relationship with the [[AGENCY|Cabarrus County CDSA]] provide a strong foundation for intervention. Continued support for both languages is recommended, as bilingualism is not contributing to, and should not be discontinued as a strategy for addressing, his language disorder.
