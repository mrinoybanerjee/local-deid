IMPRESIONES CLÍNICAS / CLINICAL IMPRESSIONS

Based on standardized assessment results, parent report, and clinical observation, [[CHILD|Mateo]] presents with a Developmental Language Disorder affecting both receptive and expressive domains, with expressive language more significantly impacted, present across both of his languages, Spanish and English. His scores fall below the eligible range on standardized bilingual measures in at least one domain, and his performance is consistent with a true language disorder rather than typical bilingual language development, given that deficits are observed across both languages rather than confined to the language of lesser exposure.

Concurrently, [[CHILD|Mateo]] demonstrates a moderate speech sound disorder characterized by phonological error patterns that cross both languages, including final consonant deletion, cluster reduction, gliding, and liquid distortion. Given his documented history of recurrent otitis media with effusion and subsequent tympanostomy tube placement, a component of his speech-language delay may be attributable to fluctuating conductive hearing loss during a critical period of early phonological and lexical development; however, the persistence of errors following resolution of middle ear pathology (confirmed by passing audiological screening on [[DATE|10/02/2025]]) suggests that hearing history alone does not fully account for his current profile.

[[CHILD|Mateo]]'s pragmatic communication skills, including joint attention, social reciprocity, and functional play, appear to be developing appropriately relative to his language level, and no concerns regarding autism spectrum features were identified during this evaluation. His frustration behaviors (pulling, pointing, vocalizing) appear secondary to limited expressive language rather than indicative of a primary behavioral or social-communication disorder.

RECOMENDACIONES / RECOMMENDATIONS

1. [[CHILD|Mateo]] meets eligibility criteria for early intervention services under Part C through [[AGENCY|CDSA de Durham]], based on standardized scores falling more than [[KEEP|1.5 standard deviations]] below the mean in the expressive language domain across both languages.

2. It is recommended that [[CHILD|Mateo]] receive individual speech-language therapy services a minimum of [[KEEP|two times]] per week for [[KEEP|45-minute]] sessions, delivered bilingually to support development in both Spanish and English, consistent with current best-practice guidance for bilingual language learners with a true language disorder.

3. Therapy should incorporate a combination of naturalistic, parent-coaching approaches, including principles from the [[KEEP|Hanen]] "It Takes Two to Talk" program, alongside more structured motor-based approaches such as [[KEEP|PROMPT]] to address the speech sound errors noted above. A [[KEEP|Cycles]] phonological approach is also recommended to target the cluster reduction and final consonant deletion patterns identified in both languages.

4. [[PARENT|Lucía]] and [[PARENT|Diego]] should be included as active participants in therapy sessions and coached in strategies to expand [[CHILD|Mateo]]'s utterances at home, with specific emphasis on modeling two- and three-word combinations in Spanish, the family's primary language.

6. Coordination with [[DAYCARE|Centro de Cuidado Infantil Pequeños Pasos]] staff, including Ms. [[TEACHER|Lopez]], is recommended to ensure consistent use of simplified language models and visual supports across environments.
