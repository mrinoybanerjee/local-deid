VII. PRODUCCION DE SONIDOS DEL HABLA / SPEECH SOUND PRODUCTION

Connected speech samples were obtained in both English and Spanish during structured play and conversation. In English, [[CHILD|Mateo]]'s intelligibility was judged to be approximately 60 percent to unfamiliar listeners in context and improved to roughly 80 percent to familiar listeners such as his parents and the examiner by the end of the session. In Spanish, intelligibility was estimated at 70 percent to unfamiliar listeners and approximately 85 percent to familiar listeners.

Phonological error patterns observed included consistent final consonant deletion (for example, "ca" for [[KEEP|"cat"]] and "pe" for [[KEEP|"perro"]], deleting the final /r/), cluster reduction (for example, [[KEEP|"paw"]] for [[KEEP|"spoon"]] and [[KEEP|"mesa"]] produced as [[KEEP|"mea"]]), and fronting of velar sounds, with [[KEEP|/k/]] frequently produced as [[KEEP|[t]]] and [[KEEP|/g/]] produced as [[KEEP|[d]]]. During a structured articulation probe targeting word-initial [[KEEP|/k/]], [[CHILD|Mateo]] produced the target sound correctly in [[KEEP|8/10]] trials with [[KEEP|mod cues]], an accuracy rate of approximately [[KEEP|80%]]. On a separate probe targeting final consonants in English, accuracy was [[KEEP|3/10]], or [[KEEP|30%]], even with [[KEEP|max cues]].

During play with the stuffed dog, [[CHILD|Mateo]] was heard to say "[[PET|Rocky]] go fast" while running a toy across the table, producing the target as approximately [[KEEP|"Wocky go fah"]] with gliding of /r/ and final consonant deletion. Later in the session, when shown a family photograph, he pointed and said "[[SIBLING|Camila]] no quiere," demonstrating appropriate use of a two-word combination with age-appropriate negation structure despite reduced intelligibility of the proper name itself.

Oral mechanism examination revealed structures within normal limits for strength, symmetry, and range of motion. No signs of dysarthria or significant oral-motor weakness were observed. Diadochokinetic rates for repeated syllable sequences were judged to be mildly slow but within functional limits for a child of his age. No concerns regarding voice quality, resonance, or fluency were noted; speech rate and rhythm were judged to be within normal limits in both languages, with no evidence of stuttering-like behaviors.

VIII. ANALISIS DE MUESTRA DE LENGUAJE / LANGUAGE SAMPLE ANALYSIS

A 100-utterance connected speech sample was collected and transcribed, combining both English and Spanish utterances consistent with [[CHILD|Mateo]]'s natural code-switching patterns. Mean length of utterance in morphemes (MLU-m) was calculated at [[KEEP|2.8]], which falls below the expected range of [[KEEP|3.5-4.5]] morphemes for a child of his chronological age. [[CHILD|Mateo]] primarily used two- and three-word combinations such as [[KEEP|"mas agua"]], [[KEEP|"Rocky come"]], and [[KEEP|"no quiero eso"]], with limited use of grammatical morphemes including plural -s, third person singular -s, and regular past tense -ed in English, and limited use of verb conjugation beyond present tense forms in Spanish.

Code-switching was frequent and appeared to follow typical patterns for a young bilingual child rather than confusion between the two language systems; for example, [[CHILD|Mateo]] was observed to say "[[KEEP|quiero jugar]] with [[KEEP|Rocky]]," appropriately embedding an English prepositional phrase within a Spanish-dominant utterance, labeled under the SALT transcription convention as [[KEEP|[EW]]] for English word insertion. This pattern of intra-sentential code-switching is considered developmentally appropriate and was not counted as an error in scoring.

Comprehension of basic following directions was assessed informally. [[CHILD|Mateo]] followed one-step directions with [[KEEP|90%]] accuracy and two-step directions with [[KEEP|60%]] accuracy, or [[KEEP|6 of 10]] trials correct, in both English and Spanish. He demonstrated understanding of basic spatial concepts (in, on, under) with [[KEEP|8/10]] accuracy but showed inconsistent comprehension of temporal concepts (before, after) at [[KEEP|40%]] accuracy.

X. IMPRESIONES / IMPRESSIONS

[[CHILD|Mateo]] presents with a moderate expressive language delay and mild to moderate receptive language delay across both Spanish and English, consistent with a diagnosis of a primary language disorder rather than typical second-language acquisition, given that delays are present across both of his languages relative to bilingual developmental expectations. His speech sound production is characterized by multiple phonological processes including final consonant deletion, cluster reduction, and velar fronting, which are contributing significantly to reduced intelligibility across both languages and across listener familiarity. His receptive language skills, while also below age expectations, are relatively stronger than his expressive skills, a pattern consistent with his history and with typical early intervention trajectories.

[[CHILD|Mateo]]'s nonverbal cognitive skills, as estimated by the [[KEEP|Bayley-4]] Cognitive Composite, fall within the average range, supporting that his language and speech sound difficulties are not attributable to global developmental delay. His bilingual language environment is considered a strength to be supported rather than a cause of his delays, and services should continue to be delivered using a bilingual approach consistent with current best practice guidelines for dual-language learners.
