LANGUAGE SAMPLE AND SPEECH SOUND ANALYSIS

A 100-utterance connected speech sample was collected across both languages during play-based interaction. In Spanish, [[CHILD|Mateo]]'s mean length of utterance was [[KEEP|2.1]] morphemes, below expectations for his age. He produced mostly two-word combinations such as "[[KEEP|más agua]]" and "[[KEEP|no quiero]]," with limited use of grammatical morphemes such as plural markers and verb conjugations. In English, his MLU was [[KEEP|1.6]] morphemes, with single-word and occasional two-word utterances such as "[[KEEP|more juice]]" and "[[KEEP|go bye-bye]]."

Articulation testing and conversational sampling identified a moderate-to-severe speech sound disorder affecting intelligibility in both languages. [[CHILD|Mateo]] demonstrated gliding of liquids, producing [[KEEP|/w/]] for [[KEEP|/ɾ/]] in Spanish (e.g., "[[KEEP|pewo]]" for "[[KEEP|perro]]") and [[KEEP|/w/]] for [[KEEP|/r/]] in English. He also demonstrated final consonant deletion and stopping of fricatives, producing [[KEEP|[t]]] for [[KEEP|/s/]] in word-initial position (e.g., "[[KEEP|topa]]" for "[[KEEP|sopa]]"). Overall intelligibility to unfamiliar listeners was estimated at approximately [[KEEP|40%]] in connected speech, improving to roughly [[KEEP|65%]] with known context. On a single-word naming task, [[CHILD|Mateo]] correctly produced target consonants in [[KEEP|8/10]] trials for early-developing sounds but only [[KEEP|3/10]] trials for later-developing sounds such as [[KEEP|/r/]] and consonant clusters, an accuracy rate of approximately [[KEEP|80%]] and [[KEEP|30%]] respectively.

Dynamic assessment, in which this clinician modeled target structures and provided [[KEEP|mod cues]] (moderate verbal and visual cueing), indicated that [[CHILD|Mateo]] was responsive to instruction: with [[KEEP|mod cues]], he correctly imitated subject-verb-object word order in [[KEEP|7 of 10]] trials, up from [[KEEP|2 of 10]] trials without support. This pattern of improved performance with cueing and modeling is a favorable prognostic indicator and is inconsistent with a primary diagnosis of autism spectrum disorder or global developmental delay in isolation.

ORAL-MOTOR AND FEEDING OBSERVATIONS

Oral-motor examination revealed structures within normal limits for strength, symmetry, and range of motion of the lips, tongue, and jaw. Diadochokinetic rates were judged age-appropriate informally. No overt dysarthric or apraxic features were observed, though inconsistent sound substitutions across repeated productions of the same word (e.g., "[[KEEP|perro]]" produced as "[[KEEP|pewo]]," "[[KEEP|peo]]," and "[[KEEP|pedo]]" across three trials) raise a mild concern for a motor planning component that should be monitored over time. No feeding or swallowing concerns were reported by [[PARENT|Lucía Rodríguez Flores]], and [[CHILD|Mateo]] eats a full range of textures without difficulty.

CLINICAL IMPRESSIONS

Based on standardized testing, language sampling, dynamic assessment, and parent report, [[CHILD|Mateo Alejandro Flores]] presents with a moderate-to-severe mixed receptive-expressive language disorder affecting both Spanish and English, consistent with [[KEEP|F80.2]], along with a moderate-to-severe speech sound disorder affecting intelligibility in both languages. These findings are not attributable solely to typical second-language acquisition or bilingual development, as [[CHILD|Mateo]] demonstrates delays relative to same-age peers in his dominant home language, Spanish, as well as in English. His pattern of stimulability and positive response to cueing during dynamic assessment suggests good candidacy for intervention and a reasonably favorable prognosis with consistent services.

The combination of expressive and receptive deficits, reduced intelligibility, and history of recurrent otitis media with tube placement is consistent with a multifactorial presentation in which early and recurrent conductive hearing loss likely contributed to, though does not fully account for, his current language and speech profile. Continued audiological monitoring is recommended given his history.

[[CHILD|Mateo]] does not currently present with features suggestive of autism spectrum disorder; his social engagement, joint attention, pretend play, and response to intervention during dynamic assessment were all age-appropriate or favorable. No further autism-specific evaluation is recommended at this time, though the team should remain alert to any emerging concerns.
