IMPRESSIONS

Based on standardized testing, parent and teacher report, and behavioral observation, [[CHILD|Mateo]] presents with a moderate-to-severe expressive language delay and a mild-to-moderate receptive language delay affecting both Spanish and English, consistent with a primary language impairment rather than a language difference secondary to bilingual exposure alone. This impression is supported by the consistency of delay patterns across both of [[CHILD|Mateo]]'s languages, as recommended practice for bilingual assessment, and by the relative strengths noted in his home-dominant language (Spanish) compared to the less-exposed language (English), which argues against a language-difference-only explanation. [[CHILD|Mateo]] additionally presents with a mild speech sound disorder characterized by reduced intelligibility and persistent developmental error patterns beyond expected age of resolution, again consistent across both languages. No concerns regarding hearing, oral-motor structure and function, or social communication were identified at this time, though continued monitoring of pragmatic skills is recommended as expressive language improves. Relevant diagnostic coding for today's visit includes [[KEEP|ICD-10 F80.1]] (Expressive language disorder), [[KEEP|ICD-10 F80.2]] (Mixed receptive-expressive language disorder), and [[KEEP|ICD-10 F80.0]] (Phonological disorder), with [[KEEP|CPT 92523]] billed for today's combined speech and language evaluation.
