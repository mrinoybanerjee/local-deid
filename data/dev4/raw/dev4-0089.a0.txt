**7. CLINICAL IMPRESSIONS**

[[CHILD|Mateo]] presents with a significant expressive and receptive language delay across both Spanish and English, consistent with a diagnosis of Language Disorder affecting both of his languages, rather than a difference attributable solely to bilingual exposure. His performance on the [[KEEP|Rossetti Infant-Toddler Language Scale]], [[KEEP|Bayley-4]], and [[KEEP|PLS-5]] converge to indicate expressive language functioning at approximately the [[KEEP|14-16 month]] level against a chronological age of [[KEEP|2;7]], representing a delay of roughly [[KEEP|12-14 months]]. Receptive language, while also delayed, is relatively stronger than expressive language, a pattern consistent with an expressive-predominant language disorder.

Articulation and phonology are also significantly delayed, with a restricted consonant inventory and consistent final consonant deletion that further limits intelligibility. Given the combination of limited expressive vocabulary, limited phonemic inventory, and parent report of frustration during communication breakdowns, [[CHILD|Mateo]] meets criteria for early intervention eligibility under both language delay and speech sound disorder categories per North Carolina's Infant-Toddler Program guidelines.

No concerns regarding oral-motor structure, hearing acuity (pending formal audiological evaluation), or social-pragmatic skills were identified at this time. [[CHILD|Mateo]]'s play skills, joint attention, and nonverbal communication are age-appropriate strengths that will support intervention.
