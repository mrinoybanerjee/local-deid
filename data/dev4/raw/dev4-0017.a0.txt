[[CHILD|Teo]] presented with a moderate-to-severe phonological disorder affecting intelligibility in both languages. Phonological processes noted across English and Spanish samples included: final consonant deletion (e.g., "ca_" for "cat"), stopping of fricatives (e.g., [[KEEP|/s/]] → [[KEEP|[t]]], [[KEEP|/f/]] → [[KEEP|[p]]]), cluster reduction (e.g., "pay" for "play"), and gliding of liquids (e.g., [[KEEP|/ɾ/]] → [[KEEP|[w]]] in Spanish words such as "ca[[KEEP|[w]]]o" for "carro"). Intelligibility in connected speech was estimated at [[KEEP|30%]] for unfamiliar listeners and [[KEEP|60%]] for familiar listeners.

A brief excerpt from the conversational language sample, transcribed using SALT conventions, illustrates these patterns:

C: "el perro [EW] co[[KEEP|[w]]]e" (the dog runs)
E: ¿quién corre?
C: "[[PET|Rocky]] y [[SIBLING|Sol]] jugaron en el patio" ("[[PET|Rocky]] and [[SIBLING|Sol]] played in the yard")

These samples were judged consistent with a phonological disorder present across both of [[CHILD|Teo]]'s languages, rather than a language-specific articulation difference attributable to typical bilingual phonological development.

5.3 Receptive and Expressive Language

In English, [[CHILD|Teo]] demonstrated difficulty following two-step directions without visual support and produced primarily 3-4 word utterances, often omitting grammatical morphemes (e.g., "he go school" for "he goes to school"). In Spanish, similar morphosyntactic difficulty was noted, including inconsistent use of gender agreement and verb conjugation (e.g., "la perro" instead of "el perro"; "yo quiere" instead of "yo quiero"). These error patterns were present across both languages at a rate exceeding what would be expected for a typically developing bilingual child of his age, supporting a primary language impairment rather than a difference attributable to second-language acquisition alone.

During the narrative retell task, [[CHILD|Teo]] produced an 8-utterance story with limited use of connectives and frequent code-switching mid-sentence, such as: "y entonces el dog, I mean el perro, se fue a la casa de [[SIBLING|Sol]]." This type of intrasentential code-switching is a typical and expected feature of bilingual language use and was not counted as an error; however, the overall narrative was judged brief and poorly sequenced relative to age expectations in both languages.

7. SUMMARY OF FINDINGS / RESUMEN DE HALLAZGOS

[[CHILD|Teo]] presents with a moderate-to-severe phonological disorder and a mixed receptive-expressive language disorder affecting both English and Spanish, consistent with a primary (not language-difference-related) communication disorder. His difficulties are present across both languages in a pattern inconsistent with typical second-language acquisition or typical bilingual phonological variation, supporting true impairment rather than a dialectal or bilingual difference. His strong stimulability and positive response to cueing, along with his social motivation to communicate, are significant strengths that support a favorable prognosis with consistent intervention.

Diagnostic impressions: Phonological disorder ([[KEEP|ICD-10: F80.0]]); Mixed receptive-expressive language disorder ([[KEEP|ICD-10: F80.2]]).
