IMPRESSIONS

[[CHILD|Majo]] presents with a severe mixed receptive-expressive language disorder in her primary language (Spanish), as evidenced by standard scores on the [[KEEP|PLS-5 Spanish]] falling more than [[KEEP|2 standard deviations]] below the mean across both receptive and expressive domains (Total Language Score [[KEEP|66]], [[KEEP|1st percentile]], CI [[KEEP|61-71]]). Given that English exposure is minimal and that the family's stated goal is for [[CHILD|Majo]] to communicate effectively first in Spanish at home, her English screening scores are not interpreted as evidence of a distinct, separate disorder but rather reflect limited current exposure overlaid on an underlying primary language impairment. Co-occurring speech sound disorder is present, with articulation/phonology standard score of [[KEEP|72]] ([[KEEP|3rd percentile]]) on the [[KEEP|GFTA-3]] (Spanish administration), characterized by cluster reduction, final consonant deletion, and liquid simplification patterns consistent with a phonological process disorder rather than a purely articulatory/motor-based disorder. These findings are consistent with the diagnostic impression of a Language Disorder with accompanying Speech Sound Disorder (ICD-10: [[KEEP|F80.2]], Mixed Receptive-Expressive Language Disorder; ICD-10: [[KEEP|F80.0]], Phonological Disorder), warranting continued intervention per IFSP.

History of recurrent otitis media and PE tube placement is a relevant contributing factor to the speech sound disorder, though the persistence of errors well past the resolution of middle ear issues (confirmed otitis-free since early [[KEEP|2026]]) suggests the disorder is not solely attributable to fluctuating hearing history and warrants treatment independent of medical status. Family's bilingual environment should be supported and not viewed as causative or contributory to the disorder; rather, intervention should proceed bilingually, consistent with best practice for dual-language learners, honoring the home language of Spanish while supporting emerging English exposure through [[SIBLING|Daniela]], the library playgroup, and eventual preschool enrollment.

[[CHILD|Majo]]'s pragmatic communication style — primarily gesture- and pointing-based with limited spontaneous verbal initiation — combined with parent-reported frustration behaviors (crying, pulling at clothing) suggests communication breakdown is contributing to behavioral frustration at home, which should improve as expressive language skills develop through intervention.
