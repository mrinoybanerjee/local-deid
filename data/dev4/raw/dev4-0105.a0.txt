SPEECH SOUND PRODUCTION

On the [[KEEP|GFTA-3]], [[CHILD|Teo]] demonstrated the following error patterns in English single-word productions: final consonant deletion (for example, "ca" for "cat," target [[KEEP|/kaet/]]), fronting of velars (he produced "tat" for "cat," substituting [[KEEP|[t]]] for [[KEEP|/k/]]), and stopping of fricatives (he produced "pish" as "pit," substituting [[KEEP|[t]]] for [[KEEP|/s/]]). On the informal Spanish screening, similar patterns were noted, including simplification of consonant clusters (he produced "pato" for "plato," omitting the liquid) and occasional substitution of [[KEEP|[d]]] for the tapped [[KEEP|/ɾ/]] in words such as "pero," which he produced as "pedo" (phonetically [[KEEP|[ˈpe.do]]]).

Intelligibility was judged to be approximately [[KEEP|50%]] to unfamiliar listeners and [[KEEP|70-80%]] to familiar listeners, consistent with parent report. These error patterns occur in both Spanish and English and are not attributable to normal cross-linguistic transfer alone, suggesting a speech sound disorder affecting both languages rather than a difference related solely to bilingualism.

CLINICAL IMPRESSIONS / IMPRESIONES CLINICAS

Based on standardized testing, parent interview, language sampling, and clinical observation, [[CHILD|Mateo Alejandro Flores Jimenez]] presents with a mixed expressive-receptive language disorder affecting both Spanish and English, co-occurring with a speech sound disorder affecting both languages. ICD-10 diagnostic code [[KEEP|F80.2]] (mixed receptive-expressive language disorder) and [[KEEP|F80.0]] (phonological disorder) are applied. Billing for the combined evaluation session is coded under CPT [[KEEP|92523]] (evaluation of speech sound production with evaluation of language comprehension and expression).

The pattern of delay present in both of [[CHILD|Teo]]'s languages, rather than isolated to one, supports a diagnosis of true language and speech disorder rather than a language difference attributable solely to simultaneous bilingual acquisition or reduced input in either single language. The combination of conceptually scored vocabulary estimates closer to age level, alongside cross-linguistic expressive and phonological weaknesses, indicates that while bilingualism is not the cause of [[CHILD|Teo]]'s difficulties, intervention must address both of his languages to be effective and ecologically appropriate for his home environment.

[[CHILD|Teo]]'s strengths include strong social engagement, intact play skills, good comprehension of simple directions in both languages, and a highly supportive family who provides rich language input through extended family, church community, and library programming. Risk factors include recurrent early otitis media, which may have contributed to inconsistent auditory input during a key developmental window, and reduced intelligibility, which appears to be contributing to frustration and some reduction in initiating communication with unfamiliar listeners, including peers at his Early Head Start classroom.
