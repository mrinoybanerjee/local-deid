SPEECH SOUND PRODUCTION

In single-word and connected-speech sampling across both languages, [[CHILD|María José]] demonstrated the following error patterns: fronting of [[KEEP|/k/]] and [[KEEP|/g/]] (e.g., "[[KEEP|tapo]]" for "capo"), gliding of [[KEEP|/r/]] and the Spanish tap [[KEEP|/ɾ/]] (e.g., "[[KEEP|uata]]" for "rata"), and final consonant deletion in English multisyllabic words. Overall speech intelligibility was estimated at approximately [[KEEP|60-70%]] to unfamiliar listeners in connected speech, improving to [[KEEP|85%]] in structured single-word tasks with [[KEEP|min-mod cues]]. These error patterns were consistent across both Spanish and English, supporting a true phonological disorder rather than a dialectal or second-language difference, per bilingual scoring guidelines.

LANGUAGE SAMPLE AND PRAGMATICS

A 50-utterance spontaneous language sample was collected in Spanish and coded using SALT, including instances of [[KEEP|[EW]]] codes for word-level errors. Mean length of utterance was below age expectations, with frequent omission of grammatical morphemes (plural markers, verb conjugations) in both languages. [[CHILD|María José]] used primarily [[KEEP|2-3]] word utterances, with occasional [[KEEP|4]]-word combinations. She demonstrated appropriate eye contact, turn-taking, and joint attention during structured and semi-structured tasks, and responded to her name consistently. No concerns regarding social communication or pragmatic skills were noted by examiner or parent.

RECEPTIVE AND EXPRESSIVE VOCABULARY

Informal bilingual vocabulary screening suggested a combined (conceptual) vocabulary within low-average range when both languages were considered together, though each language in isolation fell below age expectations. This pattern is consistent with typical bilingual development but was judged, in combination with standardized scores and parent/teacher report, to reflect a true underlying language disorder rather than solely a bilingual language-learning difference.

IMPRESSIONS

Based on standardized testing, language sampling, parent and teacher report, and classroom observation, [[CHILD|María José]] presents with a Mixed Receptive-Expressive Language Disorder ([[KEEP|F80.2]]) affecting both Spanish and English, along with a co-occurring Speech Sound Disorder ([[KEEP|F80.0]]) characterized by phonological error patterns present in both languages. Findings are consistent across formal testing, informal sampling, and functional report from multiple sources (parent, teacher, examiner), supporting the diagnostic impression that these difficulties reflect a true disorder rather than typical second-language acquisition or dialectal variation.

Strengths noted include strong social engagement, appropriate pragmatic skills, intact hearing (with tubes in place), and a supportive, language-rich bilingual home environment. Areas of concern include reduced intelligibility, limited expressive vocabulary in both languages, morphosyntactic difficulties, and below-average receptive and expressive language scores.
