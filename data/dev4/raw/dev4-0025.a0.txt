María José Castillo Hernández, known to everyone in her family as Majo, was born on April 2, 2023, and currently lives with her parents at 214 Maple Creek Lane, Burlington, 27215. This periodic IFSP review was completed on October 2, 2026, in the family's home, with her mother, Lucía Hernández Mendoza, her father, Carlos Castillo Ruiz, her service coordinator, Daniela Pérez, with Piedmont CDSA, and this writer, Andrea Soto, M.S., CCC-SLP, a bilingual pediatric speech-language pathologist with Carolina Pediatric Therapy Associates. Majo's case is tracked under CDSA record PCD-48217 and Medicaid member number 739215608A. The family's previous full IFSP was completed on April 10, 2026, and the next annual review is scheduled for April 10, 2027.

Lucía and Carlos moved to Burlington in 2019 from La Ceiba Vieja, a small village in the department of Intibucá, Honduras, where Lucía's mother and most of her siblings still live. Lucía works the overnight shift at Cook's Poultry Processing, and Carlos is employed with Blue Ridge Construction LLC, so their schedules rotate frequently. Because of this, Majo's grandmother, who everyone calls Abuela Rosa, lives in the home and provides most of her daytime care, including bringing her to appointments when Lucía and Carlos cannot attend. Majo's older sister, Ximena Castillo Hernández, is 7 and in second grade at Hillcrest Elementary School, part of the Alamance-Burlington School System. Her teacher, Mrs. Ferris, has mentioned that Ximena sometimes translates for her parents at school pickup and during parent conferences, something the family specifically hopes Majo will not need to rely on once she reaches school age herself.

During the review, Lucía described her priorities en sus propias palabras: "Lo que más queremos es que Majo pueda decir lo que necesita sin frustrarse tanto. A veces grita o nos jala la mano porque no le entendemos, and that really breaks my heart, especially cuando estamos en la tienda o en la iglesia y la gente nos mira." Carlos added, "Queremos que ella esté lista para el kínder, that she can play with other kids and they understand her, y que no se sienta diferente de su hermana." Abuela Rosa, who spends the most hours with Majo, said simply, "Yo solo quiero entenderla mejor. A veces no sé si tiene hambre o si le duele algo, porque no me lo puede decir."

Rutinas diarias / Daily Routines: Majo wakes around 7:00 a.m. and eats breakfast with Abuela Rosa, usually avena or eggs, before Lucía or Carlos drops her at Little Sprouts Early Learning Center by 8:30 a.m. There she is in Ms. Lopez's room with eight other toddlers. Ms. Lopez reports that Majo engages well with routines and transitions but often watches rather than initiates during free play, and that she enjoys sitting next to her classmate, Noah, during snack time, though the two mostly play side by side rather than together. Abuela Rosa picks Majo up at 4:00 p.m., and the family typically eats dinner together around 6:30 p.m., often joined by Majo's cousin, Emiliano, a nine-year-old who lives two houses down and frequently comes over after school. Bath time is around 7:30 p.m., followed by songs in Spanish and a short bedtime book, with lights out close to 8:15 p.m. Lucía noted that Majo responds best to routines that are sung rather than spoken, and that she will attempt new words more readily during these musical moments than at other times of day.

The family has also received support from WIC and from Smart Start of Alamance County for childcare subsidy assistance. More recently, Lucía was referred to the Alianza de Recursos Comunitarios para Familias Hispanas del Condado de Alamance (ARCFHA) for help navigating housing paperwork and food assistance applications; since that referral, the family has worked primarily with ARCFHA, and Lucía reported that ARCFHA has been especially helpful in explaining documents en español that she previously had to ask neighbors to translate.

Formal assessment using the Preschool Language Scales, Fifth Edition (PLS-5) Spanish-English bilingual edition yielded an Auditory Comprehension standard score of 73 (4th percentile) and an Expressive Communication standard score of 68 (2nd percentile), with a 90% confidence interval of 65-77 on the expressive measure. Majo's chronological age at testing was 3;6, and her expressive language age equivalent fell at 20 months, within an estimated range of 18-22 months. During the oral motor and speech sample, she produced "mami" and "papi" consistently, approximated /pa/ for the target word "más" with moderate cues, and produced [pa] for "pan," omitting the final consonant. She was stimulable for /m/ and /p/ in isolation and in CV syllables with minimal cues, but final consonants in /p, m, n/ were consistently omitted or glottalized across both English and Spanish samples. Birth history is notable for a gestational age of 36 weeks and a birth weight of 5 lbs 4 oz, with Apgar scores of 8 and 9. Majo's pediatrician, Dr. Marcus Owens, at Alamance Regional Pediatrics, confirmed a passed newborn hearing screen, and a more recent audiological evaluation was within normal limits bilaterally, ruling out a hearing-based explanation for her delays. She also receives physical therapy weekly from Jenna Caldwell, PT, who is addressing mild gross motor delays related to low tone, and an occupational therapy evaluation has been requested to assess fine motor skills relevant to self-feeding and early writing readiness.

Resultados del IFSP / IFSP Outcomes:

Outcome 1: Majo will use two- to three-word combinations in Spanish and/or English to request desired items and activities across home and daycare settings, increasing from a baseline of 3 of 10 opportunities to 8 of 10 opportunities across three consecutive data collection sessions. Strategies include parent-implemented Hanen It Takes Two to Talk techniques during daily routines, modeling expansions of Majo's one-word utterances rather than correcting them, and bilingual visual supports introduced in Little Sprouts Early Learning Center by Ms. Lopez to support requesting during snack and play.

Outcome 2: Majo will reduce frustration-related behaviors, including hitting and pulling on a caregiver's hand, by instead using a picture exchange system (PECS) or a consistent gesture to request a break or indicate "more," as observed and logged by Abuela Rosa and Lucía at home across at least four of five consecutive days.

Outcome 3: Majo will improve intelligibility of final consonants in single words, targeting /p, m, n/ in final position, moving from 10% intelligibility to 50% intelligibility as measured by a 50-word probe administered in both English and Spanish, using a Cycles phonological intervention approach with weekly target rotation.

Outcome 4: Majo will increase joint attention and turn-taking with peers during structured play at daycare, moving from primarily observing peers to initiating at least two turn-taking exchanges per 15-minute play session, supported by Ms. Lopez and modeled through parallel play activities with Noah.

Speech-language services began June 15, 2026, under CPT code 92523 and ICD-10 code F80.2, delivered twice weekly for 30-minute sessions split between the family's home and Little Sprouts Early Learning Center. Physical therapy continues under a separate authorization with Jenna Caldwell, and the pending occupational therapy evaluation will be scheduled through Piedmont CDSA once approved. This periodic review, held October 2, 2026, confirmed that all four outcomes remain appropriate, with Outcome 1 showing the most measurable progress since April. The next full annual IFSP review is scheduled for April 10, 2027, and the team agreed to an additional six-month check-in around January 15, 2027, at the family's request, given their interest in closely monitoring Majo's expressive growth before she transitions out of Part C services.
