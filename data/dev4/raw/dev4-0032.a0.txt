ARTICULATION AND PHONOLOGY

On the [[KEEP|GFTA-3]], [[CHILD|Mateo]] demonstrated multiple phonological error patterns including final consonant deletion, stopping of fricatives, and gliding of liquids. He substituted [[KEEP|[w]]] for [[KEEP|/l/]] and [[KEEP|/r/]] (e.g., [[KEEP|"wion"]] for [[KEEP|"lion"]]), and deleted final consonants in words such as [[KEEP|"ca"]] for [[KEEP|"cat"]]. In structured probes, he produced the target [[KEEP|/k/]] correctly in [[KEEP|8/10]] trials with [[KEEP|minimal cues]] and the Spanish tap [[KEEP|/ɾ/]] in [[KEEP|3 of 5]] trials with [[KEEP|moderate cues]]. His intelligibility was judged at approximately [[KEEP|50%]] to unfamiliar listeners and [[KEEP|80%]] to familiar listeners, consistent with parent and teacher report. A transcription excerpt from the language sample included a word-finding error coded [[KEEP|[EW]]] when he paused mid-utterance searching for the word for "ball."

EXPRESSIVE AND RECEPTIVE LANGUAGE

[[CHILD|Mateo]]'s mean length of utterance in Spanish was [[KEEP|2.3]] morphemes, and in English was [[KEEP|1.8]] morphemes, both below age expectations ([[KEEP|2;6–3;0]] typical range for this age). He used primarily noun-verb combinations and omitted articles, plural markers, and verb tense markers in both languages. During the classroom observation, [[TEACHER|Ms. Flores]] noted that [[CHILD|Mateo]] used gesture and pointing with classmates [[PEER|Ethan]] and [[PEER|Zoe]] rather than verbal requests. At home, [[PARENT|Carlos Cruz]] reported that [[CHILD|Mateo]] currently uses a modified picture exchange system, having been introduced to [[KEEP|PECS]] during his IFSP services with [[PROVIDER|Brittany Shaw]]; the family would like to reduce reliance on this system as verbal language increases.

SOCIAL COMMUNICATION AND PRAGMATICS

[[CHILD|Mateo]] initiated joint attention appropriately and engaged in reciprocal play during the session, laughing and making eye contact when the examiner imitated his sounds. He showed some frustration-related behaviors, including throwing toys, when he could not make himself understood, consistent with reports from [[FAMILY|Luz]] and [[TEACHER|Ms. Flores]]. No concerns regarding restricted interests or repetitive behaviors were noted by caregivers or during observation, and [[PROVIDER|Dr. Ravi Patel]]'s records do not reflect an autism diagnosis.

ORAL-MOTOR AND HEARING SCREENING

Oral-motor structures and function were within normal limits for speech production. [[CHILD|Mateo]] passed a pure-tone hearing screening bilaterally at [[KEEP|20 dB HL]] across speech frequencies, administered with interpretation support.

CLINICAL IMPRESSIONS

[[CHILD|Mateo]] presents with a mixed receptive-expressive language disorder ([[KEEP|F80.2]]) affecting both Spanish and English, along with a moderate phonological disorder. His language delays are evident across both languages, ruling out typical second-language acquisition as the primary explanation for his difficulties. His reduced intelligibility, limited grammatical morphology, and frustration-related behaviors are consistent with a communication disorder requiring direct intervention. [[CHILD|Mateo]]'s strong joint attention, social engagement, and play skills are notable strengths that support a positive prognosis with consistent intervention and family involvement.

RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Individual speech-language therapy twice weekly, billed under [[KEEP|CPT 92507]], targeting expressive syntax, phonological patterns, and functional vocabulary in both languages.
2. Continued use of [[KEEP|PECS]] as a bridge strategy, fading supports as verbal output increases, consistent with prior IFSP goals.
3. Incorporation of [[KEEP|PROMPT]] tactile-kinesthetic cueing to support motor planning for speech sound production, particularly for liquids and final consonants.
4. A [[KEEP|Cycles]] phonological approach to target error patterns in blocks, alternating between English and Spanish target sounds.
5. Parent coaching using [[KEEP|Hanen]] "[[KEEP|It Takes Two to Talk]]" strategies to increase responsive communication at home, delivered bilingually with support from [[PROVIDER|Marisol Peña]] as needed.
6. Consideration of the [[KEEP|Kaufman]] Speech to Language Protocol if motor planning difficulties persist despite the above approaches.
7. Classroom consultation with [[TEACHER|Ms. Flores]] and [[TEACHER|Ms. Nguyen]] at [[DAYCARE|Tiny Acorns Learning Cottage]] to support carryover of vocabulary and requesting strategies.
8. Re-evaluation in [[KEEP|6 months]] to monitor progress and adjust the plan of care.
