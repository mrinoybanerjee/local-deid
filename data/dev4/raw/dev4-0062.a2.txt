ARTICULATION AND PHONOLOGY

Oral-motor examination revealed adequate structure and function of the oral mechanism, with mild reduced diadochokinetic rate noted. Connected speech intelligibility was estimated at approximately [[KEEP|40%]] for unfamiliar listeners and [[KEEP|65%]] for familiar listeners, including his parents and teachers.

Phonological process analysis identified consistent patterns of final consonant deletion (e.g., "[[KEEP|ca]]" for target "[[KEEP|cat]]"), cluster reduction (e.g., "[[KEEP|poon]]" for target "[[KEEP|spoon]]"), and fronting of velar sounds (e.g., substitution of [[KEEP|/t/]] for [[KEEP|/k/]], as in "[[KEEP|tootie]]" for target "[[KEEP|cookie]]"). Gliding of liquids was also noted, with [[KEEP|[w]]] substituted for [[KEEP|/ɾ/]] in Spanish productions, such as "[[KEEP|cawo]]" for target "[[KEEP|carro]]." These patterns were consistent across both English and Spanish samples, supporting a diagnosis of a primary speech sound disorder rather than a feature of typical bilingual phonological development.

During structured probes, [[CHILD|Teo]] produced target words with [[KEEP|8/10]] accuracy ([[KEEP|80%]]) when given moderate cueing ([[KEEP|mod cues]]) for single syllable CV and CVC words, but accuracy dropped to [[KEEP|3 of 10]] ([[KEEP|30%]]) for multisyllabic words without support. With maximal cueing ([[KEEP|max cues]]), accuracy on multisyllabic targets improved to [[KEEP|7/10]] ([[KEEP|70%]]).

LANGUAGE SAMPLE

A 50-utterance spontaneous language sample was collected in both English and Spanish during structured play. Mean length of utterance was [[KEEP|2.8]] morphemes in English and [[KEEP|3.1]] morphemes in Spanish, both below expected ranges for his age. [[CHILD|Teo]] relied heavily on gesture and single-word utterances to communicate needs, and was coded using SALT conventions where overgeneralized word forms were marked [[KEEP|[EW]]]. He demonstrated emerging use of early semantic relations (agent + action, e.g., "[[CHILD|Teo]] go") but limited use of grammatical morphemes, including plural -s, past tense -ed, and copula "is/está."

IMPRESSIONS

Based on standardized testing, parent and teacher report, and clinical observation, [[CHILD|Teo]] presents with a mixed receptive-expressive language disorder and a moderate speech sound disorder affecting intelligibility in both English and Spanish. His difficulties are consistent across both languages, confirming a primary disorder rather than second-language acquisition variability. This is consistent with a diagnosis of [[KEEP|F80.2]] (Mixed Receptive-Expressive Language Disorder). His reduced intelligibility, phonological process patterns, and delayed grammatical development are significantly impacting his functional communication at home, at [[DAYCARE|Little Sprouts Daycare]], and in social interactions with peers such as [[PEER|Ethan]] and [[PEER|Ava]].

[[CHILD|Teo]] qualifies for speech-language services under North Carolina's Infant-Toddler Program eligibility criteria based on a delay of 25% or greater in two developmental domains. His current OT services with [[PROVIDER|Megan Price]] should continue concurrently, as sensory and fine motor factors may be contributing to feeding and oral-motor coordination patterns relevant to speech production.
