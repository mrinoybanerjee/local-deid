**LANGUAGE SAMPLE AND INFORMAL OBSERVATIONS**

A [[KEEP|50-utterance]] conversational language sample was collected in each language during play with toy animals and a farm set. [[CHILD|Teo]]'s mean length of utterance in Spanish was [[KEEP|2.3 morphemes]] and in English was [[KEEP|2.1 morphemes]], both below expectations for his age. Utterances were coded with SALT conventions; omissions of grammatical morphemes were marked with bound-morpheme codes such as [[KEEP|[EW]]] for early word forms. [[CHILD|Teo]] relied heavily on nouns and a limited set of early verbs ("[[KEEP|want]]," "[[KEEP|go]]," "[[KEEP|no]]"), with frequent omission of articles, copulas, and subject-verb agreement markers in both languages. He combined two to three words at most (e.g., "[[CHILD|Teo]] quiere agua," "more juice please").

During a structured narrative retell task using a wordless picture book, [[CHILD|Teo]] produced primarily single-word labels and required [[KEEP|maximal cues]] to sequence events, correctly ordering only [[KEEP|2 of 5]] story elements. In contrast, during unstructured play he initiated requests and protests consistently, and he used gestures and eye contact effectively to repair communication breakdowns, suggesting relatively intact pragmatic skills despite limited structural language.

Intelligibility was judged to be approximately [[KEEP|50%]] to an unfamiliar listener in connected speech and approximately [[KEEP|75%]] to a familiar listener, consistent with parent and teacher report.

**CLINICAL IMPRESSIONS**

Based on standardized and informal assessment, parent and teacher report, and clinical observation, [[CHILD|Teo]] presents with a mixed receptive-expressive language delay affecting both Spanish and English, consistent with a true bilingual language disorder rather than typical second-language acquisition. Scores fall consistently below average across both standardized languages and both receptive and expressive domains, and conceptual scoring, while higher, remains within the delayed range. [[CHILD|Teo]] also presents with a moderate speech sound disorder affecting both languages, characterized by fronting, gliding, and inconsistent production of the Spanish tap [[KEEP|/ɾ/]], consistent with a phonological pattern rather than an isolated articulation error.

[[CHILD|Teo]]'s strengths include relatively intact pragmatic and social communication skills, strong nonverbal problem-solving during play, and a supportive, language-rich bilingual home and daycare environment. His history of recurrent otitis media during a developmentally sensitive period for speech-sound learning is a likely contributing factor, though current hearing is within normal limits.

These findings are consistent with the concerns raised by [[PARENT|Lucía Cruz Ramírez]] and [[PARENT|Jesús Hernández Flores]], by Ms. [[TEACHER|Ana López]] at [[DAYCARE|Little Learners Daycare]], and by [[PROVIDER|Dr. Anjali Patel]], and support continued eligibility for IFSP services as well as the addition of direct speech-language therapy.
