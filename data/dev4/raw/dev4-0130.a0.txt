The following standardized and informal measures were administered: [[KEEP|Bilingual English-Spanish Assessment (BESA)]] – Morphosyntax, Semantics, and Phonology subtests; [[KEEP|Goldman-Fristoe Test of Articulation-3 (GFTA-3)]]; [[KEEP|Preschool Language Scales, Fifth Edition (PLS-5), Spanish Edition]] – Auditory Comprehension and Expressive Communication subtests; an English-language spontaneous speech-language sample; a Spanish-language spontaneous speech-language sample obtained with parental and interpreter support; an oral mechanism examination; and a hearing screening (pure-tone, bilateral, pass/refer).

TEST RESULTS

Oral Mechanism Examination: Structures and function of the lips, tongue, hard and soft palate, and jaw were within normal limits for speech production. No signs of structural abnormality, overt or submucous cleft, or dysarthria were observed. Diadochokinetic rates were judged to be mildly reduced for age but adequate to support intelligible connected speech.

Hearing Screening: [[CHILD|Teo]] passed a bilateral pure-tone hearing screening at [[KEEP|20 dB HL]] across [[KEEP|500, 1000, 2000, and 4000 Hz]], conducted at the start of the first evaluation session, consistent with [[PARENT|Lucía]]'s report of no current hearing concerns.

Articulation and Phonology: On the [[KEEP|GFTA-3]], [[CHILD|Teo]] obtained a raw score corresponding to a Standard Score of [[KEEP|71]] (95% CI: [[KEEP|65-77]]), placing him at the [[KEEP|3rd percentile]] for his age. Error patterns observed included gliding of liquids (for example, "wion" for "lion"), final consonant deletion, and cluster reduction, consistent across both English and Spanish productions, indicating a phonological disorder rather than a dialectal or bilingual-related difference, since these patterns occurred in both of his languages and are not accounted for by typical Spanish-influenced English or typical second-language acquisition patterns. Specific phoneme errors included inconsistent production of /s/ clusters, substitution of [w] for /ɾ/ in Spanish words (for example, "[[KEEP|pewo]]" for "perro"), and omission of final /s/ in both languages. [[CHILD|Teo]] was stimulable for /k/ and /g/ in isolation and in syllables with [[KEEP|moderate cues]], but not in connected speech.

Spontaneous language samples in both English and Spanish supported the standardized findings. In a 15-minute Spanish sample, [[CHILD|Teo]]'s Mean Length of Utterance was [[KEEP|2.8 morphemes]], below expectations for his age. He demonstrated omission of grammatical morphemes, including articles and verb conjugation markers, and relied heavily on two-word combinations (for example, "[[KEEP|más agua]]," "[[KEEP|no quiero]]"). In the English sample, [[CHILD|Teo]] produced primarily single words and occasional two-word combinations (for example, "[[KEEP|more juice]]," "[[KEEP|go home]]"), with minimal use of grammatical morphemes such as plural -s, present progressive -ing, and third-person singular -s, which is lower than would be expected even for a child in the early stages of English acquisition relative to his length of exposure.

Narrative and Pragmatic Skills: [[CHILD|Teo]] demonstrated emerging but limited narrative skills in both languages, typically labeling pictures rather than producing connected narratives. When asked to retell a simple three-event story in Spanish with picture support, he produced one to two relevant utterances with [[KEEP|moderate cues]] and prompting, falling short of the expected [[KEEP|3 of 5]] story grammar elements for his age. Pragmatic skills, including eye contact, turn-taking, and joint attention, were judged to be age-appropriate in both structured and unstructured contexts.

IMPRESSIONS

Based on the results of this comprehensive bilingual evaluation, [[CHILD|Teo]] presents with a speech sound disorder (phonological disorder) and a language disorder affecting both his receptive and expressive language skills, evident across both of his languages, Spanish and English. His difficulties cannot be attributed solely to second-language acquisition, limited English exposure, or dialectal variation, given that error patterns, below-average standard scores, and reduced morpheme production were documented consistently in both Spanish, his dominant and first-exposed language, and English. This cross-linguistic pattern, combined with his history of prematurity, recurrent otitis media, and early expressive language delay, is consistent with a primary speech-language impairment.

[[CHILD|Teo]]'s reduced speech intelligibility, estimated at approximately [[KEEP|40%]] with unfamiliar listeners, is significantly impacting his ability to participate in classroom instruction and peer interaction, as reflected in reports from [[TEACHER|Ms. Whitfield]] regarding his reliance on peer interpretation. His scores across formal measures in both languages fall within the clinically significant range (below the [[KEEP|10th percentile]]), supporting eligibility for speech-language intervention services under North Carolina's Developmental Disabilities criteria and corresponding educational eligibility categories.
