Articulation and Phonology

Conversational speech samples in both languages revealed a moderate phonological disorder characterized by final consonant deletion, cluster reduction, and gliding of liquids. In English, [[CHILD|Mateo]] produced "[[KEEP|wabbit]]" for the target word "rabbit," substituting [[KEEP|[w]]] for [[KEEP|/r/]]. He also produced "[[KEEP|poo]]" for "spoon," demonstrating cluster reduction and final consonant deletion. In Spanish, [[CHILD|Mateo]] demonstrated age-appropriate use of the trilled [[KEEP|/r/]] in words such as "[[KEEP|perro]]," though he substituted [[KEEP|/ɾ/]] with [[KEEP|[l]]] in "[[KEEP|flor]]," producing "[[KEEP|flol]]." Of note, several English phonological patterns observed (gliding, cluster reduction) are consistent with typical cross-linguistic transfer patterns in Spanish-English bilingual development and were weighted accordingly rather than scored as purely disordered.

Intelligibility in connected speech was estimated at [[KEEP|60%]] to familiar listeners and [[KEEP|30%]] to unfamiliar listeners across both languages, consistent with parent report. [[CHILD|Mateo]] demonstrated a phonetic inventory inclusive of [[KEEP|/p, b, m, n, t, d, k, g, f, s, j/]] across both languages, with notable absence of [[KEEP|/r/]], [[KEEP|/ɾ/]] (except in select words), [[KEEP|/ʃ/]], and consonant clusters.

During a 20-minute play-based language sample conducted at [[TIME|10:15 AM]] on [[DATE|March 3, 2025]], [[CHILD|Mateo]] produced primarily 2-3 word utterances in both languages, with mean length of utterance (MLU) calculated at [[KEEP|2.1]] morphemes in English and [[KEEP|2.4]] morphemes in Spanish. CLINICAL IMPRESSIONS

Based on standardized testing, parent and teacher report, and clinical observation, [[CHILD|Mateo]] presents with a moderate expressive and receptive language delay affecting both Spanish and English, alongside a moderate phonological disorder characterized by cluster reduction, final consonant deletion, and gliding. Importantly, because [[CHILD|Mateo]] demonstrates delays across both of his languages relative to bilingual normative data, his profile is consistent with a true language disorder rather than a language difference attributable solely to bilingual exposure or second-language acquisition. His stronger performance in Spanish relative to English is consistent with his primary language exposure at home and should be considered when interpreting overall severity.

[[CHILD|Mateo]]'s reduced intelligibility across both languages is contributing to communicative frustration, as evidenced by behavioral episodes reported by both parents and his teacher, Ms. [[TEACHER|Hollis]]. His pragmatic and play skills remain a relative strength and support a positive prognosis for intervention. Given his history of recurrent otitis media, continued coordination with audiology and his ENT, Dr. [[PROVIDER|Alvarado]], is recommended to monitor hearing status, as fluctuating conductive hearing loss may have contributed to and may continue to impact speech sound development.

[[CHILD|Mateo]] meets eligibility criteria for speech-language intervention services under [[AGENCY|Cumberland County CDSA]] guidelines based on standard scores more than 1.5 standard deviations below the mean in both receptive and expressive domains across both languages.
