AUDITORY COMPREHENSION

On the [[KEEP|PLS-5 Spanish-English]] Auditory Comprehension subtest, [[CHILD|Majo]] demonstrated emerging understanding of basic spatial concepts, following two-step directions, and answering simple wh-questions, but had notable difficulty with comprehension of possessive and plural morphology in both languages. She correctly followed [[KEEP|6 of 10]] two-step directions and identified [[KEEP|3 of 5]] basic prepositions ("en," "debajo," "arriba") when presented in Spanish, compared to [[KEEP|4 of 5]] in English, suggesting relative strength in English for this specific skill, likely reflecting recent classroom exposure.

EXPRESSIVE LANGUAGE AND VOCABULARY

Expressively, [[CHILD|Majo]] used primarily three- to four-word utterances with frequent omission of grammatical morphemes in both languages, such as articles and verb tense markers. On the [[KEEP|BESA]] Morphosyntax subtest, she scored in the below-average range, with particular difficulty producing past-tense verb forms and subject-verb agreement in Spanish. During the language sample, she was observed to code-switch frequently within utterances, which is a typical and expected feature of bilingual language development and was not counted as an error.

Semantic skills were relatively stronger, with vocabulary use judged age-appropriate for everyday household and school-related topics in Spanish, though more limited in English, consistent with her shorter exposure to that language.

SPEECH SOUND PRODUCTION

Oral mechanism examination revealed structures and function within normal limits for speech, with adequate labial, lingual, and velopharyngeal movement. Diadochokinetic rates were judged age-appropriate.

On the [[KEEP|GFTA-3]], [[CHILD|Majo]] demonstrated a standard score of [[KEEP|73]] ([[KEEP|4th percentile]]), indicating moderate articulation delay relative to same-age peers. Error patterns included consistent substitution of [[KEEP|/t/]] for [[KEEP|/k/]] in word-initial position (e.g., target "[[KEEP|casa]]" produced as "[[KEEP|tasa]]"), gliding of [[KEEP|/r/]] to [[KEEP|[w]]] (e.g., target "[[KEEP|perro]]" produced as "[[KEEP|pewo]]"), and stopping of [[KEEP|/s/]] in final position. She was stimulable for [[KEEP|/k/]] with [[KEEP|moderate cues]] in isolation and in syllables, and for [[KEEP|/s/]] with [[KEEP|maximal cues]].

During the spontaneous language sample, [[CHILD|Majo]] was heard to say "[[KEEP|Tanela, ven aqui]]" when calling her dog, substituting [[KEEP|/t/]] for [[KEEP|/k/]] in the pet's name; this was coded as a [[KEEP|/k/]]-for-[[KEEP|/t/]] substitution error pattern rather than as a change to the pet's identity. She also produced "[[KEEP|me gusta pan]]" when attempting to request "[[KEEP|pez]]" (fish) at the classroom aquarium, which appeared to reflect a lexical rather than articulatory error and was noted for further monitoring.

IMPRESSIONS

Based on parent report, teacher report, classroom observation, and standardized and informal assessment results, [[CHILD|Maria Jose Hernandez Castillo]] presents with a bilingual language disorder affecting both receptive and expressive language in Spanish and English, alongside a moderate speech sound disorder. Her language difficulties are present across both of her languages and are not attributable solely to typical second-language acquisition or dialectal variation, supporting a true language disorder rather than a language difference. Her articulation errors, including substitution and gliding patterns, are contributing significantly to reduced intelligibility, particularly with unfamiliar communication partners such as classmates at [[SCHOOL|Riverbend Elementary School]].

These combined language and speech sound difficulties are likely to impact [[CHILD|Majo]]'s academic readiness for kindergarten, her social interactions with peers such as [[PEER|Teo]], and her participation in classroom activities under Ms. [[TEACHER|Patterson]]'s instruction.

1. Bilingual developmental language disorder, expressive and receptive, Spanish and English
