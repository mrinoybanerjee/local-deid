5.2 Speech Sound Production

In connected speech sampled across both languages, [[CHILD|Mateo]] demonstrated a moderate phonological disorder affecting intelligibility. He substituted [[KEEP|/t/]] for [[KEEP|/k/]] (e.g., producing "[[KEEP|tasa]]" for the target "[[KEEP|casa]]") and [[KEEP|/d/]] for [[KEEP|/g/]] (e.g., "[[KEEP|dato]]" for "[[KEEP|gato]]"), consistent with fronting that is typically resolved by [[KEEP|3;0]] in monolingual Spanish-speaking children. He also exhibited final consonant deletion in English (e.g., "[[KEEP|ca]]" for "[[KEEP|cat]]") and cluster reduction (e.g., "[[KEEP|top]]" for "[[KEEP|stop]]"). Stimulability testing showed [[CHILD|Mateo]] could produce [[KEEP|/k/]] and [[KEEP|/g/]] in isolation with [[KEEP|mod cues]], and in syllables with [[KEEP|max cues]]. Overall intelligibility was estimated at [[KEEP|40%]] to unfamiliar listeners in connected speech, and [[KEEP|70%]] to familiar listeners (mother, grandmother).

5.3 Language

Receptively, [[CHILD|Mateo]] followed two-step directions in Spanish [[KEEP|8 of 10]] trials but only [[KEEP|3 of 10]] trials in English. He identified [[KEEP|9/10]] common objects by function in Spanish compared to [[KEEP|5/10]] in English. Expressively, his Spanish mean length of utterance (MLU) was calculated at [[KEEP|3.2 morphemes]], within the low average range for his age; his English MLU was [[KEEP|1.8 morphemes]], below expectations even accounting for limited exposure. He used present-tense verb forms inconsistently in Spanish, omitting the required subject-verb agreement marker in "[[KEEP|él come]]" produced as "[[KEEP|él comer]]," and relied heavily on gesture and single-word utterances in English.

5.4 Bilingual Considerations

Error patterns were compared against typical bilingual Spanish-English development to differentiate language difference from language disorder. [[CHILD|Mateo]]'s difficulties — reduced MLU, limited morphosyntax, and a phonological disorder — were present across both of his languages, which supports a true language disorder rather than a difference attributable solely to dual-language exposure. His stronger performance in Spanish is consistent with Spanish being the dominant input language at home.

6. IMPRESIONES / CLINICAL IMPRESSIONS
