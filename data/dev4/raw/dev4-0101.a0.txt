7.3 Articulation

On the [[KEEP|GFTA-3]] (English), [[CHILD|Mateo]] achieved a standard score of [[KEEP|71]] ([[KEEP|3rd percentile]]), with error patterns including final consonant deletion, fronting of velars (/k/ and /g/ produced as [t] and [d]), and gliding of liquids (/r/ and /l/ produced as [w]). On the [[KEEP|CPAC-S]] (Spanish), similar patterns emerged, including simplification of the trilled /r/ to [[KEEP|[d]]] and frequent cluster reduction (e.g., "[[KEEP|[pato]]]" for "[[KEEP|plato]]").

Target word "[[KEEP|carro]]" was produced as "[[KEEP|[kaːo]]]" with [[KEEP|moderate cues]]; target word "cat" was produced by [[CHILD|Mateo]] as "[[KEEP|tat]]." Speech intelligibility was rated as poor to familiar listeners (approximately [[KEEP|40%]] intelligible in connected speech) and very poor to unfamiliar listeners.

7.4 Language Sample Analysis

A 50-utterance bilingual language sample was collected during structured play with blocks and a toy farm set. Mean length of utterance (MLU) in morphemes was [[KEEP|1.8]] across both languages combined, significantly below the expected range of [[KEEP|3.0-4.0]] for his age. [[CHILD|Mateo]] used primarily single words and occasional two-word combinations ("[[KEEP|más agua]]," "[[KEEP|no quiero]]," "want ball"). Code-switching within single utterances was noted (e.g., "quiero [[KEEP|more]]"), consistent with typical bilingual development and not considered evidence of disorder in itself.

SALT coding identified frequent use of nonverbal communication, including pointing [[KEEP|[NV]]] and eye gaze shifts, used in [[KEEP|[EW]]] (end of word/utterance abandonment) patterns when [[CHILD|Mateo]] appeared to lose a word mid-attempt.

8. CLINICAL IMPRESSIONS

Based on standardized testing, parent report, language sampling, and clinical observation, [[CHILD|Mateo]] presents with a Developmental Language Disorder affecting both Spanish and English, with associated Speech Sound Disorder characterized by multiple phonological process errors (final consonant deletion, velar fronting, liquid gliding, cluster reduction) present across both languages. Because deficits are present in both of [[CHILD|Mateo]]'s languages relative to same-age bilingual peers, and given a positive family history, these findings are consistent with a primary language disorder rather than a difference attributable solely to bilingual language acquisition or reduced exposure.

Relevant ICD-10 codes: [[KEEP|F80.2]] (Mixed Receptive-Expressive Language Disorder), [[KEEP|F80.0]] (Phonological Disorder/Speech Sound Disorder), [[KEEP|H65.9]] (history of otitis media, resolved). Relevant CPT codes for this evaluation: [[KEEP|92523]] (evaluation of speech sound production with evaluation of language comprehension and expression), [[KEEP|92521]] (evaluation of speech fluency, not billed), modifier [[KEEP|-59]] applied where applicable per payer guidelines.

9. RECOMMENDATIONS

9.1 [[CHILD|Mateo]] is recommended for ongoing bilingual speech-language therapy, individual sessions, [[KEEP|2]] times per week, [[KEEP|45]] minutes per session, with services delivered in both Spanish and English per family preference and IFSP team decision.

9.2 Therapy should incorporate a combination of evidence-based approaches, including:
- [[KEEP|Hanen]] "It Takes Two to Talk" strategies for caregiver coaching, to support mother [[PARENT|Rosa Idalia Hernández Molina]] and grandmother [[FAMILY|Juana Molina de Hernández]] in using facilitative language techniques at home in Spanish
- [[KEEP|PROMPT]] (Prompts for Restructuring Oral Muscular Phonetic Targets) to address motor-based speech sound errors across both languages
- [[KEEP|Cycles]] phonological approach targeting the identified error patterns (final consonant deletion, velar fronting, gliding, cluster reduction) in a bilingual cycling format alternating Spanish and English targets
- Visual supports modeled on [[KEEP|PECS]] principles to reduce communicative frustration, particularly during transitions and requesting routines at [[AGENCY|Early Head Start]]
- [[KEEP|Kaufman]] Speech to Language Protocol techniques as a supplemental approach for shaping approximations toward target words
