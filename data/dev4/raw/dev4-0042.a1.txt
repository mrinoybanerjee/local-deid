IMPRESSIONS

Based on standardized testing, informal measures, language sampling, and parent and teacher report, [[CHILD|Majo]] presents with a moderate-to-severe expressive and receptive language delay affecting both English and Spanish, consistent with a bilingual presentation of Developmental Language Disorder rather than a language difference attributable solely to dual-language exposure. Her language delay is present across both languages, which supports a true disorder rather than typical second-language acquisition patterns. [[CHILD|Majo]] also demonstrates a moderate speech sound disorder affecting both languages, with specific difficulty producing /ɾ/ and medial /s/ in Spanish and final consonants and multisyllabic words in English. Given her history of recurrent otitis media and tympanostomy tube placement, a hearing re-screening is also recommended to rule out any residual conductive component contributing to her speech and language profile.

[[CHILD|Majo]]'s social communication skills, play skills, and attention appear to be relative strengths and are not of clinical concern at this time. Her rich bilingual home environment, including frequent interaction with her grandmother [[FAMILY|Lupita]], her cousin [[FAMILY|Juanito]], and her classmate [[PEER|Teo]], appears to provide strong relational and motivational support for communication, and should be leveraged as a resource in treatment planning rather than viewed as a barrier.

RECOMMENDATIONS

1. [[CHILD|Majo]] continues to qualify for speech-language intervention services through [[AGENCY|Briar County CDSA]] under her existing IFSP. It is recommended that direct therapy frequency increase to two 45-minute sessions per week, to be provided bilingually given [[CHILD|Majo]]'s dual-language home environment.

2. Therapy should target expressive vocabulary growth, use of grammatical morphemes (plurals, past tense, possessives) in both English and Spanish, and increased MLU through structured play-based activities and modeling.

3. Speech sound intervention should target /ɾ/ and medial /s/ in Spanish and final consonants and multisyllabic word shapes in English, using a cycles-based approach ([[KEEP|Cycles]]) given the number of sound classes affected.

4. A hearing re-screening or full audiological evaluation is recommended given [[CHILD|Majo]]'s history of recurrent otitis media and tube placement, to rule out any residual hearing component.

5. Continued coordination with Mrs. [[TEACHER|Debbie Lopez]] and the team at [[SCHOOL|Lindale Elementary School]] is recommended to support classroom language use and to monitor progress toward Pre-K readiness goals.

6. Home carryover strategies will be provided in both English and Spanish for [[PARENT|Lucia Ortiz Ramirez]], [[PARENT|Carlos Hernandez Flores]], and [[FAMILY|Lupita]], given their roles as [[CHILD|Majo]]'s primary caregivers and communication partners.

7. Re-evaluation is recommended in approximately six months, or sooner if significant progress is noted, to update [[CHILD|Majo]]'s IFSP goals in coordination with [[PROVIDER|Monica Reyes]] at [[AGENCY|Briar County CDSA]].
