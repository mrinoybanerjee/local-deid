RESULTS: SPEECH SOUND PRODUCTION

On the [[KEEP|GFTA-3]], [[CHILD|Mateo]] obtained a standard score of [[KEEP|71]] ([[KEEP|65-77]], 95% CI), [[KEEP|3rd]] percentile, consistent with a moderate speech sound disorder relative to monolingual English norms. Analysis of the sounds affected in both languages suggested patterns shared across English and Spanish phonological systems rather than isolated to one language, supporting a true speech sound disorder rather than typical cross-linguistic transfer alone.

Phonological process analysis revealed consistent final consonant deletion (for example, target "cat" produced as [[KEEP|"ca"]]), stopping of fricatives (target "sun" produced as [[KEEP|"tun"]]), and gliding of liquids, with /r/ produced as [w] and /l/ produced as [j] in word-initial position. In Spanish, the tap [[KEEP|/ɾ/]] was consistently produced as [[KEEP|[d]]] (target "pero" produced as [[KEEP|"pedo"]], noted with the family's acknowledgment and good humor during testing).

Stimulability testing showed that [[CHILD|Mateo]] could produce [[KEEP|/k/]] correctly in isolation and in [[KEEP|8/10]] ([[KEEP|80%]]) of trials with mod cues, and could approximate [[KEEP|/s/]] in [[KEEP|3 of 5]] trials ([[KEEP|60%]]) with max cues. He was not stimulable for /r/ in either language even with max cues. Intelligibility in connected speech was estimated at approximately [[KEEP|40%]] to unfamiliar listeners and [[KEEP|65%]] to familiar listeners, based on parent report and examiner judgment during the language sample.

An oral peripheral examination revealed structures and function within normal limits, including adequate lip, tongue, and jaw range of motion and strength for speech purposes, symmetrical facial musculature, and no evidence of tongue-tie, cleft, or other structural anomaly. Diadochokinetic rates were judged age-appropriate for gross oral motor sequencing tasks.

CLINICAL IMPRESSIONS

Results of this evaluation are consistent with a moderate to severe mixed receptive-expressive language disorder, ICD-10 code [[KEEP|F80.2]], affecting both of [[CHILD|Mateo]]'s languages, Spanish and English, and therefore not attributable to typical bilingual acquisition alone. [[CHILD|Mateo]] also presents with a moderate speech sound disorder, ICD-10 code [[KEEP|F80.0]], characterized by phonological processes and distortions affecting intelligibility across both languages.

The pattern of deficits present in both of [[CHILD|Mateo]]'s languages, together with the history of slow early vocabulary growth, limited progress during a prior episode of Part C intervention, and reduced intelligibility noted by multiple independent observers including his teacher and his mother, supports a primary language and speech sound disorder rather than a language difference secondary to bilingual exposure or an isolated articulation issue. The recurrent history of otitis media with effusion likely contributed to early delays, though current hearing is within normal limits and does not fully account for the persistence and severity of current deficits.

Given reported behavioral frustration, limited sustained attention, and the family history noted above, continued monitoring for broader developmental concerns, including possible referral for an autism-specific evaluation if social communication concerns increase, is recommended, though no diagnostic indicators of autism spectrum disorder were observed during this limited sample.

RECOMMENDATIONS

1. [[CHILD|Mateo]] qualifies for and should receive individual speech-language therapy services, recommended at a frequency of two sessions per week, 30 minutes each, targeting expressive vocabulary, grammatical morphology, and speech sound production in both Spanish and English. CPT code [[KEEP|92523]] was used for the combined speech sound and language evaluation billed under this report; individual treatment sessions should be billed under CPT code [[KEEP|92507]].

2. Therapy should be delivered using a bilingual service delivery model whenever possible, incorporating both Spanish and English targets rather than English only, given the family's stated goal of maintaining Spanish proficiency and the evidence of delay present in both languages.

3. A structured vocabulary and early grammar approach, such as [[KEEP|Hanen]] "It Takes Two to Talk" strategies, is recommended for caregiver coaching, alongside direct child-focused articulation and phonology intervention using a [[KEEP|Cycles]] approach to target stimulable sounds and phonological patterns identified above.

4. Given limited success of gesture-only communication at home and school, introduction of a structured core vocabulary visual support system, informed by [[KEEP|PECS]] principles, is recommended to reduce frustration and support functional requesting while verbal skills develop; this is not recommended as a replacement for verbal language goals.

5. [[CHILD|Mateo]]'s family should request a re-referral to [[SCHOOL|Durham Public Schools]] for reconsideration of exceptional children's eligibility given the results of this independent evaluation, which document deficits in both languages beyond what was identified in the prior school-based evaluation.

6. A repeat audiological evaluation is recommended in six months given the extensive history of otitis media, even though the most recent screening was within normal limits, to monitor for recurrence.

7. Continued coaching for [[PARENT|Lucia Hernandez-Soto]] and [[PARENT|Javier Hernandez-Soto]] on strategies to expand [[CHILD|Mateo]]'s utterances at home in Spanish is recommended, including modeling two- and three-word combinations and reducing reliance on yes/no questions in favor of open-ended prompts; [[FAMILY|Rosa del Rio]] should be included in this coaching given her significant caregiving role.

8. Progress should be reassessed in approximately six months using comparable measures, including repeat administration of relevant [[KEEP|PLS-5]] subtests, to monitor rate of growth and continued need for services.

This evaluation was conducted in English and Spanish by a bilingual, bicultural speech-language pathologist familiar with typical bilingual language development patterns in order to minimize the risk of over- or under-identification of a true disorder.
