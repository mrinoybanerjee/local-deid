IFSP OUTCOMES AND STRATEGIES / RESULTADOS Y ESTRATEGIAS

Outcome 1: [[CHILD|Chuy]] will use two-word combinations to request desired items and actions during daily routines, increasing from his current single-word level to at least [[KEEP|10 different two-word combinations]] within six months.
Strategies: Model expansion in both languages ("[[KEEP|más agua]]," "[[KEEP|up please]]") during meals and play; [[PARENT|Lupita]] will pause and wait after modeling to give [[CHILD|Chuy]] a turn; use visual supports (first/then picture cards) to pair words with gestures; family to practice during the midday park routine and bedtime books.

Outcome 2: [[CHILD|Chuy]] will replace hitting/grabbing with an acceptable communicative alternative (word, sign, or gesture for "mine" or "my turn") in at least [[KEEP|8 of 10]] observed opportunities with peers and his brother.
Strategies: Teach a simple sign for "mine" alongside the word "[[KEEP|mío]]"; [[PARENT|José Luis Martínez Cruz]] and [[PARENT|Guadalupe Flores Ramírez]] will narrate turn-taking during block play with [[SIBLING|Juan Martínez Flores Jr.]]; provider to model this approach using principles drawn from [[KEEP|Hanen]]'s "It Takes Two to Talk" during the next two home visits.

Outcome 3: The family will understand typical bilingual language milestones and distinguish them from [[CHILD|Chuy]]'s identified delay, so they can advocate for continued services even when extended family (such as [[FAMILY|Rosa Elena Cruz Villalobos]]) suggests waiting.
Strategies: Service coordinator [[PROVIDER|Carla Jiménez]] will share bilingual developmental milestone handouts in Spanish; SLP [[PROVIDER|Daniela Ortiz]] will review total-vocabulary versus single-language-vocabulary counting with the family, since [[CHILD|Chuy]]'s combined vocabulary of [[KEEP|25 words]] is more informative than either language alone; discuss at next review how to respond to well-meaning family pressure to "esperar."

Outcome 4: [[CHILD|Chuy]] will increase his repertoire of consonant sounds in word-initial position, specifically /p/, /m/, /b/, and /t/, achieving [[KEEP|80%]] accuracy in structured play across [[KEEP|3 consecutive sessions]].
Strategies: Use [[KEEP|PROMPT]]-informed tactile cueing for bilabial sounds; practice target words "[[KEEP|papá]]," "[[KEEP|más]]," "[[KEEP|bebé]]," and "[[KEEP|toto]]" (a family word for shoes) during structured play; direct SLP sessions twice weekly, [[KEEP|30 minutes]] each, co-treated bilingually.
