Articulation and Phonology

[[CHILD|Mateo]] demonstrated the following sound errors on the GFTA-3 and during spontaneous speech sampling: fronting of velars (/k/ and /g/ produced as /t/ and /d/, e.g., "[[KEEP|tat]]" for target "[[KEEP|cat]]"), final consonant deletion (e.g., "[[KEEP|ba]]" for target "[[KEEP|ball]]"), and liquid gliding, with /l/ and /ɾ/ produced as [w] (e.g., "[[KEEP|wuego]]" for target "[[KEEP|luego]]"). His speech intelligibility was judged to be approximately [[KEEP|40%]] intelligible to unfamiliar listeners in connected speech, improving to roughly [[KEEP|60%]] with context and gesture support. With moderate cues, [[CHILD|Mateo]] was able to produce the target sounds in isolation with [[KEEP|8/10]] accuracy for /k/ and [[KEEP|7/10]] for /g/, suggesting stimulability and good prognosis with intervention.

IMPRESSIONS

Based on comprehensive assessment across both of [[CHILD|Mateo]]'s languages, results are consistent with a diagnosis of Developmental Language Disorder (ICD-10 [[KEEP|F80.9]]) affecting both receptive and expressive language, with co-occurring Speech Sound Disorder (ICD-10 [[KEEP|F80.0]]). Deficits are present across both English and Spanish, which rules out typical bilingual second-language acquisition patterns or language difference as the sole explanation for his profile; a true disorder affecting the underlying language system is indicated.

[[CHILD|Mateo]]'s strengths include good stimulability for speech sound targets with minimal to moderate cueing, strong nonverbal problem-solving skills noted informally during testing, a supportive home environment with rich language exposure in Spanish, and motivation to communicate despite frustration. Areas of concern include significantly reduced intelligibility across both languages, limited grammatical morphology, and reduced opportunities for Spanish language modeling in his current daycare placement, which may be compounding rather than causing his underlying disorder.
