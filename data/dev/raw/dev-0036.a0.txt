Nonverbal cognitive skills, as measured by the Leiter-3, were within the average range, suggesting that [[CHILD|Mateo]]'s language and speech sound difficulties are not attributable to global cognitive delay. Receptive language in Spanish was mildly below average, while expressive language was significantly below average in both languages, consistent with a mixed receptive-expressive language disorder affecting both of [[CHILD|Mateo]]'s languages rather than a difference attributable to typical bilingual acquisition.

Speech Sound Production

Conversational speech sampling in both languages yielded an estimated intelligibility of approximately 40% to unfamiliar listeners and 65% to familiar listeners (parent estimate). On the GFTA-3, [[CHILD|Mateo]] demonstrated the following error patterns, consistent across both languages:

- Final consonant deletion (e.g., "pato" produced as "pa" for the target /pato/; "sol" produced as "so")
- Stopping of fricatives, with /s/ frequently realized as [t], e.g., target "sapo" produced as "tapo"
- Gliding of liquids, with /r/ realized as [w], e.g., target "perro" produced as "pewo," and English "rabbit" produced as "wabbit"
- Cluster reduction, e.g., "flor" produced as "foh"

In Spanish sampling, [[CHILD|Mateo]] produced "nana" for target "manzana" and "pego" for target "perro," each with mod cues (moderate cueing) required to approximate the target. In English sampling, he produced "tat" for "cat" and "doo" for "shoe." SALT-coded transcription of the Spanish sample noted frequent [EW] (error, word-level) and occasional [FP] (filled pause) codes, consistent with word-finding and production difficulty rather than purely motoric limitations. Target phoneme /k/ was inconsistently achieved, often substituted with [t]; /g/ was similarly fronted. Overall accuracy for word-final consonants was 3/10 correct across both languages, and overall percentage of consonants correct was estimated at 58%, below the expected range for his age.

Oral-Motor and Hearing Status

Oral-motor examination revealed structures and function within normal limits for speech; no evidence of frenulum restriction, asymmetry, or dysarthric features was observed. Diadochokinetic rates were age-appropriate. As noted above, [[CHILD|Mateo]]'s most recent hearing screening on [[DATE|9/18/2025]] was within normal limits bilaterally, and there is no current concern for a hearing-based contribution to his speech and language profile, though continued audiological monitoring is recommended given his otitis media history.

Language Sampling and Pragmatics

During play-based sampling, [[CHILD|Mateo]] used primarily single words and occasional two-word combinations (e.g., "más agua," "no quiero") in both languages, with mean length of utterance estimated at 1.8 morphemes in Spanish and 1.6 morphemes in English, both significantly below age expectations (typically 4;7 would be expected to be above 4.0). He demonstrated appropriate eye contact, joint attention, and turn-taking during structured play, and responded to his name and simple one-step directions in both languages. He showed frustration behaviors (gesturing, pulling the examiner's hand) when unable to make himself understood verbally, which is consistent with the pattern described by Ms. [[TEACHER|Amanda Lopez]] at [[DAYCARE|Little Sprouts Academy]].

IMPRESIONES CLÍNICAS / CLINICAL IMPRESSIONS

Based on parent and teacher report, standardized testing in both of [[CHILD|Mateo]]'s languages, and direct observation, [[CHILD|Mateo]] presents with a mixed receptive-expressive language disorder (ICD-10 F80.2) and a speech sound disorder (ICD-10 F80.1) affecting both Spanish and English. Because deficits are present and proportionally similar across both languages, this profile is consistent with a true communication disorder rather than a language difference attributable to typical bilingual or second-language acquisition. Nonverbal cognition is a relative strength and should be leveraged in intervention planning. [[CHILD|Mateo]]'s history of recurrent otitis media during a critical period of early speech and language development (12–30 months) is a plausible contributing factor, though his current hearing status is within normal limits and does not fully account for the persistence of his difficulties at this point.

[[CHILD|Mateo]]'s family provides a rich, supportive bilingual environment, and his sister [[SIBLING|Paz]]'s frequent interpreting on his behalf, while well-intentioned, may be reducing his opportunities for independent communication attempts in less familiar settings. This should be addressed through family coaching.

RECOMENDACIONES / RECOMMENDATIONS
