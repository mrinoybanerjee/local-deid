**Resumen / Summary**

[[CHILD|Mateo]] is a 4-year-2-month-old bilingual Spanish/English-speaking boy who presents with a moderate expressive language delay and a mild-to-moderate phonological disorder affecting intelligibility in both languages. He was referred by his pediatrician, [[PROVIDER|Dr. Anthony Weiss]], of [[HOSPITAL|Catawba Valley Pediatrics]], after his 4-year well-child visit raised concerns about limited sentence length and reduced speech clarity. His mother, [[PARENT|Lucía Rodríguez]], reports that at home "casi siempre repite las últimas palabras, pero no forma oraciones completas" (he almost always repeats the last words, but doesn't form full sentences). Testing was conducted in both English and Spanish to appropriately characterize his bilingual language profile per best practice for dual-language learners.

Articulation testing revealed phonological patterns consistent with final consonant deletion (e.g., "ga" for "gato"), cluster reduction (e.g., "pato" for "plato"), and stopping of fricatives (e.g., [t] for /s/ in word-initial position), occurring in approximately 40% of possible contexts across both languages. These patterns are considered developmentally appropriate for Spanish-influenced English in some cases but excessive in frequency for his chronological age, even accounting for dialectal and bilingual phonological variation.

Expressively, [[CHILD|Mateo]] primarily produced two- to three-word combinations in both languages (e.g., "quiero más jugo," "dog go outside") with frequent omission of grammatical morphemes, including Spanish verb conjugation endings and English third-person -s and past tense -ed. Receptively, he followed two-step directions in Spanish with 8/10 accuracy and in English with 6/10 accuracy. During a narrative retell task using a wordless picture book, [[CHILD|Mateo]] required moderate cues (mod cues) to sequence events and relied heavily on gestures, coded as [EW] for word-finding difficulty during the sample.

Pragmatic skills, including joint attention, turn-taking, and requesting, were judged age-appropriate by parent report and clinical observation. [[CHILD|Mateo]] initiated communication frequently with both the examiner, [[PROVIDER|Gabriela Fuentes]], and the interpreter, [[PROVIDER|Marisol Peña]], using a mix of gestures, single words, and short phrases in both languages.

**Impresiones / Impressions**

[[CHILD|Mateo]] presents with a moderate expressive language delay and a mild receptive language delay affecting both Spanish and English, consistent with a true bilingual language disorder rather than a language difference attributable to typical second-language acquisition. His speech sound errors, while showing some overlap with normal bilingual phonological transfer, occur with sufficient frequency and consistency to also warrant a diagnosis of a mild-to-moderate phonological disorder in both languages. His history of recurrent otitis media may have contributed to early speech sound development but does not fully account for his current profile, particularly given his documented normal hearing on the [[DATE|June 2, 2025]] audiogram.

[[CHILD|Mateo]]'s strong pragmatic skills, intact play skills, and supportive, language-rich home environment are significant protective factors. The family's commitment to bilingualism should be supported and encouraged throughout intervention; research consistently shows that supporting both languages, rather than reducing exposure to one, best supports long-term language outcomes for bilingual children.
