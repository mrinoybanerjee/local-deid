BEHAVIORAL OBSERVATIONS

During both evaluation sessions, [[CHILD|Teo]] presented as a friendly, curious child who separated easily from his mother and engaged readily with this examiner and with the interpreter, [[PROVIDER|Patricia Gómez]]. He demonstrated age-appropriate play skills, including symbolic play with toy animals and a pretend kitchen set, and engaged in reciprocal turn-taking during structured and unstructured tasks. Attention and engagement were adequate for the duration of both sessions, though some redirection was needed during longer verbal tasks.

Speech intelligibility was notably reduced in connected speech, estimated at approximately 40% for unfamiliar listeners and 65% for familiar listeners (parent report), consistent with concerns raised by [[TEACHER|la maestra Carmen]] and [[PARENT|Lucía]]. [[CHILD|Teo]] was observed to use a limited consonant inventory, with frequent final consonant deletion (e.g., "ca" for "cat"), cluster reduction ("poon" for "spoon"), and gliding of liquids (e.g., "wed" for "red," producing /w/ for /r/). In Spanish, similar patterns were observed, with simplification of consonant clusters in words like "problema," produced as [poblema], and difficulty with the trilled /r/ sound. [[CHILD|Teo]] was stimulable for several target sounds with [[TIME|moderate]] cueing, achieving correct productions of /k/ and /g/ in isolation with mod cues during structured drill tasks.

Oral mechanism examination revealed adequate structure and function for speech purposes, with no evidence of structural abnormality, dysarthria, or apraxia-related symptoms. Diadochokinetic rates were judged to be within age expectations.

Spontaneous language samples were collected in both English and Spanish during play-based interaction. In English, [[CHILD|Teo]] produced primarily 2- to 3-word utterances (e.g., "I want that," "dog go outside"), with occasional 4-word combinations. Mean length of utterance in morphemes (MLU-m) was calculated at 2.8, below the expected range for his chronological age. SALT coding of the sample identified frequent omission of grammatical morphemes, coded as [EW] for several utterances involving omitted copula "is" (e.g., "he happy" for "he is happy"). In Spanish, utterances were similarly limited in length and complexity, with omission of articles and inconsistent verb conjugation (e.g., "yo quiero ir" produced as "yo queer ir").

Articulation testing revealed a moderate phonological disorder affecting both languages, with phonological processes including final consonant deletion, cluster reduction, gliding, and stopping of fricatives. Target word probes elicited productions such as "sun" produced as [tʊn] and "shoe" produced as [tu], with the fricative /ʃ/ consistently realized as [t]. In quoted productions, [[CHILD|Teo]] was able to approximate the word "casa" as "tasa" and "perro" as "peo," demonstrating difficulty with the trilled /r/ phoneme. Stimulability testing showed that [[CHILD|Teo]] could approximate several target sounds with moderate cueing, including /s/ and /ʃ/ in isolation, though not yet in connected speech.

Receptive and expressive vocabulary in both languages fell within the low-average to below-average range, with notably stronger vocabulary in Spanish for home and family-related concepts and stronger vocabulary in English for daycare and school-related concepts, consistent with the bilingual language environment described by the family.

IMPRESSIONS

Based on parent and teacher report, direct observation, and standardized and informal assessment results, [[CHILD|Teo]] presents with a moderate speech sound disorder affecting intelligibility in both English and Spanish, alongside a mild-to-moderate expressive and receptive language delay in both languages. Because deficits are present and proportionally similar across both of [[CHILD|Teo]]'s languages, this profile is consistent with a true communication disorder rather than a language difference attributable to typical bilingual development or reduced exposure to either language. [[CHILD|Teo]]'s strengths include strong social engagement, intact play skills, adequate oral-motor structure and function, and stimulability for several error sounds with cueing support, all of which are positive prognostic indicators for therapy.

The history of chronic otitis media with effusion, treated with tympanostomy tubes placed on [[DATE|June 4, 2024]], may have contributed to early speech sound development delays, though current hearing status is reported as normal. [[CHILD|Teo]]'s rich home language environment, supported by his mother, father, and grandmother ([[FAMILY|doña Lupe]]), provides a strong foundation for bilingual intervention, and his family's engagement with community supports, including [[AGENCY|WIC]], [[AGENCY|Smart Start]], and [[AGENCY|Cabarrus County DSS]], reflects a supportive and resourceful family system.
