CLINICAL IMPRESSIONS

Resumen Clínico (Clinical Summary)
  - [[CHILD|Mateo Flores]], a 4;7-year-old bilingual Spanish-English speaking male, presents with a mixed receptive-expressive language disorder (F80.2) and a moderate phonological disorder, affecting both languages but more pronounced in English, his less dominant language.
  - Deficits are evident relative to normative expectations for Spanish, his primary language of exposure, indicating a true language disorder rather than a language difference attributable solely to bilingual acquisition or reduced English exposure.
  - Reduced speech intelligibility (approximately 40% to unfamiliar listeners) and limited expressive output (MLU below age expectations in both languages) are contributing to functional communication breakdowns at home and in the daycare setting, as well as emerging behavioral frustration.
  - Recurrent early otitis media with confirmed conductive hearing loss history (resolved post-tube placement) may have contributed to early phonological and language delays, though current hearing is documented within normal limits.
  - Family provides a linguistically rich, supportive home environment; services should be delivered bilingually or with strong Spanish-language support to align with the family's primary language and maximize carryover.

DIAGNOSES
  - Mixed Receptive-Expressive Language Disorder (ICD-10: F80.2)
  - Phonological Disorder, moderate severity
