On the GFTA-3, [[CHILD|Mateo]] produced numerous sound errors affecting intelligibility, including stopping of fricatives, gliding of liquids, and final consonant deletion. These error patterns were judged to reflect a true speech sound disorder rather than typical second-language transfer, given their presence across both languages and their inconsistency with developmental norms for his age in either language.

LANGUAGE SAMPLE

A 20-minute bilingual play-based language sample was collected using toy animals, a dollhouse, and a set of vehicles, with [[CHILD|Mateo]]'s mother present to support elicitation in Spanish and the clinician eliciting in English. [[CHILD|Mateo]]'s mean length of utterance (MLU) in morphemes was calculated at 2.1 in English and 2.6 in Spanish, both below age expectations of approximately 3.5–4.5 morphemes for a child his age. SALT coding of the transcript indicated frequent [EW] (error word) codes associated with omitted grammatical morphemes, including omission of articles, copula "is/es," and plural markers in both languages. [[CHILD|Mateo]] used primarily single words and two-word combinations such as "más agua," "no quiero," and "Teo jugar," with few examples of three-word utterances observed. He demonstrated use of present-tense verbs but did not use past-tense marking in either language during the sample.

Pragmatically, [[CHILD|Mateo]] demonstrated joint attention, turn-taking during simple games, and functional use of gestures to request and comment. He made eye contact appropriately and responded to his name consistently. He engaged in brief back-and-forth exchanges with the clinician when supported with visual cues and modeling, and his mother reported that this behavior was consistent with how he interacts with [[FAMILY|Rosa]] and [[SIBLING|Camila]] at home.

CLINICAL IMPRESSIONS

Based on parent report, teacher report, standardized test results, and clinical observation, [[CHILD|Mateo]] presents with a mixed receptive-expressive language disorder (ICD-10: F80.2) affecting both Spanish and English, along with a speech sound disorder (ICD-10: F80.0) affecting intelligibility in both languages. His language delay cannot be attributed solely to bilingual exposure, as deficits are evident relative to same-age bilingual peers on a measure normed for bilingual children (BESA) as well as on English-only measures. His speech sound errors are consistent across both languages and exceed what would be expected from typical cross-linguistic transfer or second-language acquisition at this stage.

[[CHILD|Mateo]]'s history of recurrent otitis media, while not currently associated with a measurable hearing loss, may have contributed to early language learning difficulty and should continue to be monitored closely given the established relationship between chronic middle ear effusion and speech-language development in young children.

[[CHILD|Mateo]] demonstrates relative strengths in nonverbal communication, joint attention, play skills, and social engagement, which are positive prognostic indicators for therapy. His home language environment, which includes consistent Spanish input from his mother and grandmother, should be supported and maintained rather than discouraged, consistent with current evidence-based practice for bilingual children with communication disorders.
