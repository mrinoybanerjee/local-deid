CLINICAL IMPRESSIONS

Based on today's assessment, [[CHILD|Majo]] presents with a speech sound disorder and an expressive-receptive language disorder affecting both Spanish and English, consistent with a primary language and speech impairment rather than a pattern attributable to typical bilingual development alone. Her scores fall well below expectations in both languages assessed, and her phonological error patterns in Spanish mirror those observed in English, which argues against normal cross-linguistic transfer as the sole explanation for her presentation. Her reported history of chronic otitis media with effusion during a developmentally sensitive period for speech and language acquisition, along with her NICU course following preterm birth, are considered possible contributing factors, although her most recent hearing screening is within normal limits. Given her current language exposure of roughly 70% Spanish, intervention should be delivered primarily in Spanish, with ongoing attention to cross-linguistic skills as her English exposure continues to increase through her brother, [[SIBLING|Diego Hernandez Lopez]], and her daycare environment. [[CHILD|Majo]]'s strong stimulability for several error sounds and her engaged, social presentation during testing are favorable prognostic indicators.
