5.2 Speech Sound Production

Percentage of Consonants Correct (PCC) was calculated from spontaneous speech samples obtained in each language. In the Spanish sample, PCC was 62%; in the English sample, PCC was 58%. Error patterns were consistent across both languages and included final consonant deletion, cluster reduction, and gliding of liquids.

Examples of [[CHILD|Majo]]'s Spanish productions included "tete" for "leche," reflecting fronting and stopping, and "nana" for "banana," reflecting weak syllable deletion. In English, [[CHILD|Majo]] produced "wabbit" for "rabbit" (gliding of /r/) and "pay" for "play" (cluster reduction). Stimulability testing showed that [[CHILD|Majo]] could approximate the target /l/ in isolation with mod cues but reduced clusters again at the word level. Several target words were coded [EW] during the English sample for whole-word errors affecting intelligibility.

5.3 Intelligibility

Using a structured rating scale, [[CHILD|Majo]]'s connected speech was approximately 7/10 words intelligible to a familiar listener (her mother) in context, and approximately 3/10 words intelligible to an unfamiliar listener (this examiner) without contextual support, in both languages. This represents a clinically significant intelligibility deficit for a child of 4;6.

5.4 Language Sampling

Mean length of utterance in words, calculated from the Spanish sample, was 2.8 words per utterance, below expectations for her age. [[CHILD|Majo]] used primarily two- and three-word combinations, with limited use of grammatical morphemes in either language (for example, plural markers and verb tense inflections were frequently omitted). Vocabulary diversity, as measured by number of different words across the combined sample, was also below expectations relative to typical peers.

6. CLINICAL IMPRESSIONS

Results of this evaluation indicate that [[CHILD|Majo]] presents with a speech sound disorder and a mixed receptive-expressive language disorder affecting both of her languages, Spanish and English. Her language scores fell in the below-average to well-below-average range in both languages, with Spanish scores somewhat stronger than English scores, consistent with her relative language exposure. Because deficits are present across both languages rather than isolated to the second, non-dominant language, these findings are not accounted for by typical bilingual language-learning patterns alone and are consistent with a true underlying language impairment (ICD-10: F80.1, Expressive Language Disorder, with receptive involvement).

[[CHILD|Majo]]'s speech sound production also fell in the well-below-average range, with reduced intelligibility across both languages that is significant for her age and exceeds what would be expected from typical bilingual phonological development or residual effects of her history of otitis media alone (ICD-10: F80.0, Phonological Disorder). Her history of recurrent otitis media with effusion likely contributed to, though does not fully account for, her current speech and language profile, particularly given her now-normal hearing.

[[CHILD|Majo]]'s nonverbal communication skills, social engagement, play skills, and oral-motor function are judged to be age-appropriate and are considered relative strengths that will support her progress in intervention.

7. SUMMARY

[[CHILD|María José Fernández López]], a 4;6-year-old bilingual Spanish-English-speaking girl, was evaluated at the request of her family and daycare teacher due to concerns regarding intelligibility and expressive language. Results of standardized testing and language sampling in both Spanish and English indicate a speech sound disorder and a mixed receptive-expressive language disorder affecting both languages. [[CHILD|Majo]] demonstrates relative strengths in social communication, play, and nonverbal skills. She meets criteria for continued speech-language intervention services.
