Language Sample Analysis

A 50-utterance spontaneous language sample was collected in Spanish, yielding a mean length of utterance (MLU) of 2.1 morphemes, which falls well below age expectations. A parallel 30-utterance sample in English yielded an MLU of 1.6 morphemes. Across both samples, [[CHILD|Mateo]] relied heavily on single-word utterances and two-word combinations (for example, "mas agua," "no quiero," "perro grande"), with limited use of grammatical morphemes such as plural markers and verb tense inflections in either language. SALT coding identified frequent use of the [EW] code for word-finding difficulty and reduced use of bound morphemes relative to same-age bilingual peers.

Articulation and Phonology

On the GFTA-3, [[CHILD|Mateo]] demonstrated the following sound errors in English: final consonant deletion (for example, "bu" for "bus"), stopping of fricatives (for example, "tu" for "shoe," producing /t/ for /sh/), and gliding of liquids (for example, "wabbit" for "rabbit," [w] for /r/). In Spanish, similar patterns of final consonant deletion and gliding were observed, with the trilled /r/ consistently produced as [l], consistent with a phonological process pattern rather than an articulation disorder isolated to one language. Intelligibility was judged to be approximately 6/10 in known context and 3/10 to unfamiliar listeners, which is reduced for his age in both languages.

Receptive and Expressive Language

[[CHILD|Mateo]]'s receptive language skills, while also below age expectations, were relatively stronger than his expressive language skills across both languages, consistent with a receptive-expressive gap commonly seen in true language impairment rather than second-language acquisition alone. He followed one-step directions reliably (8/10 trials) but struggled with two-step directions involving spatial concepts (3/10 trials). Vocabulary knowledge, assessed informally through labeling and category tasks, was estimated to be below the 10th percentile in both languages when scores were considered individually, though his total conceptual vocabulary across both languages was somewhat stronger, suggesting that language exposure patterns alone do not fully account for his difficulties.

CLINICAL IMPRESSIONS

Based on standardized testing, language sampling, parent and teacher report, and clinical observation, [[CHILD|Mateo]] presents with a developmental language disorder affecting both Spanish and English, characterized by significantly reduced expressive vocabulary, limited morphosyntactic development, and a co-occurring phonological disorder affecting speech sound production in both languages. His pattern of errors and the gap between receptive and expressive skills, present across both languages rather than isolated to the language of lesser exposure, support a diagnosis of language disorder rather than a language difference attributable solely to bilingual development. His case is consistent with ICD-10 code F80.1 (expressive language disorder) and F80.0 (phonological disorder), and his presentation meets criteria under CPT code 92523 (evaluation of speech sound production with evaluation of language comprehension and expression).

[[CHILD|Mateo]]'s reduced intelligibility and limited expressive language are likely contributing to the frustration behaviors reported by his mother, [[PARENT|Lucia]], and by [[TEACHER|la maestra Carmen]] at daycare. Continued delay in both languages, rather than a lag confined to his less-dominant language, supports continued eligibility for speech-language intervention services as he transitions from Part C to Part B services.
