Resultados de la evaluación / Present Levels of Development

Esta servidora, [[PROVIDER|Marisol Delgado Peña]], MS, CCC-SLP, bilingual speech-language pathologist with [[EMPLOYER|Carolina Pediatric Therapy Services, PLLC]], completó la reevaluación de lenguaje el [[DATE|28 de mayo de 2025]] con la interpretación de [[PROVIDER|Ana Beltrán]], interpreter certified through [[AGENCY|Piedmont Regional CDSA]].

On the Preschool Language Scales, Fifth Edition (PLS-5), Spanish-English bilingual form, [[CHILD|Mateo]] obtained a standard score of 72 on the Auditory Comprehension subtest (3rd percentile) and a standard score of 68 on the Expressive Communication subtest (2nd percentile), both more than 1.5 standard deviations below the mean for his age. On the Goldman-Fristoe Test of Articulation-3 (GFTA-3), he produced target consonants correctly in 6/10 opportunities at the word level, with the most consistent errors on /k/ and /g/, which he frequently fronted to [t] and [d] (e.g., target "gato" produced as [ˈtato]). Spontaneous language sample analysis, coded in SALT, showed a mean length of utterance of 1.4 morphemes and frequent code-switching within utterances, marked [CS] in the transcript (e.g., "quiero [CS] water," "mas [CS] please"), which is developmentally appropriate for a bilingual toddler and not itself a disorder marker.

During structured play, [[CHILD|Mateo]] requested preferred items with moderate verbal cues approximately 40% of the time, improving to 70% with simultaneous Spanish and English verbal models. He imitated the target word "agua" as [ˈwawa] and "más" as [ˈmas] with minimal cues. These findings are consistent with a mixed receptive-expressive language delay in both of [[CHILD|Mateo]]'s languages, which supports continued eligibility for Part C services and does not reflect simple bilingual exposure, contrary to the concern [[PARENT|Lucía]] mentioned above.

I explained to the family, using the interpreter, that children raised bilingually do not experience language delay "because of" two languages; research consistently shows no such causal link, and that [[CHILD|Mateo]]'s profile shows delay across both languages, which is the pattern we would expect to see and treat regardless of how many languages he hears at home. [[PARENT|Lucía]] seemed relieved to hear this and said, "Entonces puedo seguir hablándole español en casa," to which I responded that yes, continuing Spanish at home is strongly recommended and will support, not hinder, his progress in English.
