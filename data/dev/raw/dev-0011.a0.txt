ASSESSMENT PROCEDURES AND BEHAVIORAL OBSERVATIONS

This evaluation included a parent interview conducted in both English and Spanish with support from interpreter [[PROVIDER|Maria Gutierrez]], a review of early intervention records provided by [[PROVIDER|Mr. Tom Bradley]], administration of standardized and non-standardized measures in both English and Spanish, a connected speech and language sample, an oral mechanism examination, and informal observation of play-based interaction. Testing occurred across two sessions at the clinic's office in [[PLACE|Brantley Falls, NC]], each lasting approximately 60 minutes.

[[CHILD|Mateo]] separated easily from his mother and engaged readily with toys and testing materials. He demonstrated a friendly, social demeanor and attempted most tasks presented to him, though he required frequent repetition of directions and occasional simplification of task demands. Attention was adequate for structured tasks but began to wane toward the end of each session, at which point brief movement breaks were provided. Oral mechanism examination revealed structures and function within normal limits for speech production, with adequate diadochokinetic rates for his age. Hearing was grossly screened using behavioral observation audiometry, and [[CHILD|Mateo]] responded consistently to speech and environmental sounds at conversational and soft-conversational levels; however, a formal audiological evaluation is recommended given the absence of any screening since infancy.

On the GFTA-3, administered in English, [[CHILD|Mateo]] produced consistent substitutions and omissions that reduced intelligibility, including fronting of velar sounds (producing "doat" for "goat"), stopping of fricatives (producing "tun" for "sun"), and final consonant deletion (producing "cu" for "cup"). Phonological process analysis via the KLPA-3 identified fronting, stopping, and final consonant deletion as persisting beyond the age at which these processes are typically suppressed. In connected speech, intelligibility was estimated at approximately 50 percent to unfamiliar listeners and 70 percent to familiar listeners, consistent with parent and teacher report.

A parallel informal phonological inventory was conducted in Spanish using single-word picture naming. [[CHILD|Mateo]] demonstrated similar patterns of fronting and final consonant deletion in Spanish productions, for example producing [to] for "sol" and [ka.'be.o] for "cabello," suggesting that his speech sound difficulties are not limited to English and are unlikely to reflect typical cross-linguistic transfer alone.

Language sample analysis, coded using SALT conventions, indicated a mean length of utterance of 3.1 morphemes in English and 3.4 morphemes in Spanish, both below expectations for his chronological age. [[CHILD|Mateo]] used primarily subject-verb or verb-object combinations, with limited use of grammatical morphemes such as third-person "-s," copula "is/was," and Spanish verb conjugation beyond third-person singular present tense. Code-switching was noted intrasententially at a rate judged consistent with, rather than exceeding, typical patterns for a child with comparable language exposure, and was not interpreted as evidence of disorder in isolation. One utterance was coded as [[[EW]] (excessive whining) due to frustration during a task requiring sustained verbal output.

On the BESA, [[CHILD|Mateo]] scored below the typical range in both morphosyntax and semantics in Spanish, and his PLS-5 scores in English similarly fell well below age expectations in both receptive and expressive domains. Because deficits were observed across both of his languages, in both standardized and informal measures, his language difficulties cannot be attributed solely to reduced exposure to either language and are consistent with a primary language impairment occurring alongside a speech sound disorder.

Qualitative observations during play included limited use of pretend play narration, reliance on gesture to supplement verbal requests, and occasional perseverative use of favorite phrases such as "no quiero" when frustrated. [[CHILD|Mateo]] required moderate cues (mod cues) to imitate multisyllabic words accurately and benefited from visual supports paired with verbal models.

IMPRESSIONS

Based on parent report, review of early intervention history, and results of this bilingual speech-language evaluation, [[CHILD|Mateo]] presents with a moderate speech sound disorder characterized by persistent phonological processes (fronting, stopping, final consonant deletion) affecting intelligibility in both English and Spanish, and a moderate-to-severe expressive and receptive language disorder affecting both of his languages. Given that deficits are present across both languages and across multiple assessment methods, these findings are not consistent with typical second-language acquisition or with normal variation seen in bilingual children. His history of prematurity, NICU stay, and a possible family history of late language emergence (noted for his father, [[PARENT|Hector]]) are relevant contributing factors but do not fully account for the magnitude of his current deficits.

[[CHILD|Mateo]] meets eligibility criteria for ongoing speech-language intervention under [[AGENCY|Piedmont CDSA]]'s Infant-Toddler Program and is approaching the transition age for preschool special education services through [[SCHOOL|Brantley Falls Elementary School]]'s Exceptional Children's program. Coordination between [[PROVIDER|Mr. Tom Bradley]], the family, and [[DAYCARE|Little Sprouts Early Head Start]] staff, including [[TEACHER|la maestra Carmen]] and [[TEACHER|Miss Ana]], will be important to ensure continuity of services during this transition.
